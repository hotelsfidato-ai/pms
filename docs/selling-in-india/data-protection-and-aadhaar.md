## 1. DPDP Rules 2025: when things start, the Board and Consent Managers

- **The Rules were notified as G.S.R. 846(E), dated 13 Nov 2025.** They were made under s.40 of the DPDP Act and follow the draft G.S.R. 02(E) of 3 Jan 2025.
  - Source: Rules, preamble and r.1. [Link](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
  - Status: in force as phased below.
  - Confidence: **high**, read from the gazette.

- **The Rules start in three steps (r.1(2)-(4)):**
  - **At once (13 Nov 2025):** r.1, r.2 and r.17-21. These cover definitions and how the Board is set up and runs.
  - **One year after publication (13 or 14 Nov 2026):** r.4, the registration and duties of Consent Managers.
  - **Eighteen months after publication (13 or 14 May 2027):** r.3 (notice), r.5-16 (security, breach reporting, erasure, contact details, children, SDFs, rights, cross-border transfer, research) and r.22-23.
  - Source: r.1. Status: as stated.
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **The Act starts on the same three steps (G.S.R. 843(E), 13 Nov 2025, under s.1(2)):**
  - **At once:** s.1(2), 2, 18-26, 35, 38-43, 44(1) and 44(3). Section 44(3) is the amendment to the RTI Act.
  - **After 1 year:** s.6(9) (Consent Manager registration) and s.27(1)(d).
  - **After 18 months:** s.3-5, s.6(1)-(8) and (10), s.7-17, s.27 except (1)(d), s.28-34, s.36, s.37 and **s.44(2)**. Section 44(2) is the amendment to the IT Act that removes s.43A.
  - So none of a hotel's duties as a data fiduciary is in force today.
  - Source: G.S.R. 843(E). [Link](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf)
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **Board established, but nobody appointed to it.**
  - The Data Protection Board of India was established from 13 Nov 2025, with its head office in the NCR.
  - Source: G.S.R. 844(E). [Link](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/cc217843dc3bcb37b2b05bcc3b4e031f.pdf)
  - On 6 May 2026 MeitY invited applications for 1 Chairperson and 4 Members (F. No. 2(1)/2026-Pers.I). [Link](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/05/cd481c027470b420b4cb85fb40a91c53.pdf)
  - LiveLaw (1 Aug 2026) said the Board still had no Chairperson or Members. [Link](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/india-data-protection-board-established-law-543751)
  - dpdprules.org (updated 23 Sep 2026) says the posts were still vacant on 28 Aug 2026. [Link](https://dpdprules.org/timeline)
  - One search snippet named a Chairperson from Wikipedia. It has no source and looks like vandalism, so I ignored it.
  - **I found no Board website.** dpb.gov.in, dpbi.gov.in and "dpdpa-grievance.gov.in" (which some blogs cite) do not resolve.
  - Status: established in law, not working in practice.
  - Confidence: **high** for the establishment. **Medium** for the vacancy after August, because the last evidence is a secondary source dated 28 Aug.

- **Consent Manager registration opens on 13 or 14 Nov 2026.** That is when r.4 and s.6(9) start. Applications go to the Board in the form it publishes on its website (r.4(1)). The conditions are in the First Schedule, including an Indian company with net worth of at least ₹2 crore.
  - Status: not yet in force. No application form or portal was found. It is unclear how registration can open while the Board has no members.
  - Confidence: **high** for the date, **low** that it will happen on time.

- **MeitY proposed shortening the 18 months to 12. It has not been notified.**
  - At a stakeholder meeting on 22-23 Jan 2026, MeitY floated making all obligations start on 13 Nov 2026, and possibly bringing forward s.16 (cross-border) and s.36 / r.23 (power to call for information). Comments were due by 4 Feb 2026.
  - Sources (secondary): [Business Standard 22 Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html); [S.S.   - **No amending gazette notice was found.** dpdprules.org found none as of 6 Sep 2026.
  - Status: proposed only. May 2027 stands.
  - Confidence: **medium**. No primary MeitY text of the proposal exists; it is all press reports.

## 2. What a hotel must do as a data fiduciary

A hotel decides why and how guest data is processed, so it is a **Data Fiduciary** (s.2(i)). Everything in this section starts on **13 May 2027** unless stated otherwise.

### Scope

- **The Act covers digital personal data, including paper data that is later scanned.** It also covers processing outside India if it is connected to offering goods or services in India (s.3(a)-(b)). So a scanned ID or a typed-up paper register is covered.
  - Confidence: **high**.
- **The exemption for foreigners' data does not cover foreign guests.** Section 17(1)(d) exempts processing of people *not in India* under a contract with a person *outside* India, which is the outsourcing case. A foreign guest staying in India is in India, so the Act applies to them.
  - Confidence: **high** on the text.

### Legal ground: consent or "legitimate use"

- **There are only two grounds for processing (s.4):** consent (s.6), or a legitimate use listed in s.7.
- **Consent (s.6)** must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous. It covers only data needed for the stated purpose. Any part of a consent that breaks the law is void (s.6(2)). If there is a dispute, the fiduciary must prove that notice was given and consent obtained (s.6(10)). Confidence: **high**.
- **Legitimate uses relevant to a hotel:**
  - **s.7(a):** the guest *voluntarily* gives data for a specified purpose, such as a booking or a stay, and has not said they object.
  - **s.7(d):** meeting a legal obligation to *disclose* information to the State. Example: the foreign-guest return (Form III, formerly Form C) under Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025 s.8; see §6.
  - **s.7(e):** complying with a court order or judgment.
  - Section 7(c) is for the *State's* own functions and does not cover a hotel.
- **What this means for ID collected because the law requires it:**
  - **Foreign guests:** passport and visa data needed for Form III can rely on **s.7(d)** without consent. Confidence: **medium**, because nothing has tested it yet.
  - **Indian guests:** police register rules are local (state or city). Where such a rule obliges the hotel to give guest data to the police, s.7(d) fits. Otherwise the hotel relies on **s.7(a)**. Confidence: **medium**.
  - **Marketing, profiling, and sharing with third parties for their own use:** these need **consent**. Confidence: **high**.
- **The notice duty follows the consent request.** The s.5 notice is required for requests for consent "under section 6". Processing under legitimate use does not strictly need a s.5 notice.
  - The access and correction rights in s.11 and s.12 still cover s.7(a) data.
  - Best practice is one notice covering both grounds.
  - Confidence: **medium**. This is my reading of the text; there is no guidance yet.

### Notice: s.5 and r.3

- **What the notice must contain:**
  - It must stand alone, understandable without any other information (r.3(a)).
  - It must be in clear, plain language and include at least:
    - an **itemised description of the personal data**;
    - the **specified purpose(s)**;
    - a **specific description of the goods, services or uses** the processing enables (r.3(b)).
  - It must give a **particular link** to the website or app, and a description of any other means, by which the guest can:
    - **withdraw consent**, as easily as it was given;
    - **exercise their rights** under the Act;
    - **complain to the Board** (r.3(c)).
  - The guest must be able to read the notice in **English or any Eighth Schedule language** (s.5(3), s.6(3)).
  - The consent request must give the **contact details of the DPO or authorised person** (s.6(3)).
  - Existing guests who consented **before** commencement must get a notice "as soon as reasonably practicable" (s.5(2)).
  - Confidence: **high**.

### Withdrawing consent

- **Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent (s.6(4)).** Earlier processing stays lawful (s.6(5)).
- **After withdrawal, the fiduciary must stop processing, and make its processors stop, "within a reasonable time"** unless the law requires or allows the processing (s.6(6)).
- **The data must then be erased** (s.8(7)(a)).
- Confidence: **high**.

### Accuracy and accountability

- **Data must be complete, accurate and consistent** if it is used to make a decision about the guest or is disclosed to another fiduciary (s.8(3)).
- **The fiduciary must take technical and organisational measures (s.8(4)).**
- **The fiduciary is responsible for its processors "irrespective of any agreement to the contrary" (s.8(1)).**
- Confidence: **high**.

### Reasonable security safeguards: s.8(5) and r.6

The minimum, which covers processing done by processors too:

- **(a)** encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens;
- **(b)** access control over the computer resources of both the fiduciary *and* the processor;
- **(c)** visibility of who accessed the data, through **logs, monitoring and review**, so that unauthorised access can be detected, investigated and fixed;
- **(d)** the ability to keep processing if data is compromised, for example **backups**;
- **(e)** keeping those **logs and personal data for one year**, unless another law says otherwise;
- **(f)** **security terms in the contract with the processor**;
- **(g)** technical and organisational measures that make the safeguards work.

Status: starts 13 May 2027. Penalty up to **₹250 crore**. Confidence: **high**.

### Minimum retention of logs: r.8(3)

- **Personal data, traffic data and processing logs must be kept for at least one year from processing.** This applies to data processed by the fiduciary "or on its behalf by a Data Processor". The purposes are those in the Seventh Schedule: State use for sovereignty or security, performance of legal functions, and SDF assessment.
- After that year the data and logs must be erased, unless another law requires longer retention.
- Illustration 2 in the Rules says the fiduciary must make sure its **cloud processor** keeps the data and logs for a year.
- Confidence: **high** on the text.

### Personal data breach: s.8(6) and r.7

- **To each affected guest, "without delay"**, through their user account or a contact channel they registered:
  - a description of the breach (nature, extent, timing);
  - the likely consequences for them;
  - mitigation already taken or under way;
  - safety steps they can take;
  - business contact details of someone who can answer questions (r.7(1)).
- **To the Board, "without delay":** a description of the breach, with its nature, extent, timing, location and likely impact (r.7(2)(a)).
- **To the Board, within 72 hours** of becoming aware, or longer if the Board allows it in writing (r.7(2)(b)):
  - updated and detailed information;
  - the broad facts, circumstances and reasons;
  - mitigation measures taken;
  - any findings about who caused the breach;
  - remedial measures to prevent it happening again;
  - a report on the notices sent to affected guests.
- There is **no minimum-harm threshold**: every personal data breach must be reported.
- Penalty up to **₹200 crore**.
- The **CERT-In 6-hour report** applies in parallel and is already in force; see §5.
- Confidence: **high**.

### Erasure and retention: s.8(7)-(8), r.8 and Third Schedule

- **The general rule (s.8(7)):**
  - Erase data when consent is withdrawn, or when "it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose is no longer being served", whichever comes first, **unless retention is necessary to comply with law**.
  - Make processors erase too.
  - Illustration II in the Act: a bank keeps KYC records for the period the law requires.
  - Confidence: **high**.
- **The 3-year inactivity rule and the 48-hour warning (r.8(1)-(2), Third Schedule) do not cover a hotel.** They apply only to:
  - e-commerce entities with **2 crore or more** registered users in India;
  - online gaming intermediaries with **50 lakh or more**;
  - social media intermediaries with **2 crore or more**.
  - For those, data is erased 3 years after the person last engaged or after the Rules commenced, whichever is later, with a warning at least 48 hours before.
  - An ordinary hotel or hotel group is **not** in these classes, so the general s.8(7) test applies. I found no prescribed number of days for hotels.
  - Confidence: **high** that hotels are outside the Schedule. A very large hotel booking platform could be an "e-commerce entity" by user count.

### Children: s.9, r.10 and r.12 with the Fourth Schedule

- **A child is anyone under 18 (s.2(f)).**
- **Verifiable parental consent is needed before processing *any* personal data of a child (s.9(1)).**
  - Under r.10, the fiduciary must check that the person consenting as parent is an identifiable adult. It does this using reliable identity and age details **it already holds** (r.10(1)(a)), for example the parent's own check-in ID, or through a virtual token such as DigiLocker.
- **No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children (s.9(3)).**
- **No processing likely to harm a child's well-being (s.9(2)).**
- **The Fourth Schedule exemptions do not cover hotels.**
  - Part A covers health establishments, educational institutions, crèches and school transport.
  - Part B covers State functions, email accounts, real-time location for safety, blocking harmful content, and age checks.
- **Does registering a child guest trigger s.9(1)?** On the plain words, yes: s.9(1) has no carve-out for s.7 legitimate uses. In practice the parent or guardian usually books and checks in with the child, so recording the parent's consent on the registration and checking against the parent's ID (r.10(1)(a)) satisfies it.
  - A child checking in **alone** needs a policy decision.
- Penalty up to **₹200 crore**.
- Confidence: **medium**. The interaction between s.7 and s.9 is untested.

### Guests' rights: s.11-14 and r.14

- **Access (s.11):**
  - A summary of the personal data held and the processing done.
  - **The identities of every other fiduciary *and processor* the data was shared with**, with a description of what was shared. For a hotel this includes OTAs, the channel manager, payment gateway and the PMS vendor.
  - Sharing with law enforcement on their written request is exempt (s.11(2)).
- **Correction, completion, updating and erasure (s.12).** Erasure can be refused if the data is needed for the purpose or for legal compliance (s.12(3)).
- **Grievance redressal (s.13).** The guest must use it before going to the Board.
- **Nominee (s.14; r.14(4)).**
- **What r.14 requires:**
  - Publish on the website or app how to make a request and which identifiers are needed (r.14(1)).
  - Publish the grievance response period, which may be **at most 90 days**, and have the measures in place to meet it (r.14(3)).
  - PIB describes 90 days as the maximum for access, correction, update and erasure requests.
- Confidence: **high**.

### Contact person and DPO: s.8(9)-(10), r.9 and s.10

- **Every fiduciary must publish prominently on its website or app, *and* in every reply to a rights request, the business contact of a DPO (if it has one) or of a person who can answer questions (r.9).**
- **Every fiduciary must have an effective grievance mechanism (s.8(10)).**
- **A DPO is mandatory only for a Significant Data Fiduciary.** The DPO must be based in India and report to the board (s.10(2)(a)).
- Confidence: **high**.

### Significant Data Fiduciary: s.10 and r.13

- **There are no numeric thresholds. The Central Government notifies SDFs based on:**
  - volume and sensitivity of data;
  - risk to guests' rights;
  - sovereignty and integrity of India;
  - electoral democracy;
  - security of the State;
  - public order.
- **SDF duties:**
  - a DPO and an independent data auditor;
  - a yearly DPIA and audit, with a report to the Board (r.13(1)-(2));
  - due diligence on algorithms (r.13(3));
  - possible localisation of data specified on a committee's recommendation (r.13(4)).
- **Status: no SDF has been notified.** Confidence: **medium**; I found no notice, but that is proving a negative.
- A hotel group is unlikely to be designated but could be. A **processor cannot be an SDF**: the concept applies only to fiduciaries.

### Cross-border transfer: s.16 and r.15

- **Transfer is allowed except to countries the Government lists as restricted (s.16(1)).** Stricter sectoral laws still apply (s.16(2)).
- **Rule 15:** the fiduciary must meet any requirements set by general or special order about making data available to a **foreign State** or its agencies.
- **Status: no country is listed and no r.15 order has been issued.** Both start 13 May 2027 unless the compression proposal happens.
- Confidence: **medium-high**.

### Penalties: s.33 and the Schedule

| Breach | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|
| Security safeguards, s.8(5) | ₹250 crore |
| Breach notification, s.8(6) | ₹200 crore |
| Children, s.9 | ₹200 crore |
| SDF duties, s.10 | ₹150 crore |
| Guest's own duties, s.15 | ₹10,000 |
| Breach of a voluntary undertaking | Up to the amount for the original breach |
| Any other provision of the Act or Rules | ₹50 crore |

The penalty-imposing provision s.33 is not yet in force (18-month step). Confidence: **high**.

## 3. The vendor as Data Processor

- **A processor can be used only "under a valid contract" (s.8(2)).** The fiduciary may use one "for any activity related to offering of goods or services to Data Principals". Confidence: **high**.
- **What the law requires the contract to contain:**
  - **Security terms:** "appropriate provision … for taking reasonable security safeguards" (r.6(1)(f)).
  - **By implication, the processor must also be bound to:**
    - the access control, logs, monitoring and backup safeguards in r.6(1)(b)-(e), which name the processor's own computer resources;
    - **stop processing when a guest withdraws consent** (s.6(6));
    - **erase data the fiduciary provided** (s.8(7)(b));
    - **keep data, traffic data and logs for at least one year** (r.8(3) and its Illustration 2);
    - **tell the fiduciary about breaches promptly.** The fiduciary's clock under r.7 starts "on becoming aware".
  - Confidence: **high** for what the text requires, **medium** for the implied terms.
- **The processor has no direct duties under the DPDP Act.** The Act puts every duty on the fiduciary, "irrespective of any agreement to the contrary" (s.8(1)). A processor that uses guest data **for its own purposes** becomes a fiduciary itself.
  - Confidence: **high** on the text.
- **Laws that *do* bind the vendor directly:**
  - **CERT-In Directions**, No. 20(3)/2022-CERT-In, 28 Apr 2022, under IT Act s.70B(6). They cover service providers, data centres and body corporates:
    - report cyber incidents to CERT-In **within 6 hours** of noticing them;
    - keep ICT system logs for a **rolling 180 days within India**;
    - synchronise clocks to NIC or NPL NTP servers.
    - In force since about 27 June 2022. Penalty under s.70B(7) up to 1 year or ₹1 lakh.
    - [Link](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf). Confidence: **high**.
  - **IT Act s.72A:** a service provider under a lawful contract who discloses personal information without consent or in breach of contract, intending or knowing it will cause wrongful loss or gain, faces up to 3 years or ₹5 lakh. DPDP does not repeal it. [Secondary text](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/69360334/); I could not reach India Code. Confidence: **high**.
  - **IT Act s.43A and the SPDI Rules** until they end in May 2027; see §5.
- **Aadhaar limits on the vendor:**
  - Offline verification **cannot be done on behalf of another entity** (Auth. Regs 2021, reg 16A(2)). The UIDAI OVSE FAQ repeats this.
  - So each hotel (legal entity) must be the OVSE itself. A vendor-run shared "verification service" is not allowed.
  - Confidence: **medium**. It is untested how far a vendor can host the hotel's OVSE callback as the hotel's processor; see "Unresolved".

## 4. Aadhaar: can a hotel collect and store the full 12-digit number?

**Short answer: No.**
- A hotel that checks a guest's Aadhaar (card, photocopy, e-Aadhaar, QR code or the app) is an "offline verification-seeking entity" (OVSE).
- Section 8A(4)(b) of the Act says an OVSE shall not "collect, use, or store an Aadhaar number or biometric information of any individual for any purpose".
- A hotel may keep **masked** Aadhaar data (last 4 digits only), with consent.
- A hotel may not make Aadhaar the only ID it accepts.

Details:

- **Using Aadhaar is voluntary (s.4(3), as substituted by Act 14 of 2019 from 25 Jul 2019).** A holder "may voluntarily use" it by authentication or offline verification.
  - An OVSE must tell the person the alternatives (s.8A(3)(c)).
  - **No service may be refused** if the person can identify themselves another viable way (Auth. Regs 2021, reg 5(1)(c) and 5(3)).
  - [Act](https://backend.uidai.gov.in/get/files/media/document/2026-05/images_Aadhaar_Act_2016_English_0.pdf). Confidence: **high**.

- **Section 8A, the OVSE duties (inserted 2019):**
  - **Before** verifying, get consent from the person, or from a parent or guardian for a child.
  - Use the collected data **only** for the verification.
  - Tell the person what information is shared, what it will be used for, and what the alternatives are.
  - **Never** subject the person to authentication, and **never collect, use or store the Aadhaar number or biometrics**.
  - Misuse is an offence under s.40(b): up to 3 years, or a fine up to ₹10,000 (₹1 lakh for a company).
  - Civil penalty under s.33A: up to ₹1 crore per contravention, plus ₹10 lakh a day while it continues.
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **Sections 8 and 29:**
  - Section 8 governs **authentication** by *requesting entities* (AUA/KUA). A hotel is not one unless it is approved; see the Good Governance Rules below.
  - Section 29(3): identity information held by an OVSE may be used or disclosed only for the purposes **told to the person in writing**, in clear language.
  - Section 29(4): no Aadhaar number, demographic data or photograph may be published, displayed or posted publicly.
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **Section 57 is gone.**
  - In *K.S. Puttaswamy v Union of India*, (2019) 1 SCC 1, decided 26 Sep 2018, the Supreme Court struck down the part of s.57 that let a body corporate or person use Aadhaar under a **contract**. [Link](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/127517806/)
  - Parliament then **omitted s.57 entirely** (Act 14 of 2019, s.25, from 25 Jul 2019, S.O. 2649(E)).
  - A private hotel therefore has no general power to demand or use Aadhaar. It may only take part in the voluntary offline verification the Act allows.
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016, reg 6.**
  - [Secondary text](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/65276361/). The primary UIDAI and India Code copies were unreachable or scanned.
  - What it says:
    - (1) Never publish or display an Aadhaar number publicly.
    - (2) Anyone holding Aadhaar numbers must keep them and any database containing them **secure and confidential**.
    - (3) No database may be made public unless the numbers are redacted.
    - (4) Aadhaar numbers may be sent over the internet only if encrypted.
    - (5) **Do not keep Aadhaar numbers, or any document or database containing them, longer than needed for the purpose given when consent was obtained.**
  - Breaking it breaches s.29(2).
  - Confidence: **high** on content.

- **Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Regulations 2021**, gazette 9 Nov 2021, amended 2022, 2023, 2024 and on 9 Dec 2025.
  - The UIDAI copy of the full consolidated text is gone (old URL returns 404). I used [Indian Kanoon's text](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/95940041/). The 2025 amendment is from the UIDAI primary PDF.
  - **reg 2(1)(mc):** "Offline Aadhaar Data" includes "the requirement of masking Aadhaar numbers before storing".
  - **reg 3A** (as substituted in 2025): the types of offline verification are:
    - QR code;
    - Paperless Offline e-KYC;
    - Aadhaar Verifiable Credential (AVC);
    - e-Aadhaar;
    - **offline paper-based verification**;
    - each with or without offline face verification.
    - So checking a physical card or photocopy **is** offline verification.
  - **reg 5 and 6:** inform the person, get consent (or a parent's or guardian's consent for a child), and **keep records of that consent**, preferably electronically.
  - **reg 10(2):** the OVSE must **tell the holder about each offline verification**, by email, SMS, other digital means or a paper acknowledgement.
  - **reg 14A(1):**
    - (b) must not collect, use or store the Aadhaar number or biometrics;
    - (d) **tell UIDAI within 72 hours** of learning of misuse or compromise of Aadhaar-related information;
    - (e) the OVSE stays responsible even if it subcontracts.
  - **reg 16A:**
    - (1) use the data only for the purpose given;
    - (2) **no verification on behalf of another entity**;
    - (3) the OVSE may store offline Aadhaar data **with consent**, securely, following UIDAI guidelines;
    - (4) **if consent is withdrawn, delete verifiably and acknowledge the deletion**.
  - **reg 20A:** optional verification logs, with consent, but "shall not, in any event, store the Aadhaar number or Virtual ID".
  - **reg 14(1)(mb):** for *requesting entities*, Aadhaar numbers on physical forms or photocopies must have the **first 8 digits redacted** before copies are stored. Masked Aadhaar shows only the last 4 digits.
  - Confidence: **high** on content, **medium** on the secondary copy of the consolidated text.

- **The 2025 amendment, notified 9 Dec 2025 (F. No. HQ-30011/5/2025-AU-HO), in force from publication.** [UIDAI PDF](https://backend.uidai.gov.in/get/files/media/document/2026-05/Regulation.pdf)
  - It adds definitions of "Aadhaar Application" (Aadhaar App, mAadhaar, QR Scanner, myAadhaar) and **"Aadhaar Verifiable Credential"**. An AVC may contain only the **last 4 digits** plus name, address, gender, date of birth and photo.
  - It adds "Offline Face Verification".
  - **New reg 13A:** an entity must **register with UIDAI as an OVSE** to use **Paperless Offline e-KYC or AVC verification through the Aadhaar Application**. It must use it only for lawful purposes, and UIDAI may charge fees.
  - **QR-code, e-Aadhaar and paper-based verification do not need registration.**
  - New reg 23A covers surrender, with preservation of logs and records. Regulation 25(1A) allows penalties on OVSEs.
  - The UIDAI OVSE page (published 13 May 2026, updated 18 Sep 2026) names **"Hospitality and Tourism"** as the first use case and gives the technical requirements:
    - SVG logo;
    - callback URL;
    - domain;
    - Class 2 or 3 certificate valid for 2 years;
    - app ID for app-to-app calls.
    - Applications go through ovse.uidai.gov.in.
    - [Page](https://uidai.gov.in/en/ovse)
  - Its FAQ says a registered OVSE **may store verifiable credentials with explicit consent**.
  - Status: **in force**. Confidence: **high**.

- **UIDAI circular of 30 Apr 2026** says AVCs shown, shared or verified through the Aadhaar App are a **legally valid proof of identity**. The **12-digit number is not transferred**.
  - [PDF](https://backend.uidai.gov.in/get/files/media/document/2026-07/Circular_30_04_2026_Validity_of_Aadhaar_Verifiable_Credential_shown_shared_andor_verified_through_Aadhaar_Application.pdf)
  - Confidence: **high** on the content. The circular number cannot be read in the scan, and the date is taken from its file name.

- **The "ban on photocopies" is announced but not law.**
  - In early December 2025 the UIDAI CEO said a rule barring hotels and event organisers from collecting or keeping Aadhaar photocopies, and requiring them to register, "has been approved". Sources: [Biometric Update 9 Dec 2025](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202512/india-to-ban-aadhaar-photocopying-as-uidai-moves-to-regulate-private-sector-verification); [MediaNama 8 Dec 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-explained-uidais-new-rule-aadhaar-based-verification/).
  - The regulation actually notified on **9 Dec 2025** only requires registration for e-KYC and AVC through the app. It **keeps paper-based verification** as a permitted type.
  - Business Today (30 Aug 2026) still calls the photocopy ban a proposal. [Link](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/do-you-still-need-aadhaar-for-hotel-check-ins-heres-what-is-changing-552102-2026-08-30)
  - Status: **not in force** on 25 Sep 2026.
  - A hotel keeping photocopies with the **full number unmasked** is already exposed under s.8A(4)(b), reg 14A(1)(b) and reg 6(5) of the Sharing Regulations.
  - Confidence: **medium**. I found no instrument; a notice could exist that I missed.

- **Aadhaar Data Vault (ADV).**
  - The 2017 circular (No. 11020/205/2017, 25 Jul 2017) has been **replaced by UIDAI Circular No. 14 of 2025 (4 Nov 2025)**. [PDF](https://uidai.gov.in/images/Circular-No.14_of-2025.pdf). The FAQ was updated 3 Nov 2025. [PDF](https://uidai.gov.in/images/FAQs_Aadhaar_Data_Vault_03112025_v10.pdf)
  - The ADV duty applies to **requesting entities** (AUA/KUA/Sub-AUA) that store full Aadhaar numbers received from **e-KYC responses**. The requirements are:
    - encryption with AES-256 or stronger;
    - HSM at FIPS 140-2 Level 3;
    - reference keys (hashes are not allowed);
    - hosting on-premises, on a MeitY-empanelled Government Community Cloud, or as ADV-as-a-service;
    - an annual SOC 2 Type II audit when hosted in the cloud.
  - Numbers typed in by the holder for authentication **may not be stored at all**.
  - An **OVSE is not subject to ADV**, because it may not store the number at all. This follows the UIDAI OVSE FAQ of 11 Jul 2024, which I only saw through [Khaitan's note of 10 Dec 2025](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-12/ERGO%20-%20Aadhaar%20Data%20Vault%20-%2010%20December%202025.pdf); the UIDAI URL returns 404.
  - Confidence: **high** for the circular, **medium** for the OVSE point.

- **Good Governance Rules (the route to full authentication).** MeitY notified the Aadhaar Authentication for Good Governance (Social Welfare, Innovation, Knowledge) Amendment Rules 2025 on **31 Jan 2025**, under s.4(4)(b)(ii).
  - **Non-government entities** can now be allowed Aadhaar **authentication** for e-commerce, travel, **tourism, hospitality**, health and similar services.
  - The entity applies through a portal to the relevant Central or State ministry. UIDAI examines it, MeitY approves, and the ministry **notifies** the entity.
  - Sources: [PIB 31 Jan 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2098223); [UIDAI press release PDF](https://uidai.gov.in/images/Press_Release-Aadhaar_Authentication_Good_Governace.pdf)
  - A hotel approved this way becomes a requesting entity. It must then meet the AUA/KUA duties and ADV rules, or the new Sub-AUA/Sub-KUA "LITE" framework for up to 5 lakh transactions a year (UIDAI Circular 9 of 2026, **16 Sep 2026**). [PDF](https://backend.uidai.gov.in/get/files/media/document/2026-09/Circular-9-of-2026-regarding-Sub-AUA-Sub-KUA-LITE-Framework.pdf)
  - I did not read the gazette text of the 2025 amendment rules; I relied on the PIB and UIDAI releases.
  - Confidence: **high** on the PIB facts, **medium** on details.

- **A May 2022 UIDAI (Bengaluru) advisory against sharing photocopies was withdrawn on 29 May 2022** ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/no-caution-just-routine-prudence-govt-withdraws-aadhaar-photocopy-warning-after-netizens-flay-uidai-advisory-399245)). It is context only and not a binding rule. Confidence: **medium**.

## 5. IT Act s.43A and the SPDI Rules 2011 until May 2027

- **Section 43A is removed by DPDP s.44(2)(a) on 13 or 14 May 2027.** The same subsection also removes s.87(2)(ob), the power the SPDI Rules were made under, so the SPDI Rules lose their legal basis on that date.
  - Until then, **s.43A is in force**: a body corporate that is negligent with reasonable security practices for sensitive personal data, causing wrongful loss or gain, must pay compensation.
  - Source: DPDP s.44(2) and G.S.R. 843(E) para (c).
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **SPDI Rules, G.S.R. 313(E), 11 Apr 2011.** [Gazette copy on DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/sites/default/files/in098en.pdf); the MeitY URL is dead.
  - **r.3: what counts as sensitive personal data.** Passwords, financial information (bank, card or payment details), health, sexual orientation, medical records and **biometrics**. **An Aadhaar number or passport number is not SPDI** in itself; card data and biometrics are.
  - **r.4:** publish a **privacy policy** on the website. It must cover practices, the types of data, the purpose, disclosures, and the security practices under r.8.
  - **r.5 (collection):**
    - **written consent**, by letter, fax or email (the PIB clarification says any electronic means is enough), before collecting sensitive data;
    - collect only what is lawful and necessary;
    - tell the person the purpose, the recipients, and who collects and who keeps the data;
    - **keep it no longer than required**;
    - let people review and correct their data;
    - offer the choice not to provide it, and the right to withdraw consent;
    - a **Grievance Officer** named on the website, who resolves complaints **within one month**.
  - **r.6:** disclosing sensitive data to third parties needs prior permission, unless the contract allows it or law requires it. Government agencies can receive it on written request. Sensitive data must not be published.
  - **r.7:** transfers only to a recipient with the same level of protection, and only if needed for the contract or with consent.
  - **r.8 (security):** a documented information security programme and policies. **IS/ISO/IEC 27001** is named as one acceptable standard. The deemed-compliance route needs an audit by an independent auditor **at least once a year** or on significant upgrades.
  - Status: in force until May 2027. Confidence: **high**.

- **MeitY clarification, press note of 24 Aug 2011** ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?relid=74990)).
  - A body corporate handling sensitive data **under a contract with a legal entity**, such as a PMS vendor for a hotel, is **not bound by r.5 and r.6**. It remains bound by r.4 and r.8.
  - The hotel, which deals directly with the guest, is bound by all of them.
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **For a PMS vendor, card data is a separate issue.** RBI's card-on-file tokenisation rules stop merchants storing full card numbers since 1 Oct 2022. I did not research this here and it needs a separate check. Confidence: **medium**, not verified.

## 6. Sector rules on keeping guest registers and ID copies

- **Foreign guests: Form III (formerly Form C) under the Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, s.8.**
  - The Act is No. 13 of 2025, gazette 4 Apr 2025, reported in force from **1 Sep 2025**.
  - The keeper of an accommodation must submit foreigners' information to the Registration Officer "in such manner as may be prescribed". Every foreign guest must give the keeper the particulars asked for.
  - Penalty under s.23(b): up to 3 years or ₹3 lakh.
  - [MHA PDF](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/Immigration_and_Foreigners_Act_2025_16092025.pdf)
  - Secondary sources say the **Immigration and Foreigners Rules 2025 (1 Sep 2025), r.17**, require **Form III online within 24 hours** of arrival and a departure report. I did not read the Rules in primary form.
  - **I found no retention period** for the hotel's own copy.
  - Confidence: **high** for s.8; **medium** for r.17 and the 24 hours; **low** that no retention period exists.

- **Indian guests: the police register.**
  - The Sarais Act 1867 covers registration and inspection of sarais. [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19045/1/a1867-22.pdf)
  - Guest-register and police-upload duties are mostly **state or city orders**: state police acts, commissioner orders, or state Sarais Act amendments.
  - **I found no central rule fixing a retention period for guest registers or ID copies.**
  - Confidence: **medium**.

- **GST: CGST Act s.36.** Books of account and records must be kept **until 72 months after the due date of the annual return** for that year. If there are proceedings, keep them one year after final disposal, whichever is later. [CBIC](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter8/section36_v1.00.html)
  - This covers invoices and accounts, which include guest names and GSTINs. It does **not** by itself justify keeping ID copies.
  - Confidence: **high**.

- **How these fit with DPDP.** The s.8(7) and s.12(3) duty to erase gives way to "retention necessary for compliance with any law".
  - Keep each **category** of data only for the period its own law requires:
    - invoices: 72 months under GST;
    - Form III data and police registers: whatever the local order says;
    - logs and processed data: at least one year under r.8(3) and r.6(1)(e).
  - ID images with no legal basis for retention should be erased once the stay and reporting are complete.
  - Aadhaar numbers must never be stored in full (§4).
  - Confidence: **medium**.

## Checklist: what a hotel PMS must support under DPDP (in time for 13 May 2027)

1. **Notice and consent capture at booking and check-in:**
   - an itemised data list, the purposes and the services;
   - links for withdrawal, rights and complaints to the Board;
   - English or any Eighth Schedule language;
   - separate, unticked consents for marketing;
   - a **stored consent record** (who, when, which notice version) so consent can be proved under s.6(10);
   - a record of which legal basis applies to each data item (consent, s.7(a), s.7(d)).
2. **Withdrawal of consent** as easy as giving it. It must reach processors and integrations such as the channel manager, CRM and email tool (s.6(4)-(6)).
3. **Rights handling:**
   - access export, including a **list of every fiduciary and processor the data was shared with** (s.11(1)(b));
   - correction and update;
   - erasure with a check for legal holds;
   - nominees;
   - a request tracker with a **90-day** SLA timer (r.14);
   - the published contact person inserted in every reply (r.9).
4. **Retention engine:**
   - retention periods per data category;
   - legal hold;
   - automatic erasure or anonymisation once the purpose ends (s.8(7));
   - **at least 1 year** of data, traffic data and logs (r.8(3), r.6(1)(e));
   - erasure carried through to backups and processors (s.8(7)(b)).
5. **Security (r.6):**
   - encryption at rest and in transit;
   - masking and tokenisation of ID numbers;
   - role-based access with least privilege;
   - **access and audit logs of who viewed or exported guest data**, kept at least 1 year;
   - monitoring and alerting;
   - tested backups and restore.
6. **Breach workflow:**
   - notices to guests through their registered channel with the r.7(1) contents;
   - an immediate Board notice and the **72-hour** report template (r.7(2));
   - alongside it the **CERT-In 6-hour** report and, for Aadhaar-related compromise, the **UIDAI 72-hour** report (reg 14A(1)(d)).
7. **Children:** an age or minor flag, the linked parent or guardian and their consent, verification against the parent's ID (r.10), and **no marketing or profiling of minors** (s.9(3)).
8. **Aadhaar:**
   - **never store the full 12-digit number**;
   - keep only the masked number (last 4 digits) or the AVC or QR output, with consent;
   - use the Aadhaar App / QR / AVC verification, with the hotel's own OVSE registration (reg 13A);
   - a consent record (reg 6);
   - a notification to the guest after each verification (reg 10(2));
   - verifiable deletion on withdrawal (reg 16A(4));
   - always offer other ID types (s.4(3) and s.8A(3)(c));
   - **no Aadhaar number in scans, logs, exports or search indexes.**
9. **Foreign guests:** passport and visa fields for Form III, a 24-hour reminder, and a record of each submission. Treat this as a s.7(d) legal duty with its own retention period.
10. **Published privacy page** covering the r.9 contact, the r.14 request method and the grievance timeline. Until May 2027 it must also meet the r.4 privacy policy contents of the SPDI Rules.

## What a hotel-vendor data processing agreement must contain

1. **Subject, purpose and instructions:** the vendor processes only on the hotel's documented instructions. It makes **no use for its own purposes** (otherwise it becomes a fiduciary) and does no sale or analytics without written permission.
2. **Security** (r.6(1)(f) and r.6(1)(a)-(e),(g)):
   - encryption and masking;
   - access control on the vendor's own systems;
   - logs and monitoring;
   - backups and continuity;
   - staff confidentiality;
   - named standards, with ISO/IEC 27001 recommended (SPDI r.8 until May 2027);
   - audit and inspection rights, and an annual audit report.
3. **Breach notice to the hotel** within a set number of hours, which must be well inside 72 hours; 6 to 24 hours is recommended because of the CERT-In 6-hour clock. It must include everything the hotel needs for r.7(1)-(2), and the vendor must cooperate.
4. **CERT-In compliance by the vendor:** 6-hour incident reporting, 180-day logs in India, and NTP synchronisation.
5. **Carrying out rights and withdrawal:** the vendor must help with access, correction and erasure within set times so the hotel meets 90 days. It must stop processing on withdrawal (s.6(6)).
6. **Retention and erasure:**
   - keep data and logs for **at least 1 year** (r.8(3));
   - erase or return data when instructed and at the end of the contract (s.8(7)(b)), including backups;
   - certify the deletion.
7. **Sub-processors:** a list, prior approval or notice, and the same duties passed down. The vendor stays liable.
8. **Where data is stored and transferred:** the hosting country or countries; no transfer to countries restricted under s.16; meeting any r.15 orders; disclosing any request from a foreign government.
9. **Aadhaar clause:**
   - the vendor never stores the full Aadhaar number;
   - the hotel remains the OVSE;
   - the vendor does not verify "on behalf of" the hotel (reg 16A(2));
   - Aadhaar compromise is reported so the hotel can tell UIDAI within 72 hours.
10. **Confidentiality and a s.72A acknowledgement**, liability and indemnity, which should include regulatory penalties caused by the vendor's breach.
11. **Cooperation with the Board, CERT-In and UIDAI** on inquiries, and preservation of evidence.
12. **Records the vendor must keep** so the hotel can answer s.11(1)(b): processing activities and every onward disclosure.

## Unresolved, or sources disagree

1. **13 or 14 November?**
   - The gazette carries the date 13 Nov 2025. The gazette ID, digital signature and PIB all say 14 Nov.
   - Most law firms compute 13 Nov 2026 and 13 May 2027. AZB, and one dpdprules result, use 14 Nov and 14 May.
   - **Recommendation: treat the 13th as binding.**
2. **Does the Board exist in practice?** It is established by G.S.R. 844(E), but I found no evidence of members being appointed. One Wikipedia snippet names a chair without any source, and I disregarded it. No Board website resolves.
3. **Is the shortened timeline really dead?** Secondary sources to 6 Sep 2026 say it was not gazetted. A quiet notice after that cannot be ruled out. Check e-Gazette before relying on May 2027.
4. **Child guests.** Section 9(1) reads as covering *all* processing of a child's data, with no carve-out for s.7 legitimate uses. There is no Fourth Schedule exemption for hospitality. How strictly the Board will read this is unknown.
5. **Legal basis for Indian guests' ID.** Whether s.7(d), a duty to disclose to the State, covers city police register orders, or only s.7(a) applies, is untested.
6. **Is a hotel that takes a photocopy an "OVSE"?** Regulation 3A lists "offline paper-based verification", which suggests yes, and then s.8A(4)(b) forbids storing the number. The photocopy ban announced in December 2025 implies UIDAI thought new rules were needed.
7. **Can a vendor host a hotel's OVSE integration?** Regulation 16A(2) bans verification "on behalf of another entity". Whether a processor hosting the hotel's callback URL counts is unclear. Ask UIDAI (ovse.registration@uidai.net.in) before building a shared service.
8. **Primary texts I could not reach:**
   - the consolidated 2021 Authentication Regulations, the 2016 Sharing Regulations and the 2024 OVSE FAQ: UIDAI's old URLs return 404 and India Code timed out, so I used Indian Kanoon and Khaitan;
   - the SPDI Rules on meity.gov.in: I used a gazette copy on DataGuidance;
   - the Immigration and Foreigners Rules 2025: secondary only.
9. **Retention periods for Form III copies and police registers:** none found centrally. They may exist in state orders.
10. **Rule 8(3)** requires *at least* a year of personal data and logs for State and SDF-assessment purposes. How this sits with erasure on request is not explained. Read it as a hold that overrides erasure for 1 year.
