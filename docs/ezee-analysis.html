The number that changes the commercial case

This property has 17 rooms, not 25

The Stay View header reads All 17 · Vacant 16 · Occupied 1 · Dirty 9, and Total Rooms on the Rates screen confirms 17 across every date.

Every commercial figure I have produced assumed 175 rooms across seven properties, from an average of 25. If 17 is closer to typical, the group is nearer 120 rooms — and the STAAH quote at ₹50 per room per month becomes about ₹6,000 a month rather than ₹8,750, or ₹72,000 a year rather than ₹1.05 lakh.

That makes the channel-manager saving larger, not smaller, since the current cost is per property. But the stakeholder document currently states 175 rooms and needs correcting. Please confirm the real room count at each of the seven.

The rate-threshold numbers give away the tax model

Those thresholds are oddly precise: 1,904.76 · 2,475.24 · 5,713.33 · 11,238.10 · 33,714.29.

1,904.76 × 1.05 = 2,000.00. 11,238.10 × 1.05 = 11,800. These are tax-exclusive values derived from round tax-inclusive intentions — somebody thought "minimum ₹2,000 including tax" and the system stored the exclusive figure.

That confirms the GST slabs in play and tells us the UI must let a manager enter either and see both. Storing only one is how a rate ends up 5% wrong on an OTA.

The commercial dimension we are missing entirely

Cashiering holds Business Source, Market Segment, Sales Person Database and Travel Agent Database — and the Guest Statistics dashboard reports Top Business Source Analysis broken down by makemytrip and Booking.com.

Our schema has reservations.source as a five-value CHECK constraint. That is not enough to answer "which channel, which agent and which segment produced this revenue", which is the question a group owner asks first. It needs to be a set of related tables, not an enum.

Cash Drawer and Expense Voucher — the shift is missing

A front desk operates in shifts. Cash goes in, petty cash goes out, and at handover the drawer is counted and reconciled against what the system says should be there.

Nothing in our model represents a shift, a drawer, or a cash-out. Without it there is no way to establish which clerk was accountable for a discrepancy — and that is the single most common source of loss in a hotel.

Statuses we do not have

The Reports menu names reservation states our CHECK constraint does not allow:

  • Void — distinct from cancelled. A void never happened; a cancellation happened and was undone. They report differently, and conflating them corrupts cancellation analysis
  • Release — a held booking that lapsed rather than being cancelled by anyone
  • Voucher number, carried alongside the reservation number. Two identifiers, because the OTA issues one and the property issues the other

                  'void',      -- struck from the record
                  'released'   -- a hold that lapsed

What this changes for Stage 1

The interface stage, which is what the builder starts on.

The Stay View is the product. It is the screen a front desk lives in all day, and these screenshots show exactly what it must carry: the filter pills across the top (All / Vacant / Occupied / Reserved / Blocked / Due Out / Dirty), room types collapsible with per-date availability and rate in the same cell, booking bars spanning dates with source icons, and the Available Inventory and Occupancy strip pinned to the bottom.

Density is the aesthetic. This is fourteen days across seventeen rooms on one screen with no scrolling, and that is not an accident — it is what a duty manager needs. Our design system already says "dense tables, quiet rows"; these screens are what that means in practice.

Two per-room icons sit beside every room number in the Stay View, before any booking. Those carry room-level flags — likely notes and maintenance. Worth confirming what they are before building the row.

Use these as the functional reference, and the CRS as the visual one

eZee shows what a screen must contain. It does not show what it should look like — that comes from brand/, and the two should not be confused.

Where eZee and the Fidato design system disagree on appearance, the design system wins. Where they disagree on what information a screen carries, eZee wins, because it is running a real property today.
