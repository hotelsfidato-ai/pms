Chapter A1

The front desk day

The screens reception lives in: the Dashboard to see the day, Arrivals to receive guests, In house and Due out to look after them and send them off, Rooms and Calendar to see the whole house, and a guide to the whole Front desk part of the menu (A1.10).

A1.1The business date

The business date is the hotel's working day. It is not always today's calendar date: it moves on only when the night audit closes the day (chapter A7). Every “today” in the system means the business date: today's arrivals, who is due out today, the Dashboard's Today figures.

The Dashboard, Rooms and Calendar show it above the heading: “Business date · Sunday, 4 October 2026”.

When the business date falls behind the real date, a yellow line appears under the top bar of every screen, for example: “Business date 11 Sep, 21 days behind today (2 Oct). Close the days in Night audit.” If you may run the night audit, Night audit in the line is a link to it.

Watch out

  • “Today” for this line is the real date at the hotel, from the server. It never comes from your computer's clock.
  • When the night audit is run on another desk, your screens pick up the new business date the next time you open a screen. Open any screen before you start the new day's work.

Why it works this way

One warning line on every screen while the date is behind: D27-237 (4.2), against the recommendation of showing it on the calendar only. Other devices kept yesterday's date after an audit until this was fixed: D27-89.

A1.2The Dashboard

The Dashboard shows the day at one look. Open it with Dashboard at the top of the menu. Read it from the top.

Today's work: four tiles

Each tile counts one job and opens its list. Its colour says what to do: orange, act now; yellow, due today; white with a green tick, done; white, nothing to do.

The figures

  • Occupancy (the dark card): today's share of rooms sold, then Month so far and Year so far. The year runs from 1 April.
  • Average rate (ARR), RevPAR and Room revenue: today in large figures. Under each, Last 14 days (a small line, from the day 13 days ago to today, today marked with a dark dot), then the month so far and the year so far. Point at the line, or tap it, to read a day. A night with no room sold has no average rate, so the Average rate line breaks there: it is a gap, never a zero.
  • Narrower than 1440 pixels wide, Occupancy has a row of its own and the three figures sit under it, three across from 768 pixels wide. Narrower than that (a phone, or a small tablet standing up) each figure is a block: its name and today's figure on a line, then Last 14 days, Month so far and Year so far side by side.
  • A word with a dotted underline explains itself: click or tap it. The small (i) beside a heading explains the whole card.

Occupancy by day, and Revenue

Under the figures, two cards side by side (one under the other on a narrower screen).

  • Occupancy by day: one bar for every night of the month, from the same figures as the Monthly statistics report. Nights up to today are in the blue of In house; nights after today are the same blue, paler, and show what is booked so far. Today's day number is in a dark mark, and today's share is written above its bar. Saturdays and Sundays are shaded. Every other night is read by pointing at its bar (or tapping it): a small box gives the share, the day and the rooms, with “booked so far” for a night to come. Press Tab to reach the chart, and the left and right arrow keys then move from night to night. Under the chart, Nights so far and Booked ahead say which blue is which; the key is left out when the month has only one kind of night.
  • Revenue (Without tax): the month so far, then a bar split into room revenue, extra revenue and each restaurant, with each part's share and amount. The bar is dark navy and greys, so no colour means anything else. Under it, Today and Year so far. The figures are the Manager report's Revenue Summary.

Nights ahead

Tonight and the 13 nights after it. Under each date: a bar, the share of rooms sold and “sold / rooms to sell”. Tonight is dark; Saturdays and Sundays are shaded. A night with no figures shows a dash. Click any night to open the Calendar.

Money

  • Collected today: payments taken today, by method, with a small bar under them that splits the day's total by method (navy, then greys). “Nothing taken yet today” before the first.
  • Owed by guests, less refunds: what every open guest bill still owes.
  • City ledger: what companies and booking sites owe on their accounts, with “Paid today ₹…”.
  • No-shows: today's, with the month so far. Click it for the No show report.

The house now, and quick reports

The house now draws every room as a small square in the Rooms colours: In house, Due out, Arriving, Free, Out of service. The line beside the heading gives the rooms, how many are empty and how many are out of service. Under it, Quick reports: Manager report, Monthly statistics, Lodging tax, City ledger, No show.

Watch out

  • A dash (-) in a money figure means the system does not hold that figure yet. It is never a zero. A count, such as arrivals, can be a real 0.
  • If the figures could not be read, a red box says “Today's figures did not load” with Try again. The dashes under it then mean “not loaded”, not “nothing”.
  • Housekeeping, the kitchen manager, the chef, the Inventory Manager and the waiter have no Dashboard. The Revenue Manager sees it, but not what departing guests owe.
  • A brand-new system with no property shows “No property yet” and an Add a property button.
  • The Dashboard's figures come from the same place as the Manager report, for the same business date, so the two always agree.
  • If the month's figures could not be read, Occupancy by day says “did not load” with Try again, and the Last 14 days rows show a dash. A dash there means “not loaded”, not “no figure”.

Why it works this way

The page is called Dashboard and carries quick reports: the owner, 14 September. Built as sketched, with today's four tiles first, colours by what staff must do, “ARR” on screens, Nights ahead and the Due out tile showing what departing guests owe: D27-243. No Dashboard for the working roles: D27-141. Money hidden from roles that do not handle bills: D27-143, D27-147. A fresh system asks for a property first: D27-248. The charts, the calendar month, the paler blue for nights to come, a gap and never a zero, Revenue leading with the month, navy-and-grey split bars, and Occupancy on its own row under 1440 pixels: D27-217 to D27-219, D27-365.

A1.3Arrivals

Who is coming today. Check-in starts here.

  1. In the menu, under Front desk, click Arrivals. It opens on Today.
  2. The line under the heading says “Expected on Sunday, 4 October 2026. Check in from the row - the panel opens over this list.” The pill beside the heading says how many are expected.
  3. To find one guest, type part of the name, the booking reference or the room number in Guest, reference or room…. The count on the right shows how many rows are left, for example “1 of 6”. Clear empties the box.
  4. Click the guest's row. The stay panel opens over the list (A1.4).
  5. Click Check in, the orange button at the top of the panel. Chapter A3 goes on from here.

The next fortnight

Click Upcoming beside Today (the same word as in the menu, under Arrivals). It lists bookings arriving from tomorrow to 14 days ahead, without cancelled or void ones (neither list counts them). It is for planning: a row opens the panel, but this list does not offer Check in.

The columns

Room, Guest (with the booking reference under the name), Type, Plan, Source, Departure, Nights (“Day use” for a room used within one day), Status and Rate per night (“Comp” when complimentary). On a narrower screen Plan and Source are hidden, then Type. A source the system does not know shows a dash; it is never guessed.

StatusMeans
Due inExpected, not yet checked in
In houseChecked in
Due outChecked in and leaving today
Checked outGone
No show · CancelledNever came · cancelled
Void · Waiting for the hotelVoided · held until the hotel confirms it (chapter A2)

Watch out

  • A red line above the list such as “… has the phone or email of …, who is blacklisted. Reason: …. Check-in is not refused - you decide.” means a booking site's guest matches a blacklisted guest. The system warns; it does not stop you.
  • An empty Room means no room is given yet. You give one at check-in (A3).
  • Check in is shown only to people who may check guests in. Housekeeping and the Revenue Manager see the list without it.
  • Nobody expected? “No arrivals today. Nobody is expected. A walk-in can be taken from any empty cell on the Calendar.”

Why it works this way

The blacklist warns and shows the reason, a person decides: D21-21, and the warning on Arrivals and at check-in: D22-8. A control is shown only to a role the server lets use it: D24-27.

Watch out

  • People who do not handle bills (the Revenue Manager, housekeeping, the kitchen manager, the till) see no Bill tab and no totals at the foot, and the guest's phone and email are blank for them.
  • No More options at all? Your role may not change bookings.

Why it works this way

One panel instead of two copies that drifted apart, rebuilt to the owner's reference layout on 14 September: the act at the top, what is owed always in view. The amount due counts tonight's room charge before the night audit posts it: D27-199. The first night is posted at check-in, and the night audit never posts it twice: D27-204. Bills and guest contacts hidden from roles that read bookings only: D27-143. Each act offered only to a role the server allows: D24-27.

A1.5In house

Every guest checked in now.

  1. In the menu, under Front desk, click In house. The heading says “Guests checked in now, on Sunday, 4 October 2026.” The pill counts the rooms, for example “11 rooms”.
  2. The switch at the top right has In house now (this list) and Due out (A1.6), each with its count.
  3. Click a guest to open the panel. For a guest who is leaving, Settle and check out takes you to their bill.

The columns are Room, Guest, Type, Plan, Arrival, Departure, Status and Balance. The balance is coloured:

BalanceMeans
₹… in redThe guest owes this
₹… over, in blueThe guest has paid more than the charges so far
₹0 in greenSettled
-No bill yet, or you do not see bills

Watch out

  • The pill counts rooms. The list can have more rows than rooms: a guest due out who has not left yet and the guest arriving into the same room are two rows for one room.
  • A guest stays “in house” until they are checked out, even after their departure date has passed. A late check-out still counts.
  • Empty list: “Nobody in house. The house is empty tonight.”

Why it works this way

“In house” means checked in and not yet checked out, the same on every screen: D17-10, D21-36. A stay with no bill shows a dash, never ₹0: D17-16.

A1.6Departures (Due out)

The guests leaving today, and what they still owe.

  1. Open it from the menu (In house, then Due out), from the Dashboard's Due out tile, or with the switch on In house.
  2. The heading says “Departing Sunday, 4 October 2026. Check-out unlocks when the bill reaches zero.”
  3. If anyone still owes money, a red box at the top says so, for example “2 departures still owing ₹…”, with “Settle from the Bill tab. Check-out stays disabled with its reason until each balance clears.”
  4. Click a guest. On this list the panel opens straight on the Bill tab.
  5. Take the payment, then check the guest out. Chapters A4 and A5 go on from here.

If the guest leaves after check-out time

The hotel's check-out time is set on Times and rules (A17.11), for example 11:00. A guest who leaves on their departure date, or after it, later than that is a late departure, and it is written down. The window comes up when you click Check out, not before.

  1. Click Check out on the guest's Bill tab. If it is a late departure, the window Leaving after check-out time opens, with the guest and the room. Its yellow note starts “Check-out here is 11:00.”
  2. Type Why. It cannot be left empty: a flight time, a manager's say-so, a room not ready on arrival.
  3. Read the line under it. Where the hotel charges for a late departure: “₹1,000 will be added to the folio. A manager can waive it - that is a decision about money and it is theirs to make.” Where it does not: “This property does not charge for a late departure, so nothing will be added. It is still recorded.” The owner, the GM and a group administrator instead have a tick, Waive the ₹1,000 charge: their decision, recorded against their name, and the late departure is still written down.
  4. Click Check out. The late check-out charge, with its GST, goes on the bill under Room before the balance is looked at. If that leaves something owed, the window closes with “Recorded - now settle the bill” and the amount. Settle it on the Bill tab (A4.7), then click Check out again.

The second click does not ask again and charges nothing more. A late departure is recorded once for a stay: its reason, its charge or its waiver.

A guest who leaves before the departure date is leaving early, whatever the hour: the window never comes up and no late fee is charged. The date that counts is the hotel's business date (A1.1), not the clock's.

Watch out

  • Check-out stays greyed out, with its reason, until the balance is zero. This is on purpose.
  • An early departure: chapter A5.
  • Nobody leaving: “Nobody departing today. Every room stays another night.”

Why it works this way

The Due out tile and list show what departing guests still owe, to the people who see money: D27-243, D27-143. Reception's card puts it in one line: “Open the guest, go to Bill - Check out opens once nothing is owed.” (Start here, the owner, 15 September.) A late departure is asked only on or after the departure date and recorded once, so a guest who paid after it is not asked or charged again: D27-314.

A1.7Rooms: the house today

Every room as a tile, for today. It answers three questions at a glance: is it free, who is in it, and what happens today.

  1. Click Rooms near the top of the menu. The heading shows the business date and the hotel's name.
  2. Read the row of count tiles. Click one to show only those rooms; the chosen tile turns dark. Click Sellable to see every room again.
  3. Click a tile with a guest's name to open their panel (A1.4).
  4. Click a tile with no guest on it, such as a Free one, to start a booking for that room, today: New booking opens with the room and the date filled in. This is how a walk-in is taken (chapter A2).

  • With no connection, the last copy stays on screen with its time: “No connection - these rooms are as they were at 4:10 pm.” Check before you sell a room shown as Free (A0.8). If there is no copy at all: “No connection. This device is offline, so the rooms cannot load.”
  • Housekeeping sees Rooms but no Assign room, and a Free tile does not open a booking for them. The chef and the Inventory Manager have no Rooms.
  • A hotel with no rooms yet shows “No rooms set up yet. Add them in Room types.”

Why it works this way

Rooms and Calendar are two screens with their own menu entries; Room type or Floor remembered per person: the owner, 14 September. “Sellable” counts sellable rooms, as the Manager report does: D27-164. “Empty” means nobody in it and sellable tonight: D21-37. A free room opens the one-page New booking: D27-163. Controls a role cannot use are hidden: D24-18, D24-27. Rooms needs bookings access: D27-142. Rooms kept for a reload with the server down: D22-23.

A1.8Calendar: the fortnight chart

Fourteen nights across, every room down. It is for planning: who is where, and what is free on which night.

  1. Click Calendar near the top of the menu. It starts on the business date.
  2. Move a week at a time with the arrows ‹ and › (Previous week, Next week). Today brings you back.
  3. Point at a booking's bar to see the guest, the dates and nights, the price per night (or “Complimentary”) and, if you see bills, the balance.
  4. Click a bar to open the stay panel (A1.4).
  5. Click an empty cell to start a booking for that room on that night: New booking opens with both filled in (chapter A2).

Reading the chart

  • The dates run across the top. The business date's column is dark. Weekends and the hotel's holidays are shaded; point at a holiday's date to see its name.
  • Each room type has a grey row. Under each night: how many of its rooms are still free (red at 0), and the room type's rack rate in thousands (for example “4.5k”), or a dash when none is set. It is the same rack rate on every night. Grouped by floor, the rate is always a dash: a floor has no one rate.
  • Each room has a row: its number, then two small marks: a sparkle (yellow = dirty, grey = clean) and a spanner (red = out of service).
  • The foot of the chart: Available rooms and Occupancy for each night, with a small orange bar.
  • The same count tiles as Rooms sit above the chart and filter it the same way.

The bars

Each bar starts with a small mark for where the booking came from: a person for a walk-in, a building for Fidato Hotels, a globe for anything else or not known.

BarMeans

A hotel can choose its own colour for each of these in Settings. If yours looks different, that is why.

Watch out

  • While the next week loads, or with no connection, the last fortnight loaded stays on screen, with a line such as “The fortnight from 11 Oct 2026 has not loaded.”
  • Someone who may not take bookings, such as housekeeping, can read the chart, but an empty cell does not open a booking for them.
  • Room types never fold away on the Calendar, on a desk or a phone.

Why it works this way

Room type groups always open: D27-237 (4.1). Each hotel picks its weekend days, for shading only: D27-243. Holidays shaded with their name, with no effect on price, and the hotel's own colours for each state: D27-260. The foot's occupancy is the Manager report's rule (complimentary in; no-shows, waiting bookings and day use out): D27-151. Out of service follows the dated block: D17-11.

Walk-ins

A record, not a form. The heading says “Guests taken at the desk without a booking.” with how many came today, and a count of those on record. Each row is a stay with source Walk-in: room, guest, type, plan, arrival, departure, nights, status and rate, newest arrival first, with Check in where it is still to do. Nothing is entered here. The box Take a walk-in from the Calendar says how: “Click any empty cell on the chart. The room and the night are already chosen by the time the form opens”, with Open Calendar beside it. Taking a walk-in is A2.5. With none yet: “No walk-ins on record”.

Online check-in

A guest checks in from their own page (A10.7 says how the link reaches them). What they send waits here for a person, in three parts.

  1. Details waiting to be checked. Each entry shows the guest, the booking's reference and when it was sent, then what they typed: ID type and number, a passport's expiry, a visa, the address. Look at the ID in your hand, then click Confirm against the ID: only then does it reach the guest's record and Form C, and the entry leaves the list with “Confirmed”. If it is not right, click Not right - take them at the desk and take the guest's details at check-in as usual. Empty: “Nothing waiting”.
  2. Requests. A spa treatment or a laundry bag a guest asked for, where the hotel has them switched on. The (i) says “Book them on the spa's or the laundry's own screen, then mark them done here.” This screen books nothing; click Done or Declined. Empty: “No requests”.
  3. Room cards. One card for each room, with a QR code. The (i) says “One card per room. A guest scans it and a code goes to the phone on their booking. Printing a new card stops the old one at once.” Each room shows “No card yet” or “Card printed” with its date. Click Print a card (or Print a new card), or Print cards for every room, which asks “Print cards for every room?” and warns “Every card already in a room stops working the moment this is pressed. Put the new ones out straight away.” The sheet then opens: “These cards are shown once. Print them now - they cannot be shown again, only replaced.” Click Print, then Done. Four cards fit an A4 page, each with the hotel, the room, the QR code and “Scan to check in.”

Watch out

  • Printing a card is making a card: the card is shown once, so the old one stops at the same moment. Print only when the card is to go into the room.
  • A guest's details are not on their record until somebody has confirmed them against the ID.

Why it works this way

Everything the guest sends is a submission that the desk confirms against the ID, under the clerk's name: D21-8. A request is confirmed by the desk and booked on the spa's or laundry's own screen: D21-9. One fixed card per room, finding whoever is checked in there now: D21-7; a reprint stops the old card at once: D21-16; the system prints a sheet and the owner chooses how it is printed: D21-62.
