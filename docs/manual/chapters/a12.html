Chapter A12

Approvals, cash expenses, notifications

For the general manager and the owner, who decide; for the Inventory Manager and anyone who asks for something; and for everyone who reads the bell. Who approves what, how to approve or refuse, what a cash expense waits for, what the person who asked sees, and what the bell and the Notifications screen tell you. Also the other screens in the System part of the menu.

A12.2Who approves what

Some things wait for a second person's yes before they count. Each one is a request: somebody asks, with a reason, and somebody else decides. Every request, whatever it is, goes to the same Approvals screen.

RequestRaised whenDecided by
Purchase requestSomebody asks to buy something on Purchasing (Ask to buy, A9). By default the Inventory Manager, the GM, the owner and an administrator may ask.The general manager, every purchase, whatever the amount.
Cash expenseMoney is paid out of a drawer: the front desk's (Cashiering) or a restaurant's tin (Close restaurant day). See A12.7.The general manager or the owner. Never an administrator.
Equipment leaving the storeEquipment goes out of a store to a room, the kitchen or the restaurant.The Inventory Manager; when they are away, the owner, an administrator or the GM.
Equipment being retiredA tagged item is retired by somebody who may not approve it, such as the Hotel Manager.The same as above.
  • Nobody approves their own request. A GM's own cash expense waits for the owner (Figure A12-04).
  • The group owner may decide any request.
  • A decision stays. An approved or refused request cannot be decided again. To go ahead after a refusal, raise a new request.
  • A refusal always has a note, so the person who asked knows what to do next. Approving needs none.

Why it works this way

The GM approves every purchase, with no amount above which the owner must: D18-4, confirming D17-35. Who may ask is a setting per hotel, changed without a new release: D18-2. Every cash expense needs a yes from the GM or the owner, never an administrator, never one's own: D27-239, D27-242. Equipment waits for the Inventory Manager, with the owner, an administrator or the GM instead: the owner, 16 September. Who decides is kept as a rule for each hotel, and a request keeps the rule it was raised under: a rule changed later never moves a request already waiting.

A12.3Approving or refusing

Open Approvals. The line under the title says “Everything waiting at this hotel, from every part of the system.” Waiting lists every request still waiting, newest first. Each card says what kind of request it is, what it is for in the asker's own words, the amount, who asked and when.

To decide a request

  1. Read the card. To see the whole request first (each line, the store it goes to), click its orange name, such as Purchase request.
  2. To approve: tap Approve. The message “Approved.” shows and the card leaves the Waiting list.
  3. To refuse: type why in A note (required to refuse), in one sentence, then tap Refuse. The message is “Refused, and they have been told why.”

What happens next belongs to the request. An approved purchase can be ordered from a supplier (A9). Approved equipment leaves the store. An approved cash expense is taken off what its drawer should hold (A12.7).

Watch out

  • Refuse with no note is not accepted: “Say why it is refused, so the person who asked knows what to do next.”
  • Approve and Refuse cannot be undone. Read the card before you tap.
  • A purchase request can also be decided on its own page, which has a third button, Send back, to ask for something to be cleared up. Approvals shows such a request as Asked for more.
  • The bell does not tell you a request is waiting. Open Approvals to see what waits on you.

Why it works this way

A GM with a purchase, two equipment requests and a cash expense waiting had to know to open four screens; now one screen shows them all, decided in place, while each service's own screen keeps its buttons: D18-19. Both make the same decision, so they never disagree. A refusal needs a reason because the person who asked has to know whether to buy it elsewhere, buy it cheaper, or not buy it.

A12.4A request that is not yours to decide

Approvals lists every request waiting at the hotel. Where you may not decide one, the card has no buttons, and a sentence says why.

A request that belongs to another role says whose it is instead, and waits for them. The GM's own cash expense in Figure A12-04 waits for the owner.

A12.5What was decided, and by whom

Tap Everything at the top right. Requests still waiting come first, then the decided ones, newest first. Each decided card says Approved or Refused, who decided it, and the note.

The person who decides sees who asked, and the person who asked sees who decided. Where a name is not yours to see, it shows a dash.

A12.6When you asked: reading the answer

The person who asked finds the answer on the request itself. For a purchase: open Purchasing and click the request.

A request still waiting says Waiting for the general manager. A cash expense shows its answer on its drawer's line (A12.7).

The Inventory Manager may decide equipment requests, but their menu is short and leaves Approvals out. It is still theirs: press Ctrl K or click Find a screen, type approv, and press Enter.

A12.7Cash expenses

A cash expense is hotel money paid out of a drawer for something the hotel needs: milk for the kitchen, a taxi, a courier, a repair. Every one waits for a yes.

To record one

  1. At the front desk, open Cashiering; in a restaurant, open Close restaurant day and its Cash step (A8.9).
  2. Under In and out of the drawer, tap Record.
  3. Choose What happened: Paid out at the desk, Expense in a restaurant.
  4. Type How much and What was it for, then tap Record.

The expense goes to Approvals as a Cash expense, named by its drawer and your words: “Fidato Restaurant tin: Milk for the kitchen”, or “Front desk drawer: …”. The GM or the owner decides it (A12.3). Not the person who paid it out.

While it is…The drawer's line saysAnd the drawer
Waiting“Waiting for a yes - not taken off the drawer yet” (in a restaurant, “… the tin yet”)Is expected to hold that money still. At the count, Paid out, waiting for a yes shows how much; if the count is short, the yellow box says how much of it is paid out and waiting.
ApprovedNothing more: it is a paid-out like any other.The money is taken off what the drawer should hold, even if the drawer has been closed since.
Refused“Not approved: the note - not taken off the drawer”The money is a shortage on that drawer, and nothing more.

Watch out

  • Record an expense as soon as the money leaves the drawer, with plain words. What was it for is the line somebody asks about tomorrow.
  • A waiting expense does not stop the drawer closing. The part of a shortage it explains needs no reason at the close; only the rest does.

Why it works this way

Every cash expense needs a yes, from the GM or the owner, never an administrator, never one's own; while waiting it is not counted and explains its part of a difference; both the desk's drawer and the restaurant tins; a refused expense is a shortage only: D27-242, following D27-239.

A12.8The bell

The bell at the top of every screen shows how many notices you have not read (9+ when there are more than nine). Click it: the list opens on the right.

  • Click a notice to go to its screen (Arrivals, Folios, the Store…). It is then marked read.
  • A notice goes by itself when the thing is done: check the guest in and the arrival notice is gone. Nothing needs clearing by hand.
  • When every notice is read, the bell's list says “You are all caught up”.

A12.9The Notifications screen

Notifications, in the System part of the menu, is the same list on a page, each notice with Open. Its first line says why the list is always right: “Derived, never stored - so nothing here can go stale while the thing it describes changes.”

KindNotices of this kind
reservationArrivals not yet checked in; table bookings guests asked for, waiting for an answer.
paymentA balance outstanding on a room due out today; money to refund to a guest who paid more than their charges.
customerGuest messages and guest requests waiting; room-service orders waiting; wake-up calls due, or asked for by guests.
approvalSomething for a manager to look at: a store's crockery and cutlery not counted for 30 days, or never; postings reversed today. It is not a request in Approvals.
systemRooms out of service. For the group owner: a hotel's bank details set, changed or removed.

Watch out

  • You see what your role may see. A guest's name only if you may read guests; an amount only if you may read bills. The Inventory Manager in Figure A12-09 reads “A balance is outstanding on room 204” where the owner reads “₹5,715 outstanding on room 204” and the guest's name.
  • Mark all as read lasts until the page is reloaded. It is kept in that browser window only; reload the page, or open the system on another computer, and the notices still standing are unread again.
  • The time on a notice is not when it arose. Most say nine in the morning of the hotel's business day.
  • Requests waiting in Approvals are not on the bell (A12.3).

Why it works this way

Each notice is worked out afresh from the same records every other screen reads, so the bell never disagrees with the folio or the report, and never has to be tidied. The bell follows the screens' own rules: names only for roles that read guests, amounts only for roles that read bills, the alert for everyone: D27-153.
