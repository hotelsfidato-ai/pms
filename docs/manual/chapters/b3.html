Chapter B3

Money rules

For the owner. The rules every rupee follows: no price the hotel did not type, the business date, the taxes, the documents and their numbers, why an issued invoice never changes, rounding, discounts, and cash. What each rule protects, and what was deliberately left for later.

B3.1No default prices: a dash, never a zero

The system ships with no prices at all: no room rate, no dish, no add-on, no hall, no laundry item, and no tax rate. Every figure is typed by somebody at the hotel. Where nothing has been typed, every screen shows a dash (-), never ₹0 D17-16 D17-49.

  • A zero must be said. A price of 0 is refused unless somebody ticks Free; a blank is "not priced yet" D27-120 D27-192. A booking at ₹0 must be ticked complimentary, by the owner, an Admin or the GM D27-59.
  • No tax rate is invented either. A new hotel has no GST rate until somebody sets one (migration 136); asked for a rate that has not been set, the database refuses rather than charging 0% (migration 067). So a dish in a section with no tax rate cannot be sold, and the till says why.
  • Unpriced means not sold. A night or a meal plan with no price is not sold, and is sent to the booking sites as closed D17-81 D27-194. A new hotel starts with no room types at all D27-263.
  • Never a figure the database has not agreed to (HANDOVER.md § 5): the screens show what the server worked out, not a sum of their own.

Why it works this way

A zero is a claim that something is free; a dash is an honest "nobody has set this". A missing tax rate that quietly became 0% would not look like a fault, it would look like a bill (the comment in migration 067). The system once shipped prices nobody at the hotel had chosen, from its templates; they were taken out (migration 032; MEMORY 63, 65), and a "Rack rate ₹0" for an unpriced room type was found and removed later (MEMORY 246). The owner's rule since: prices are the hotel's, never the system's.

B3.2The business date

Every line on every bill, every payment and every invoice carries the hotel's business date: the day the hotel is still trading in, which ends when the night audit closes it, not at midnight (A1.1).

  • Everything before the night audit belongs to that business date: a check-in, a charge, a payment, even at 5 a.m. the next morning D27-200. A guest who arrives after the audit is on the next date.
  • A person runs the night audit; it is not scheduled. At 5 Elements the GM runs it every night D21-77. A hotel that forgets stays on yesterday, which is safe and visible: every screen shows one warning line while the date is behind D27-237 (4.2). Pressing it twice changes nothing D17-12.
  • Each hotel has its own date, and two hotels are often on different days.
  • A booking's reference takes the business date's month, not the clock's D27-123.
  • A time belongs to its hotel. The server runs on GMT; a time a person types is stored and read in the hotel's own zone (migration 113; MEMORY 297).
  • Dates print as 25/08/2026 on screens, PDFs and spreadsheets D17-77.

Why it works this way

A bill settled at 00:40, before the night audit, filed under the clock's date would land on a day the hotel has not reached; the night audit would close the day without it, and the day's cash would never balance (MEMORY 19). With one date for all money, the night audit, the Manager report and the bank deposit always agree.

  • Who sets the rates: the owner, an Admin, the GM and the accountant D22-32, in Settings or on the accountant's Invoices screen D23-1.
  • A rate change has a start date: a rule applies by the charge's business date, so a GST change on a date in the future is typed ahead D27-258.
  • The tax on a line is fixed when the line is made. A rate changed today never reaches a bill already printed (migration 067). One line carries one tax rate (migration 015).
  • Always CGST and SGST, never IGST: a hotel's supply happens at the hotel, whoever the guest is and wherever they come from (migration 025).

  • The FSSAI licence number is a per-hotel setting printed on every restaurant bill and on a tax invoice carrying food, and frozen on the invoice when it is issued D26-11 D27-3.

Said differently in the documents

A room at exactly ₹7,500 a night: the decision list and the go-live plan say "5% below ₹7,500, 18% from ₹7,500" D21-65; the tax-rules migration (067), its tests and the "includes GST" split (D27-195) treat ₹7,500 itself as 5% and ₹7,500.01 as 18%. The system charges what the hotel's rule says, so the accountant's confirmation of the rule settles it.

Why an issued invoice never changes

An invoice is a legal document the guest and the tax office both hold. The database itself refuses to change or delete one, whoever asks, even its most powerful account (migration 025: a trigger, because a permission could be bypassed). A wrong invoice is answered by a credit note with its own number, so both stay on record, exactly as a wrong charge on a bill is answered by a reversal line and never rubbed out. The invoice also states the tax that was charged; it never works it out again from today's rules (MEMORY 31). The place of supply, the hotel's state and its GSTIN are stored on each invoice, so a later correction to the hotel's details never alters an invoice already issued.

Not answered in the documents

A diner who is not staying at the hotel gets the restaurant bill, which says it is not a tax invoice, and has no room bill for a GST invoice to be issued against. The documents do not say how such a guest gets a numbered GST invoice if they ask for one. Worth putting to the CA.

B3.6Rounding

  • Money is kept to the paisa, and the tax on each line is worked out on that line. Room bills, interim bills and invoices are exact, with no round-off D27-126 D27-260.
  • The restaurant: a bill paid wholly in cash is always rounded to the rupee, with a "Round off" line, so the drawer never expects paise nobody took D27-129. Otherwise each hotel chooses its restaurant round-off: Off, Manual (the Till's button) or Automatic D27-260. A restaurant bill charged to a room or to a function stays exact.
  • A room type priced as a percentage of the base is rounded to the whole rupee D27-194.
  • A rate typed "Includes GST" is split into the price before tax and the GST, so the bill and the voucher add up exactly to the typed figure. A figure that cannot split exactly to the paisa splits to the nearest total not above it, and the line says so; a typed rate the bands cannot split (₹7,875.01 to ₹8,850) is refused, in words D27-187 D27-195 D27-199. A company's contract rate is always before GST D27-233.

B3.7Discounts, promotions and complimentary

  • One rule for rooms and the restaurant: up to 10% by hand, always with a reason; more only by a named promotion D27-36. On a room it is rupees off each night, or a percentage D27-259. A typed room rate is not a discount and stays as it is D27-62.
  • Promotions start empty; the hotel names its own D27-57.
  • Complimentary: ticked by the owner, an Admin or the GM only D27-59. A complimentary room counts as sold on every report, as eZee counted it D17-72.
  • Loyalty points, where the hotel has loyalty on, come off the member's own room charges as a discount line; the earn rate is a setting with no default D27-179.

B3.8Cash

  • Every rupee belongs to a drawer D27-118. A receptionist's drawer opens by itself with their first cash after the night audit; the restaurant's opens on the float the last close kept, and only its very first day counts a float once D27-200 D27-201.
  • Every close is counted: expected against counted, a sentence for any difference, the float kept typed (above ₹0, with the next person's name), the rest to deposit. "What comes is what goes" D27-202.
  • Deposits: the GM records each day's bank deposit with the slip's photo; the owner or the accountant verifies it; a difference stays red until explained D27-190 D27-191.
  • Cash paid out (an expense from the drawer) needs a yes from the GM or the owner, never one's own; until then it is not taken off what the drawer should hold D27-242.
  • The legal limits: ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one guest for one stay or event is refused, counting the whole stay, a group as one event, and the guest's cash that day; above ₹1 lakh at one time the desk records the PAN or ticks No PAN: Form 97 taken; a foreign guest is not asked D25-40 D26-8 D26-9 D26-10.
  • Company payments are never cash: bank transfer, cheque, UPI or card D27-242.
  • More than the bill is taken only with the tick Hold it as the guest's credit, and is refunded at check-out; no money is held for a guest who has left D27-4 D27-11 D27-12.

Why it works this way

A drawer that opens by itself means nobody types a float every morning and nobody can take cash outside a shift. A difference never blocks a close, but it must have a sentence, because a manager needs the sentence, not the number. The cash limits are the law's (the Income-tax Act 2025, s.186, and Rule 159 for the PAN), applied the way the owner chose: by stay, group and day, so one large payment cannot be split to slip under them.

Left out, and why

  • GST on advances: an advance is a payment on the booking, with no receipt voucher and no tax on it, until the CA answers (docs/SELLING-IN-INDIA.md C2) D27-138.
  • Online payments: guests pay at the desk D25-36.
  • Other currencies: every figure is in rupees. A booking-site booking not in rupees waits for a person and is never converted D27-183.
  • Electronic invoicing (IRN): needed only above ₹5 crore turnover; the group is under it.
  • The accountant's ledger names for the Tally files: from the accountant; the day-book CSV until then D21-46.
