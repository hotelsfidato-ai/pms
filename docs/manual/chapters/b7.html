Chapter B7

Edge cases

For the owner. What the system does when the day does not go to plan: a full house, a guest who changes rooms, a group with one payer, a night audit with loose ends, no connection, a printer that is off, a message that fails, a hotel with nothing set up, cash over the legal limit, two people pressing at once, a day not closed. Read from the code and your decisions; the words in quotes are the system's own.

B7.1Overbooking

Two different questions: can a room type be sold past what is free, and can one room hold two guests at once.

Who booksWhen the room type is full
The desk, with "The desk may overbook" on (the default)The booking is made. New booking warns on the line, for example “2 left · this books more than that”.
The desk, with the switch offEvery room type and night is checked before anything is written: “Deluxe has no room free for every night from … to ….” A group is booked whole or not at all.
A booking site, through the channel managerAlways refused, whatever the switch says. No booking is made, the channel manager is told there are 0 rooms left on those dates at once, and Channel bookings lists it as “Refused: no room free” for the GM to sort out with the site and the guest.
The CRSRefused the same way: “Hotel full - not booked” on CRS bookings, and the salesperson is told.

One room, two guests: never. Two clerks giving the same room for overlapping nights at the same moment cannot both succeed: the database itself lets exactly one through, and a test races two of them to prove it. A guest still in a room blocks anyone else going in (B7.2).

Why it works this way

Deliberate overbooking is something every hotel does, and the desk decides; a machine has no judgement, so a booking site or the CRS is never allowed to do it (D17-43, D17-80, D27-35). A hotel that wants the desk held to the same rule switches it off (D27-243).

Watch out

  • New booking shows the same warning whether the switch is on or off; with it off, the refusal comes when the booking is saved.
  • Only New booking reads the switch. Whether changing a booking's dates also obeys it was not confirmed while writing this book.

B7.2A room move in the middle of a stay

  • Into a room somebody is still in: refused for a guest moving tonight or earlier: “… is still in room 204. Check them out first, or choose another room.” A move booked for a later night is allowed, and checked again on the night (D23-12, D24-15, D24-26).
  • Into a room booked for part of the time: “Room 204 is taken for part of that time. Pick another room, or move them on a different night.” Into a room out of service: “Room 204 is out of service.”
  • The old room is sent for cleaning, when the hotel's switch "A room a guest moved out of is sent for cleaning" is on (on by default, D27-260). A move booked for a later night dirties the old room at that night's audit (D24-15).
  • The money does not move. The bill belongs to the booking, not the room: nights already charged stay as they are, each line naming the room it was for. The dialog says “Moving rooms changes no charge on its own.”
  • A new rate on the arrival day: tonight's room line, already posted at check-in, is reversed and posted again at the new rate, with the reason "Room move" (D27-205). Later nights use the new rate.
  • Moving a guest is refused offline (A0.8).

Why it works this way

Until 23 September a mid-stay move left the old room marked as it was (FD-9), and check-in could give a room a late check-out guest was still in (FD-11). Both were fixed at your request (D23-12), and three close cousins the next day (D24-15), with the line drawn at "only when they go in now" (D24-26).

B7.3Groups, and a bill the host pays

  • A master bill. A group can have one open master bill, "Host pays". Each room is then ticked to send its room nights to the host, or to pay its own: “Nights already charged stay where they were posted.” Extras and late fees stay on the guest's own bill.
  • A guest's own figures count only the guest's own bill: the Bill tab, the Check out button, the In house list and Settle never ask a guest for nights the host pays. Folios lists the host's nights once, under Group bills, never under each guest (D27-226).
  • Check-out is per room. There is no group check-out: each room leaves against its own bill, and the master bill never holds a guest back.
  • One voucher per group, to the lead guest: the room booked first that still stands, unless the desk picks another; a changed lead says “The group's confirmation and its review request go to them.” (D22-41, D22-52).
  • A company's credit limit counts what the company owes across the whole group of hotels (D27-279).
  • Settling the master bill is done on the group's own page (Group bills on Folios opens it). The card The host's account says who it is billed to, the group's company, else the guest who leads the group, and carries the Bill tab's own Settle: any of the hotel's ways of paying, or Bill to company, which moves it to that company's City ledger. That is refused when the group is billed to no company, and over the company's credit limit it needs the reason of the owner, an Admin or the GM. Issue tax invoice comes only when every room has left and nothing is owed, after the same question as any invoice before a number is taken; it is made out to the company or else the lead guest, and nothing sends it by itself. The ₹2 lakh cash count takes the host's account together with the rooms' own bills (D27-305, D27-307; A2.6).

Watch out: found while writing this book

  • If the lead room is still a waiting booking (from the CRS), the whole group's voucher waits too (RES-6).

B7.4The night audit with loose ends

The audit refuses before it writes anything, and says all its reasons in one go:

Loose endWhat the audit says
Guests still showing due out“3 rooms are still showing due out. Check them out, or extend the stay - the audit will not decide which.”
No GST rule for rooms“This property has no GST rule for rooms, so a room charge has no rate to carry. … A missing rate is not zero - the audit will not invent one.”
A desk drawer not counted“A front-desk drawer is still open. Count the cash and close the shift - the audit cannot say what was taken at the desk until somebody has.”
The restaurant's day not closed“The till has not been closed for today. Run the day end on it first - the restaurant's takings belong in this audit, and they are not final until the drawer is counted.” Only for an outlet that had orders that day.

Open restaurant bills are stopped one step earlier: Close restaurant day refuses while a bill is open (“There is 1 bill still open.”, settle it or close it unpaid with a reason), and while a paid table was never cleared. So the audit cannot run over an open restaurant bill. A bill charged to a room while offline also keeps the day open until it reaches the server.

  • Pressed twice, at the same moment or later: the second press changes nothing and shows the record already made (D17-12). A unique record per day, and a lock, guarantee it. An audit stopped half-way can simply be run again: each night is checked for an existing charge first.
  • A night is never charged twice. The first night is posted at check-in, and the audit skips any night already covered by a room line (D27-204, D24-14). Earlier double charges were a moved guest charged twice a night, found only by closing real nights (MEMORY 171), and a split-bill stay charged twice (D27-89); both are fixed.
  • No-shows: a confirmed arrival that never came is marked no-show. A retention charge is posted only when the booking carries cancellation terms.
  • A hotel days behind closes one day per press, starting from its business date; there is no catch-up button. The first run after a gap marks every arrival up to that day that never came as a no-show (B7.13).

Correcting a day already closed

The owner, an admin or the GM, on Closed days, for this month and last, with a reason. A room, no-show or other charge is put right or taken off; the closed day's reports show the corrected figure, and the old one stays on the bill and on the correction list (D24-1 to D24-6, D25-5). Refused: a bill with a tax invoice (“Issue a credit note instead.”), drinks with an issued VAT bill, and money: payments, refunds and discounts are never changed on a closed day: “put the difference right today.” (D24-4, D25-7, D25-11).

Watch out

  • The checklist on the Night audit screen shows due-out rooms as a yellow note, yet they stop the run; and the screen does not list which rooms, drawers or outlets are in the way. Read the refusal sentence.

B7.5No connection

The rule everywhere: what can be safely held on the device is held and sent later; what cannot is refused with a sentence saying what to do instead (A0.8). In the restaurant:

ActionOffline
Building a round on the padWorks. The round is kept on the device until Send.
SendHeld: “The kitchen does not have it yet. It goes as soon as the connection is back.” It has no KOT number until it arrives. Tell the kitchen.
Cash, card or UPIRefused: “… take cash on paper and enter it when the connection is back.”
Charge to roomHeld, and sent when the line is back. The restaurant's day cannot close while it waits, so it cannot be lost.
Customise, Print bill, Close restaurant dayRefused. An add-on held offline could reach a dish after the kitchen has it.

Kept on the device for a week, so they open with no connection: properties and rooms, Arrivals, In house, Due out, Rooms and the Calendar (D22-23), Tables, Housekeeping, Work orders, the banquet diary, the menu and add-ons, and the rooms a bill can be charged to. Balances shown offline say how old they are (D27-271). Not kept: guests' documents, bills and reports, the Kitchen screen, the Till's orders.

Why it works this way

A waiter at a table should never wait on the internet, so the pad works offline until Send. Money is different: a cash payment recorded offline on two devices could be counted twice or not at all, so it is refused and written on paper; a room charge is safe to hold because the day cannot close without it (your choice of 8 September, recorded in MEMORY 199 and 200, with its risk written down).

Watch out: the risk you accepted

  • A guest can check out while a room charge is still held on a waiter's device. When it arrives, the server refuses it, because nobody is checked in there any more, and the device keeps the refusal on screen for a person. The money is not lost silently; somebody must collect it (MEMORY 200).

B7.6A KOT computer or the bill printer is off

  • Online means the printing computer was seen within the last 90 seconds, with a restaurant screen open on it (D27-161).
  • Its KOTs wait, and print when it is back, oldest first, for the current business day only. Older ones are reprinted from This computer's last 20 KOTs; the slip says REPRINT (D27-167, D27-292).
  • The red line on Tables, the Till, the Terminal and the Kitchen names it: “Bar KOTs wait: Bar PC not seen since 8:42”, or “No computer prints Bar KOTs - they are not printing” when no computer has that kind. It shows only for a kind some menu section prints to.
  • The bill printer off: “Bar PC is offline”, the bill waiting to print when it is back, with Print here to print it on the device instead. A bill printed here is taken off the printer's list, so it never prints twice.

Why it works this way

The website cannot reach a printer by its address on the hotel's network, so a printer is always reached through the computer it is connected to (D27-292). There is no fallback printer: with none, nobody prints and every restaurant screen says so in red (D27-161). Only the current business day's missed KOTs print by themselves; older ones are reprinted by hand (D27-167).

B7.7A restaurant bill closed unpaid

  • Who: anyone who may use the till, while nothing on the bill has gone to the kitchen. Once any dish was sent, only the owner, an admin, the GM, the hotel manager or the kitchen manager (D27-128, D27-130).
  • Always with a reason: “Close this bill unpaid?”, “It stays on the record with this reason - a walkout and a duplicate are different things at the end of a shift.” The bill then reads "Closed unpaid" with the reason.
  • The table turns grey, Closed unpaid - clear it, with no amount, and takes no more rounds until Clear frees it (D27-288).
  • Close restaurant day shows it as Walked out, with the amount. It is not in the night audit's restaurant takings, which count settled bills only.

Watch out

  • A table left "Closed unpaid" without Clear does not stop Close restaurant day (only paid tables do). Clear it anyway, so the next guests see a free table.

B7.8Messages to guests: failures, repeats and quiet hours

  • Retries. A sweep runs every minute. A message that fails is tried again, up to three tries, then left; each try is recorded as sent or failed, with the reason in words, such as “The receiver did not answer within twenty seconds.”
  • Never sent twice. Each message has one identity on every try, and the n8n workflow drops an identity it has already sent; a lock per booking and kind stops two sweeps sending at once (“It was sent already, so it is not sent again.”). The desk's Send voucher refuses a voucher that has gone already, or that failed three times.
  • Quiet hours. Catching up on older bookings happens only between 09:00 and 21:00, hotel time; a new booking is confirmed at once, at any hour (D22-42).
  • The switch-on day. Every confirmed future booking is owed its voucher, sent soonest arrival first, at the pace you set for the WhatsApp account; nobody guesses the pace (D21-33, D21-34).
  • Not set up: nothing is sent and nothing is recorded; the screens say “Sending is not set up yet.”
  • Groups: one voucher, to the lead guest (B7.3).
  • Two sends are new every time, by design: the desk's Send check-in link (each press is a new message), and a review request asked by hand, which is not refused a second time.

Watch out

  • After the third failed try nobody is told; the Guest messages screen shows it as failed. Look there when a guest says nothing arrived.
  • Read from the code while writing this book, not tried: the switch-on moment is recorded once for the whole system, so a hotel added later, or one that switches its vouchers off and on again, may send its backlog outside the quiet hours. Reported for checking.

B7.9A hotel with nothing set up

MissingWhat happens
No menuThe Till: “There is no menu yet”, “Nothing can be sold until the hotel enters what it sells. The prices are yours - this system ships none.” The Tables pad says the same; the Terminal, “Nothing on the menu”.
No tables“No tables have been laid out yet.” The owner, an admin or the GM get a button to set them up (D25-25).
No price for a nightNew booking shows the charges, GST and Due as a dash and says “No price for …. Set it on Rates, or type a rate on the line.” Saving without a typed rate is refused. A date with no price is sent to the channel manager as closed, so no booking site sells it (D17-81, D27-194).
No rooms“No rooms set up yet”, “Add them in Room types.” (D17-23). A hotel with no rooms reports nothing, because occupancy divides by rooms.
No GST rule for roomsThe guest is still checked in, but no room charge is posted, and the night audit refuses until the rule is set (D27-205, B7.4).
No banquet tax headingA function is warned when it is set up, and its bill is refused until the heading is set (D27-124).

Why it works this way

No default prices, anywhere: an unpriced thing shows a dash, never a zero, and nothing is ever charged at a figure nobody typed (HANDOVER § 5). A zero is allowed only when somebody ticks Free (D27-120). A night with no price is sent to the channel manager as closed, without anybody pressing anything, because the channel manager cannot take a price back once it has one (D17-81).

Watch out

  • "Add them in Room types" has no link, and Room types now lives in Settings, under Rooms.

B7.10Cash over the limit, and a payment larger than the bill

  • ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one guest is refused (Income-tax Act s.186): “Cash of ₹2,00,000 or more from one guest is not allowed (Income-tax Act s.186).”, then how much more may be cash, and the other ways to take the rest. At the desk the cash is counted across the stay, its group, and everything this guest paid in cash today on any stay; refunds do not bring it down (D25-40, D26-8). At the till, this bill's cash (D26-10).
  • More than ₹1 lakh in cash at one time needs the guest's PAN, or Form 97 ticked; a guest from abroad is not asked (D26-9, D26-10).
  • At the desk, a payment larger than the bill is held until the clerk ticks Hold it as the guest's credit: “This is ₹500.00 more than the bill; it will be held as the guest's credit.” It is refunded at check-out; no money is held for guests who have left (D27-4, D27-11, D27-12).
  • At the till the payments must equal the bill; extra cash is change, shown as Change due.
  • Cash with no drawer: the desk's drawer opens by itself the first time a receptionist takes cash after the audit; the restaurant refuses cash only before its very first float is counted (D27-200, D27-201).

B7.12Arrivals out of the ordinary

  • Checking in days early is allowed, but warned and confirmed, naming the new nights and amount; the room hold moves with it (D27-193, D27-205).
  • Checking in and out the same day: the guest owes that night; check-out is refused until it is paid, or a manager reverses it with a reason (D27-205).
  • A day use is charged its typed day price with GST at check-in, never by the night audit, and does not take the room out of that night's sale. Change dates will not turn a day use into nights or the reverse: “A day use stays a day use: its price is the day's. To stay the night, book the nights.” (D27-227, D27-229, D27-230).
  • An advance before check-in, where the hotel asks one, is never asked of a company's booking, a room the host pays for, a complimentary room, or a booking prepaid on a booking site; there is no override, and the refusal names the amount (D27-243, D27-246).
  • A no-show who did arrive: only the retention charge is taken off on Closed days; putting the booking back is the desk's, separately (D24-7).

B7.13A business date behind the real day

  • Every screen says so in the top bar: “Business date 11 Sep, 21 days behind today (2 Oct). Close the days in Night audit.” (D27-237). All properties shows the same under "Needs you".
  • System health: "Up to date", amber "Yesterday not closed yet", red "Behind by 2 days" and more (D27-113).
  • Everything is still recorded, on the hotel's business date, never the clock's: a check-in at 5 a.m. before the audit belongs to the day not yet closed (D27-200). The only refusal is a booking arriving before the business date.
  • Other devices read the date again whenever a screen opens, so none keeps yesterday's date after the audit (D27-89).
  • Catching up means closing the days one by one, each with its checks (B7.4).

Why it works this way

A hotel's day ends when somebody closes it, not at midnight: a 00:40 receipt once landed on the wrong day because the clock was trusted (MEMORY 19). A day not closed is safe, because nothing is lost and nothing is charged twice, and it is loud, because every screen says so.
