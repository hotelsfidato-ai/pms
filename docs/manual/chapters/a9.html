Chapter A9

Stores and purchasing

For the Inventory Manager, the kitchen manager and the managers who approve. How the stores and suppliers are set up, how a thing is added to the Store, received, issued, moved and counted, how something is asked for, approved, ordered and delivered, and how equipment is registered and taken out of the store.

A9.2Places and suppliers

Before anything can be received, the property needs its stores; before anything can be ordered, a supplier. Both are set up once, here.

To add a place

  1. Tap Add a place.
  2. Type its Name (“Main store”) and choose its Kind: Store - stock is kept here, Kitchen, Bar, Restaurant, Public area, Office or Other.
  3. Tap Add.

Rooms are already known; they are not added here. Tap a place to rename it, or to Switch off one no longer used.

To add a supplier

  1. Tap Add a supplier. “Only the name is needed now.”
  2. Type the Name. Add the Person to call, Phone, Email, GSTIN, Address and a Note if you have them.
  3. Tap Add.

Watch out

  • Adding and changing places and suppliers is for the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and the Inventory Manager. Everybody else reads them.
  • Two suppliers, or two places, cannot have the same name: “There is already a supplier called … here.”
  • Nothing is deleted. Switch it off: stock was kept there, and deliveries came from them.

Why it works this way

Who may add places and suppliers: D17-1. The Store, Equipment and Purchasing all read the same places and suppliers, so a supplier is typed once.

A9.3Adding something to the Store

An item is a thing the property keeps and counts. It has no price: a delivery has a price (A9.4).

  1. On the Store, tap Add an item.
  2. Type What is it (“Basmati rice”) and what it is Counted in: kg, litre, piece, cylinder.
  3. Choose Where it belongs: Kitchen, Bar, Housekeeping, Maintenance or Everything else. Nothing is chosen for you.
  4. For cups, plates and cutlery, tick Crockery or cutlery - in use at an outlet until it breaks (A9.8).
  5. If you want a warning when it runs low, type Warn me below, in the Main store. Leave it blank for no warning.
  6. Tap Add. The message says “Receiving a delivery is what gives it a quantity and a value.”

Watch out

  • A name already in the Store is not added twice. Adding it again changes that item: its unit, where it belongs and its Main store level. Only those who may correct counts can do that.
  • Zero is not “no warning”. Zero means warn me when it has run out, which is too late.
  • The kitchen manager adds items without a level. Levels are set by those who correct counts (A9.7).
  • Import adds a list of items from a file.

A9.4Receiving a delivery

A delivery is where the quantity and the cost come from. Record it when the goods arrive, with the bill in your hand.

  1. On the item, tap Receive.
  2. Choose Which store it went into, and type How many.
  3. Type What it cost, per kg, before GST. If it was free, tick Free - it cost nothing. If the bill has not come, leave the cost blank.
  4. From the bill, choose the GST rate, %. The GST amount is worked out for you; change it to what the bill says. Say whether Can this GST be claimed? is Yes or No.
  5. Add the Use by date if it has one, Who from, and the Delivery note or bill number.
  6. Tap Receive.

Watch out

  • A cost cannot be added later. A delivery received with no cost can only be priced by undoing it (A9.7) and receiving it again. A blank is better than a guess.
  • The GST is all three or none: the rate, the amount and claimable. With only one or two, Receive stays grey.
  • Only a store that is switched on takes a delivery.
  • An item held with no cost at all is counted on the Not valued tile (A9.5).

Why it works this way

An item has no price because the same rice costs differently in June and October; what the shelf is worth is the running average of what was actually paid, and a blank cost is a dash, never a zero. The cost before GST, then the bill's rate, amount and claimable: D27-238, D27-242.

A9.6Issuing

Stock given out of a store, to the kitchen, the bar or a floor, is an issue. It needs nobody's approval.

  1. On the item, tap Issue.
  2. Check Which store it comes from. Each store shows what it holds.
  3. Type How many.
  4. Choose Which outlet it went to, or Not an outlet for housekeeping or a floor.
  5. Type the Department if you use one (“Kitchen”, “Banquets”), and Why: where it went.
  6. Tap Issue.

Watch out

  • The outlet is chosen every time. Issue stays grey until you choose one, or Not an outlet.
  • An issue cannot take more than that store holds. With nothing on hand, Issue is grey: “Nothing on hand to issue - receive a delivery first.”
  • The Store needs the connection. With none, it says so and records nothing.

Why it works this way

Every issue says which outlet it went to: D22-20. That is what the Days view adds up per outlet (A9.10). The department is free text: D27-242. Stock going out of a store needs no approval; equipment does (A9.13): S12.

To move stock to another store

  1. In More, tap Move to another store.
  2. Choose From and To, type How many and, if you like, Why.
  3. Tap Move.

History, and undoing a mistake

Nothing in the Store is edited. A wrong entry is undone: a new row takes it back, and both rows stay on the history.

  1. In More, tap History.
  2. Find the wrong row and tap its undo arrow.
  3. It asks first, for example “Undo this issue of 10 kg?”. Tap Undo issue, or Cancel.
  4. Then record it again, correctly.

To set the levels

  1. In More, tap Set levels.
  2. For each store, type the figure at which to warn. “Warn when a store holds this many kg or fewer. Blank is no warning, not zero.”
  3. Tap Save. The card then reads “Warn at Main 20 · Kitchen 4”.

When a store is at or below its level, the item is on the Below level tile and its card says so in yellow, for example “Kitchen store: 4 kg, level 4” (Figure A9-20). It only warns: buying is done in Purchasing (A9.11).

Watch out

  • Correct the count, Set levels and Undo are for the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and the Inventory Manager. Others see who may.
  • An undo that would leave less than nothing in that store is refused: some of it has already gone out. Correct the count instead.
  • Undoing a move takes back both halves, in both stores.
  • A delivery's Use by date shows in History.

Why it works this way

On hand is added up from the movements every time, so nobody can type over it; a mistake is undone by its own row, and the store's past stays as it was. Levels per store, set by those who correct counts; the warning only shows: D25-12, D25-13, D25-14.

A9.8Gas cylinders, crockery and cutlery

Gas cylinders

An item counted in “cylinder” is kept as Full and Empty, because the supplier swaps empties for full ones and holds a deposit on each.

  • Receive also asks Empties the supplier took back.
  • Issue also asks Empties back from the kitchen.
  • Both are typed every time. 0 is an answer: “0 if none came back.”
  • In More, Set levels and deposit also holds Cylinders on deposit with the supplier and the Deposit per cylinder, as on the supplier's receipt. A dash until typed.

In use at the kitchen is the cylinders on deposit less the full and empty ones in the stores. If the stores hold more than is on deposit, the line says to check the count or the deposit.

Crockery and cutlery

An item ticked Crockery or cutlery can be moved into use at a restaurant, bar or kitchen place (set up in Places and suppliers): its More menu says Move into use, or to another store. It has a level there, and it is counted there. When it breaks, record it:

  1. In More, tap Broken.
  2. Choose Where it broke, type How many.
  3. Choose Who broke it: a person working here, or Not known. Nothing is chosen for you.
  4. Type Why (dropped, chipped, cracked in the wash) and tap Record breakage.

Watch out

  • Breakage is for crockery and cutlery only. Anything else that is lost is Waste.
  • Undoing an issue of cylinders does not undo the empties that came back with it: each is undone on its own line in History.
  • Empties stay in the store the full cylinder left from. The count corrects them (A9.9).

Why it works this way

Full and empty, empties typed each time, the deposit typed on the item: D17-4, D25-18, D25-19. Crockery in use at an outlet, breakage with who or Not known, and a reminder when a place goes 30 days uncounted: D27-238, D27-242.

A9.9Counting a store

A count compares what is really on the shelves with what the system says (the book), store by store.

  1. On the Store, tap Count a store, and choose the Store.
  2. Tap Print the sheet. The paper has each item, its unit and an empty box. It has no book figure: you write what is there.
  3. Count the shelves. Then type the counts in straight away.
  4. When you type a count, the line shows the book and the difference, such as “Book 4 kg · 1 fewer”. Where it differs, type Why, if known.
  5. Tap Save the count.

A drink with a bottle size (the bar's, A9.11) has two boxes: the full bottles, and the ml in the open one. Five full and 400 ml of a 750 ml bottle is one figure; the book shows in the same words, such as “Book 5 bottles + 630 ml · 0 bottles + 230 ml fewer”. The Store's cards show a drink the same way: “Bar 5 + 630 ml”.

  • Each difference is saved as a correction with your name and “Count of Kitchen store”, plus the reason typed on its line.
  • A line left blank is not counted. It is never taken as zero.
  • A cylinder item has a second line, “- empty”, for its empties.
  • At a place where crockery is in use, the sheet lists the crockery only.

Watch out

  • The store is not locked while you count. If an item moved in that store after the sheet opened, the save is refused and names it. That line takes the new book with its count cleared: count it again and save.
  • Count a store is for the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and the Inventory Manager.

Why it works this way

No lock, a blank sheet, the book shown only after the count is typed, whoever saves it, a blank line never zero: D25-15, D25-16, D25-17. A blank sheet makes the counter look at the shelf, not at the figure the system expects.

A9.10The Days view

Days shows fourteen business days of the Store, ending today: one row per item, one column per day.

  • A cell is what came in (green, +) over what went out, in the item's unit. Blank: nothing moved.
  • With All stores, a move between stores is left out: it is not in or out of the property.
  • The footer's rupees are at the running average. Wasted ₹ is waste only, not breakage.
  • Tap an item's cell in today's column to Receive or Issue it.
  • The tiles and the Store picker work as on the Shelf.

By Outlet, each outlet's cell shows what was issued to it; for those who may read reports, also what the outlet sold and the food cost %, both before GST. For anybody else those two are a dash: “Issued to each outlet. What it sold is for those who read reports.”

Why it works this way

The tiles, the fourteen days, Receive and Issue from today, the outlet view: D27-238. Sales only for those who read reports, and the food cost % as costs before GST over sales before GST: D27-242.

A9.11Buying something: Purchasing

Purchasing has two tabs: Restaurant and hotel, described first, and Bar, for the bar's bills (below). On Restaurant and hotel, a purchase goes through five stages, each shown on the order as its state:

StateWhat happens next
Waiting for approvalThe general manager approves, refuses or sends it back.
Ready to orderApproved. Every line needs an agreed price, then Send the order.
OrderedSent to the supplier. When the goods come, Record the delivery.
DeliveredReceived into the store. Done.
Refused, Sent back, CancelledIt stops here, on the record. To go ahead, raise a new request.

To ask to buy something

  1. Open Purchasing and tap Ask to buy.
  2. Choose Which store is it for: where the goods will go.
  3. Type What is it for. The general manager reads this before approving.
  4. Under What is needed, pick the item and type how much. Type the price only if one has been agreed; tick Free if it costs nothing. Add another item for more lines.
  5. Tap Send for approval. The message says it is with the general manager to approve.

The general manager decides

  1. Open the order (from Purchasing, or the Approvals inbox, A12).
  2. Read what it is for, the lines and the Total.
  3. Tap Approve this purchase. Or Refuse, or Send back for something to be cleared up: both ask you to say why, for the person who asked.

Sending the order

  1. Open the approved order. It says Ready to order.
  2. If a line has no agreed price, type it on the line. Send the order appears only when every line has one.
  3. Tap Send the order, choose Order it from, and tap Send the order.
  4. Tap Download the order: a PDF. Print it, or attach it to a message to the supplier. Nothing is sent automatically.

When the goods come

  1. Open the order. It says Ordered. Tap Record the delivery.
  2. For each line, type what Came and what it cost each, before GST. Then the bill's GST %, the GST amount, Claimable, and the Use by date if it has one.
  3. Type Anything to note: a short delivery, a damaged case, a different brand.
  4. If the goods came on credit - the supplier is paid later - tick On credit - the supplier's bill is paid later and type the supplier's Bill number, the Bill date, the Amount, as printed and the date it is Due on (filled with the bill date + 30 days; change it to what the bill says). The bill goes on Supplier dues (below).
  5. Tap Record the delivery. The goods land in the order's store, as a delivery in the Store (A9.4).

The delivered order then says, under the note, “On credit: bill … due …” and whether it is paid yet.

Watch out

  • No price is invented. A line with no agreed price reads “-”, never 0, and the order cannot be sent to a supplier until every line has one. An order with an unpriced line has no total.
  • Only items already in the Store can be asked for. Add the item first (A9.3).
  • The price can be typed until the order is sent; after that it is fixed.
  • One delivery per order. If less came, type what came: both the order and what came stay on the record.
  • A gas cylinder's delivery here does not ask for empties. Correct them on the Store's count.
  • Cancel works until it is delivered, and asks why.
  • A bill on credit needs all four: its number, its date (not in the future), its amount (more than zero, as printed) and its due date (on its own date or later). A missing one is refused and nothing is received.
  • The Inventory Manager records the supplier's bill on the delivery, but does not see Supplier dues or pay it.

The bar's bills

A bill for the bar is recorded as printed, with no approval: the bill has already come. Its bottles go into the Bar store at once, so the bar's stock is right from the moment the bill is saved. The owner, an Admin, the GM, the Inventory Manager and the bar manager (the Bar role) record them; whoever sees Purchasing or Bar stock can read them.

  1. Open Bar stock in the Restaurant menu, tap Bills, then Record a bar bill. (Or Purchasing, Bar.)
  2. Pick the Supplier from the hotel's list (a new one is added first in Places and suppliers, so one supplier never becomes two by spelling), type the Bill number as printed, the Bill date and the T.P. number (the transport permit, if the bill has one).
  3. Photo of the bill: take it with the phone's camera, or pick a file.
  4. For each line of the bill, pick the drink from the bar's list. A drink the list does not have yet: Add another drink…, then its brand as on the bill, its size in ml (a NIP is 180) and what it is counted in (bottle, can, packet, piece). It joins the bar's list.
  5. Type the line as printed: Bottles a case (48 NIPs, 12 beers of 650 ml; filled in from the drink's last bill), the Cases, any Loose bottles, the MRP and the Rate a case. The Amount works itself out: the rate for each case, and a case's share of it for each loose bottle. Add a drink for the next line.
  6. Type the Handling charges, the TCS and the Total, as printed. The line under them says what the lines, handling and TCS make, and “matches the bill”, or how much more or less it is.
  7. If the bill is on credit, tick On credit - the bill is paid later and check the date it is Due on (filled with the bill date + 30 days). Its number, date and total go on Supplier dues (below).
  8. Tap Save the bill.

The bill shows under Bills as printed: the supplier, its number, date and T.P. number, its total; each line's drink and MRP, cases and loose bottles at the rate a case, and the amount; the handling and the TCS; who recorded it. Photo of the bill opens the photo. The bottles are in the Store, in the Bar store, each at the line's amount over its bottles, each delivery's line naming the bill.

Watch out

  • A bill the lines do not make is refused, saying both figures: “The lines, handling and TCS make ₹8,000.00; the bill says ₹9,000.00. Check the cases, the rates and the charges against the bill.” Up to ₹1 apart is the bill's round-off, and saves.
  • The same bill twice is refused: “Bill A-1021 from Amit Traders is already recorded.”
  • A drink already on the Store's list is picked, not added again: “"Romanov Vodka 180 ml" is already on the store's list. Pick it instead.”
  • Every line needs its rate a case as on the bill; zero is an answer, a blank is not. A bill is not dated in the future.
  • A wrong line is undone in the Store, from the drink's History (A9.7), as any delivery is.
  • A bill on credit shows “On credit, due …” under its lines, and “not paid yet” or the day it was paid.

Supplier dues: what we owe suppliers

The Supplier dues tab of Purchasing, and Supplier dues in the Finance menu, list every bill taken on credit - from a delivery or a bar bill - with something still to pay. Each supplier has its bills (number, the order or "bar bill" it came from, bill date, due date, amount and what is Left, marked Part-paid once something is paid), its total and a link to its Statement (A11.6); the total owed to all of them is at the top. A bill past its due date is in red, with “overdue”. The Accountant, the GM, the owner and an Admin see it and use it; nobody else sees it (D27-370, D27-372).

To pay all or part of a bill:

  1. On the bill, tap Record payment.
  2. The Amount paid is filled with what is left; type less to pay a part.
  3. Pick How: Cash, Cheque, Bank transfer or UPI. For a cheque, a bank transfer or UPI the Reference (cheque, UTR or UPI number) is needed; for cash it may be left empty. Check Paid on, and type who Handed over the money or cheque.
  4. If TDS was deducted, tick TDS deducted and type its Section (194C, 194Q, 194J or 194I), Rate, % and TDS amount. The Accountant decides whether TDS applies; the system works nothing out. The supplier is paid the rest, and the payment and its TDS together come off the bill.
  5. Tap Record payment. When nothing is left, the bill leaves the list.

A credit note lowers what is owed, money only: Credit note on a bill, or beside the supplier's name for one not against a bill. Say whose note it is (the supplier's, or ours), its number, date, amount and what it is for. Goods sent back leave the store as a store movement, separately.

A mistake is never changed or deleted: Reverse a bill, a payment or a credit note and say why. Both stay on the record, with who did it. Reversing a wrong bill can carry the right one (its number, date, amount and due date), which then replaces it.

Watch out

  • A payment is never more than what is left on the bill, nor dated in the future or before the bill.
  • Nothing changes in a cash drawer or a shift: the payment is a record of what was paid.
  • A bill, payment or credit note is put right only by Reverse, with a reason.
  • What was owed on a date and the payments made are in the Supplier dues report; one supplier's ledger in the Supplier statement; TDS by section in TDS on suppliers (A11.6).

The bar's day: Bar stock

Bar stock, in the Restaurant menu after Kitchen, is the bar's one place. It has four parts: Today's sheet, Requests, Bills (above) and Set up. It is the bar manager's (the Bar role), the GM's, the owner's and an Admin's. The same parts are the Bar tab of Inventory, in Store and equipment, for the Inventory Manager; whoever sees the Store can read them.

The bar has two places, as on paper:

  • The Bar store, the godown: the supplier's bills go in here, and only sealed NIPs, bottles and packets are kept, counted whole.
  • The Bar counter: what is being sold. Every KOT sent takes from here, and the open bottle is counted in ml.

The counter asks the store for stock, and the GM or the Inventory Manager approves; approving moves it.

Bar stock is a switch for each property, in Admin, Properties (A17.14): on at every hotel open before release 14 unless the owner or an Admin turns it off; a hotel added since starts with it off until they turn it on. Off, none of this part is offered, KOTs take nothing off the bar's stock and the night audit does not wait for a bar count.

Set up: the bar's drinks

Each drink is on the bar's list once for each bottle size: Blenders Pride 180 ml and Blenders Pride 750 ml are two. The GM, the Inventory Manager, the owner and an Admin keep the list; the bar manager reads it.

  1. Open Bar stock, tap Set up, then Add a drink (or Change on one).
  2. Type its Name, and pick Counted as:
    • NIPs or bottles, poured in pegs: hard liquor and wine. Whole in the store; at the counter, the sealed ones and the open one's ml.
    • Whole bottles (beer): counted in bottles everywhere.
    • Packets, and loose ones (cigarettes): whole packets in the store; at the counter, packets and the open packet's sticks. Type How many in a packet.
  3. For liquor: its Bottle size, ml, its Kind of liquor (Whisky, Rum, Vodka, Gin, Brandy, Wine, Beer strong or mild...), the Brand, as the register names it, and Imported if it is not Indian made. These place it in the excise registers. Cigarettes and soft drinks are Not liquor, and stay out of them.
  4. Tap Save. A drink on a bar bill that is not on the list yet joins it the same way (above).

Set up: what each drink takes

Each bar dish, and each of its sizes, says what it takes from the Bar counter: a 30, 60 or 90 ml peg takes that many ml of the brand's NIP, a NIP one whole NIP, a beer one bottle. Once that is saved, every KOT sent takes it off the counter, from whichever screen sent it: the Till, the Terminal, a waiter's phone, or a guest's order accepted.

  1. Under What each drink takes from the bar, every dish in a section taxed as bar or alcohol is listed, with each size.
  2. Where the names match, a suggestion is ready: tap Use the … suggestions to fill them all, and check them.
  3. For the rest, pick the NIP or bottle each is poured from, type how much, and choose ml of it or whole.
  4. Tap Save. A dish left on Nothing set takes nothing, and the Bar stock report lists it.

How the daily sheet looks, at the top of Set up, is the owner's, an Admin's or the GM's choice: Like the paper register or Simple. It changes only how the counter's sheet is laid out.

The counter asks the store: Requests

When the counter runs low, the bar manager asks the store for whole NIPs, bottles or packets. The GM or the Inventory Manager (the owner or an Admin too) approves; approving moves them from the Bar store to the Bar counter. Nobody approves their own request.

  1. Open Bar stock, tap Requests.
  2. Under Ask the store, pick a drink (the list says what the store has) and type how many; Another drink for the next. A note if you like.
  3. Tap Ask the store. It waits, marked Waiting, with what the store holds of each.
  4. The GM or the Inventory Manager taps Approve and move, or Reject with a reason the counter reads.

  2. For each drink counted, type the full NIPs and the ml in the open one under Counted; a beer, its bottles; cigarettes, the packets and the loose sticks. A line left blank is not counted, never taken as zero.
  3. The Difference shows as you type. Tap Save the closing count; a difference is saved as a count correction against your name.
  4. Tap Bar store and count it the same way, in whole NIPs, bottles and packets.

The night audit waits for it: a day with bar drinks cannot be closed until the counter and the store are counted, each drink that holds stock or moved that day, after its last move. If the bar did not open, the GM taps The bar did not open today on the sheet, types why, and taps Mark the bar closed today; The bar opened takes it back.

In the morning, before the first KOT, the bar manager taps Morning count, counts the counter (and the store, if its key is with them) and taps Save the morning count. Nothing should have moved since the night's count: a difference shows in red in Morning, and on the day's Bar stock report for the GM.

The excise registers

The GM and the Inventory Manager print the registers the excise department asks for, laid out like the paper books, from the sheet's buttons for its day (FLR-1A/2A/3A for the day, FLR-3 for its month), or from Reports (A11.9):

  • FORM F.L.R.-1A / 2A / 3A, the day: one row per brand under its kind, with Opening Balance, Receipts, Sales and Closing Balance for each bottle size, and the T.P. No. of the day's bills.
  • FORM F.L.R.-3, the month: a row per day of Received (with the Permit No.), Sold and Closing Balance, by spirits imported and Indian made, wines, fermented and mild liquor, and each size; at the foot Opening, Received, Grand total, Less sold, Less breakages and Closing balance.

Both count whole bottles, the store and the counter together. A NIP poured as pegs counts as sold the day it is opened: the counter's open bottle is not in the closing balance. Sales are what the paper writes: opening + receipts - breakages - closing.

The day's figures are also the Bar stock report (A11.9). It can be emailed after each night audit (A17.12).

Watch out

  • A drink is taken off when its KOT is sent, not when the bill is paid, and from the counter. Struck after the bar had it, it stays off, as poured, and the report lists it.
  • A counter with nothing moved to it goes below zero as KOTs are sent: ask the store in time.
  • The store short of a drink: “The store is short: Blenders Pride 180 ml (the store has 2, 5 asked). Nothing was moved.” Reject it, and ask again for what is there.
  • The open one's ml is under its size: “Imperial Blue 180 ml: whole NIPs, and the open one's ml under 180.”
  • If a drink moved after the sheet was opened (a KOT sent), the count is refused and names it: count it again.
  • A drink with no kind of liquor set is not in the registers; one with no bottle size goes under “size not set”.

Why it works this way

The GM approves every purchase, at any amount: D18-4, D17-35. The order is a PDF sent by hand: D18-3. One Approvals inbox, with the screens keeping their own buttons: D18-19. The bar's bills, recorded directly with a photo; the bar in bottles and ml, the stock off when the KOT is sent, a daily count, the Bar stock report and its email: D27-354. The bar like the paper register: all spirits as NIPs, both sheet layouts as a setting, bills as printed, one Bar stock page in the Restaurant menu for the bar manager, the GM, the owner and an Admin: D27-356. The Rate column, the ₹1 round-off, Set up read by the bar manager: D27-357. Two places, the store and the counter; the counter asks and the GM or the Inventory Manager approves; counted three ways; the night audit waits for the closing count, the GM may mark the bar closed; the morning count; FLR-1A/2A/3A and FLR-3 like the paper, a NIP sold the day it is opened: D27-361. Bar stock as a switch for each property, fully off when off: D27-367. Other moves on the store's sheet and the Simple one, so each row adds up: D27-364. GST and use-by at delivery: D27-238.

A9.12Who may ask to buy

Who may ask to buy is a setting for each property, by role. The owner or an Admin changes it: Who may ask on Purchasing. It takes effect at once. Everybody else who opens it can read it.

It starts as the Inventory Manager, the general manager, the owner and an Admin. The general manager still approves every purchase, whoever asked.

Why it works this way

A setting per property, changed without a developer, with the kitchen or housekeeping switched on when the owner wants them: D18-2, D22-11. A role, not a person, because staff leave; switched off, never deleted.

A9.13Equipment

Equipment is the register of everything durable in the hotel, the restaurant and the rooms. Things used up (rice, gas, soap) are the Store's.

To add a piece of equipment

  1. Open Equipment and tap Add an item.
  2. Type What is it (“Split AC 1.5 ton”) and its Category: pick one this property already uses, or type a new one.
  3. Choose How is it kept track of: One by one, with a tag, or A count in one place (twelve kettles in one room is one line of 12).
  4. For a tagged item, the next free Tag is offered. If it already carries a printed label, type that instead.
  5. Choose Where is it: A room, or Another place such as the Main store.
  6. Add the Status, Supplier, Bought on, Cost, in rupees, Warranty ends and Service contract (AMC) ends if you know them. Blank if nobody knows.
  7. Tap Add. Its page opens.

Report a repair raises a repair, as Work orders does (A6): the item shows as under repair until the repair is closed. Under repair, at the top, marks it by hand. Only a tagged item is repaired; a counted line is retired or replaced.

Taking equipment out of the store

A piece of equipment that leaves a store waits for the Inventory Manager's yes. Anybody who can see it may ask.

  1. Open the item. Somebody who may not move equipment sees Ask to take out; tap it. (The Inventory Manager and managers tap Move: out of a store, it is asked for in the same way.)
  2. Choose To a room and the room, or To another place.
  3. Type Why. The person deciding reads it.
  4. Tap Ask. The message says “Asked - it moves when the Inventory Manager approves it”.

The Inventory Manager decides

  1. Open Equipment. At the top: “One request waits in the approvals inbox”, with the item, where it is going, who asked and why. In the list it says Waiting to leave the store.
  2. Tap Approve; or Refuse, which asks why. (The item's own page has the same buttons.)
  3. Approved, it is in its new place. Refused, it stays in the store, with the reason beside it.

Under repair, and retiring

  • Under repair asks What is wrong, or what was done (optional). Back in use undoes it later.
  • Retire asks why (broken beyond repair, lost, sold, given away). It stays in the register as retired, with who, when and why. A tagged item retired by somebody who may not approve, such as the hotel manager, waits in the approvals inbox for the Inventory Manager.

Watch out

  • Changing, moving and retiring equipment is for the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and the Inventory Manager. Anybody else sees those buttons grey, and may only ask for something to leave the store.
  • While a request waits, the item cannot be changed, moved or retired: it is decided first.
  • A move between rooms, or back into a store, needs nobody's yes.
  • Who did what in an item's history is shown to the owner, an Admin, the GM and the Inventory Manager. Others see a dash, except their own name.
  • A retired item is kept exactly as it was retired. Nothing is deleted.

Why it works this way

Equipment leaving the store waits for the Inventory Manager, with the owner, an Admin or the GM as backup; anybody who can see it may ask; stock goes out with no approval: S12, S15. Retiring a tagged item, in the same inbox: S16. Where a thing is comes from its approved moves, so a refused request leaves it where it was and nothing has to be undone.
