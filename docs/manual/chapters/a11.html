Chapter A11

Reports

For managers, the accountant and the owner; reception and the store for their own reports. Where the reports are, what every report has in common, and which report answers which question: the day, the month and the year, money owed, the statutory registers, the accountant's export, and the Store's reports. Reports only read: opening one changes nothing.

A11.1Finding a report

In the Front office menu, the part called Reports lists three screens: Night audit, the screen that runs the audit (see A7), All reports, and Manager report. Every other report is one click away in All reports (/reports), or one search away with Find a screen (Ctrl K, A0.6).

All reports puts the reports in four groups, each with one line saying what the report is for. You see only the reports your role may open.

GroupReportsWho opens them

Money owedCity ledger, No show, Guest ledger, Cashier shift, Supplier dues, Petty cash (A11.6)The same, except that the Revenue Manager does not open City ledger, Guest ledger, Cashier shift or Petty cash; Supplier dues only the Owner, Admin, General Manager and Accountant

One more screen sits in the Reports part of the menu: Accounting export, the month as a file for the accountant (A11.8). The Kitchen Manager, Chef, Waiter, Bar, Housekeeping and the Inventory Manager do not open All reports; the Inventory Manager reaches the Store's reports from the Store (A11.9).

To keep a report at the top

  1. Open All reports.
  2. Tap the star on the report's card. It fills in yellow.
  3. The report now also shows under Starred, above the groups. Tap the star again to take it off.

To find a report that is not in your menu

  1. Press Ctrl K, or click Find a screen at the top or at the foot of the menu.
  2. Type a word: the report's name, or a word such as “gst”, “tally” or “frro”.
  3. Click the report, or press Enter. “Not in your menu” beside it only means it is not listed in your menu: you may open it.

Watch out

  • A star is kept in this browser only. On another computer, or in another browser, your stars are not there.
  • While you type in Find a report, the Starred group is hidden. No match says “No report matches that”: try a word from a description instead.
  • The Night audit menu screen closes the day. The Night audit report only reads it (A11.4).

Why it works this way

The reports in the format of the owner's own eZee exports: D17-62. A shorter menu, with everything reachable by search: Reports lists only Night audit, All reports and Manager report (the owner, 15 September). The printed night audit is a report in All reports, apart from the screen that runs the audit: D17-78. No reports for housekeeping, the kitchen manager and the Inventory Manager, because they carry revenue: D27-141. The money-owed reports are not the revenue manager's: D27-147. Passports stay with the people who check guests in: D25-38, D27-122. The Store's readers: D27-240.

A11.2Every report works the same way

A report is laid out like a printed page: the hotel's name, the report's name and its date behind an orange bar, then one line of facts (the date or the period it covers), then the figures in bands, then the notes, then who printed it and when.

The buttons at the top

  • Print: prints the report alone, without the menu or the buttons.
  • PDF, Excel, CSV: save the report as a file (Figure A11-04). The file is made on the server from the same figures as the screen. They wait until the report has loaded. The message “PDF saved” names the file; if it fails, “The PDF was not made” says why.
  • Guest ledger, Cashier shift, GST sales register and the Form III register have Print and CSV only.

Reading the figures

  • A dash (-) is not a zero. It means the system does not hold that figure. The report's notes say which ones.
  • A figure in red is below zero. It is real: for example a guest who paid more than the bill came to.
  • An empty report still prints its date, and says in words that there was nothing (Figure A11-22).
  • Most reports print dates as 09/10/2026: day, month, year.

Which day a report is for

Except Voids, which has a From and a To, the reports have no date to choose. Each is for the hotel's business date (A1.1), or that date's month or year:

CoversReports

The 1st of the month to the business dateGuest ledger, GST sales register, Form III register, Stock movement by day, Consumption, Wastage and breakage, Count differences, Supplier spend, Supplier dues (owed on the business date; paid in the month so far)

Watch out

  • Until the night audit has run, the day's figures can still change. A report opened at noon is the day so far.
  • Downloading a file is not for everyone. Reception and a Viewer may read the reports; a PDF, Excel or CSV from the server is for the owner, an administrator, the general manager, the hotel manager, the revenue manager or the accountant. Anyone else is told “Downloading reports is for the owner, an administrator, the general manager, the hotel manager, the revenue manager or the accountant. Ask one of them.”
  • A wide report scrolls sideways inside its card. Click Collapse at the foot of the menu to give it more room.

Why it works this way

The layout of the owner's eZee exports: D17-62. A real Excel file and a CSV: D17-31. PDFs made on the server: D17-32. Dates as 25/08/2026: D17-77. A figure the system does not hold is a dash, never a zero, because a zero would be read as a fact.

A11.3The Manager report

Open Manager report in the menu (/reports/manager). It is the day in three columns: Today, PTD (period to date: from the 1st of this month) and YTD (year to date: from 1 April, the start of the financial year). It is read afresh each time it opens.

Its bands, top to bottom: Room Charges, Extra Charges (late check-out, early check-in, no-show retention, food, beverage, laundry, spa, minibar and the rest), Tax, Revenue, Payment, City Ledger, Advance Deposit Ledger, Guest Ledger, Room Summary, Statistics, POS Summary and POS Payment Summary (the restaurant), and Revenue Summary (without tax).

LineMeans
Sold RoomRooms sold. Two switches on Front desk rules (A17.11) decide what counts: A complimentary room counts as sold (on to begin with) and A day use counts as half a room sold (off to begin with). The note under the report says how this hotel counts: “Sold Room, occupancy and the average rate count rooms as this hotel says (Settings, Front desk: complimentary rooms, day use).”
Occupancy Rate (%)Rooms sold, as a share of the rooms that could be sold.
Average Daily Rate (ADR)Room charges divided by rooms sold: what a sold room earned, on average. Extras, food and tax are not in it.
Revenue Per Available RoomRoom charges divided by every room that could be sold. Unlike ADR, it falls when rooms stay empty.

Watch out

  • While A complimentary room counts as sold is on, a complimentary room lifts the occupancy and lowers the average rate, because it is sold and earns nothing. Switch it off and it does neither.
  • Tonight's room charge is in Today even before it is posted, at the booking's rate.
  • Some City Ledger and Guest Ledger lines show “-”: the system does not hold them yet. The notes under the report say which.

Why it works this way

The owner's eZee Manager report, line for line: D17-62. A complimentary room counts as sold, as in eZee: D17-72. One rule for occupancy on every screen, this report's, and the report read afresh when opened: D27-151. What counts as sold follows the hotel's two switches, in this report and in the statistics (A11.5): D27-307.

A11.4The Night audit report

Open All reports, Night audit report (/reports/night-audit). It is one day, to the paisa: every room charged and every room given free, who checked out, the day's sales, every receipt, the rooms and guests, and the restaurant. It is wider than the screen: it scrolls sideways.

  • Booking is the booking's reference. Nrml. Tariff is the room's normal price that night; Ofrd. Tariff is what this guest is charged. A room type with no normal price shows “-” in Var %.
  • Checkin By is a dash when the system holds no record of who checked the guest in.
  • In Checked Out, a Balance may be below zero: the guest paid more than the bill came to.

Between them are Misc. Charges (everything charged that day apart from the rooms) and, at the end, POS Payment Summary (how the restaurant's bills were paid).

Watch out

  • Occupied means checked in. A booking for tonight whose guest has not arrived is under Reserve.
  • Before the audit runs, a guest who has not arrived is still in Room Charges. They go when the night audit marks the no-show.
  • A night's room charge is shown on the night it pays for, whatever day it was posted.
  • A bill sent to a company is not a receipt: it is on the Manager report as Charges Raised.
  • Charges, item discounts and tips are not recorded in this system: in the POS Summary they show “-”.

Why it works this way

A report in All reports, not part of the screen that runs the audit: D17-78. The Booking column prints the booking reference: D17-76. The guest not yet arrived stays in Room Charges until the audit marks the no-show, so this report agrees with the Manager report: D17-94.

Voids: what was voided, closed unpaid or struck in the restaurant

Voids (/reports/voids) lists every restaurant bill that was voided after it was paid, every order closed unpaid and every dish struck, for the days you choose, by outlet and day. It opens on the month so far; change From and To to look at other days.

  • Voided bill: a paid bill the owner, an administrator or the general manager voided. Its amount is what the guest had paid. Asked by names the waiter, when the general manager voided it on a waiter's request.
  • Closed unpaid: an order closed without payment, with the reason typed. Its amount is what the guest would have paid.
  • Struck dish: a dish taken off an order. Its amount is its price times its quantity, before GST. Kitchen had it says Yes when it was struck after its kitchen ticket was sent, No when it was struck before.

Watch out

  • An order closed unpaid shows who closed it and when from 28 October 2026 (D27-372); one closed before that shows “-” for both, as only its reason was kept.
  • A dish struck before the kitchen had it may have no reason: the reason column then shows “-”.
  • The three kinds are never added together: a bill's amount has GST, a struck dish's does not.
  • A bill given free (NC) and a discount are not voids and are not on this report.
  • The people who can open it are those who open the other reports (A11.1). A waiter, the bar, the kitchen manager and the chef cannot. A PDF, Excel or CSV is for the owner, an administrator, the general manager, the hotel manager, the revenue manager or the accountant.

Why it works this way

The restaurant's voids as one report, on screen and as a PDF: D27-370. Voiding a paid bill: D27-324. Striking a dish the kitchen has: D27-119.

A11.5Occupancy and statistics: the month and the year

  • Monthly occupancy (/reports/monthly-occupancy): the business date's month as a chart, one bar per day. Full houses and empty days both stand out.
  • Monthly statistics (/reports/monthly-stats): the same month as a grid, one row per day.
  • Yearly statistics (/reports/yearly-stats): the same columns, one row per month from January to December, then a Grand Total worked out again from the year's figures, not an average of the months.
  • All three count rooms sold the way the Manager report does, by the hotel's two switches (A11.3, A17.11): Occ %, ADR and the bars follow them. Night Sold (the paying rooms) and Complimentary stay columns of their own.

Watch out

  • Days after today have figures too. Nights already booked are counted at the booking's rate. They change if the booking changes.
  • Occ % and ADR count rooms sold as the hotel's two switches say: with A complimentary room counts as sold on, complimentary rooms are counted with the rooms sold; with A day use counts as half a room sold on, each day use is half a room. The first note under the report says which: “Occupancy and ADR count rooms sold as this hotel's switches say (Settings, Front desk rules): a complimentary room counts as sold; a day use is not counted.”
  • Rooms are counted from the day the hotel first traded (the note under the report gives the date). Days before it show nothing.

Why it works this way

A stay's room charge is spread over its nights, and nights booked ahead show at the booking's rate, as in eZee: D17-71. Complimentary rooms count as sold unless the hotel switches that off: D17-72, D27-307. Available rooms start on the first trading day: D17-93.

A11.6Money owed: City ledger, No show, Guest ledger, Cashier shift, Supplier dues, Petty cash

City ledger

City ledger (/reports/city-ledger) is the bills sent to a company or a booking site, account by account, for the business date: Opening Balance, Debit (bills sent to the account), Credit (what the account paid, recorded on Company accounts), Closing Balance.

The switch is on when the report opens. Turn it off to see every account, even those with nothing on them. The switch itself is never printed.

No show

No show (/reports/no-show) lists the bookings marked no-show that were due to arrive on the business date: booking, guest, room type, arrival, departure, pax, source and rate. On a day when everybody came, it still prints and says “Nobody failed to arrive on this day.” (Figure A11-22).

Guest ledger

Guest ledger (/reports/guest-ledger) is every guest's bill that moved from the 1st of the month to the business date: Opening, Charges, Received, Closing.

Opening is the balance before the window; Closing includes the last day. A charge that was reversed, and its reversal, are both left out.

Cashier shift

Cashier shift (/reports/cashier-shift) is what each cashier took on the business date, by method: how many receipts, and how much.

Petty cash

Petty cash (/reports/petty-cash) is every expense paid out of a restaurant's tin (A8.9) and the front desk's drawer (A4.10), together. It opens on the 1st of the month to the business date; change the two dates above it to see other days.

  • Expenses, by day: one line per expense - the date, where (the outlet, or Front desk), its category, what it was for, the amount, who recorded it, its approval (Approved, Waiting, Asked for more, Refused, or Not asked for one from before approvals began) and Yes under Photo when the bill's photo is on it. Each day ends with its total, and the report with the total of all.
  • By category: Kitchen, Housekeeping, Maintenance, Office and Other - how many expenses, and how much is Counted, Waiting for a yes and Refused.

Watch out

  • Petty cash totals count an expense once it is approved, as the drawer and the tin count it. One waiting for a yes or refused is listed but not added; By category shows it in its own column.
  • In Petty cash the restaurant's expenses fall on their business day; the front desk's on the business day its drawer was opened.
  • A dash for a category is an expense recorded before categories began (27 October 2026).
  • A receipt that was reversed is not money the cashier holds, so it is not counted - the same rule as the Manager report.
  • “not on record” is never filled in with a name: naming somebody would be worse than the gap.
  • The Revenue Manager does not see City ledger, Guest ledger, Cashier shift or Petty cash.

Supplier dues

The Supplier dues report (/reports/supplier-dues) is what the hotel owes, not what it is owed: the suppliers' bills taken on credit (A9.11). Owed to each supplier lists, under each supplier, every bill dated by the business date and not paid by it - its date, its due date, the days it is overdue and its amount - with the supplier's total, and the total owed at the foot. Payments made lists the bills paid from the 1st of the month to the business date: the day, the supplier, the bill, how it was paid, its reference, who recorded it and the amount, with the total paid. It is the Owner's, an Admin's, the General Manager's and the Accountant's only; it has PDF, Excel and CSV like the others.

Supplier statement, and TDS on suppliers

The Supplier statement (/reports/supplier-statement, or Statement beside a supplier on Supplier dues) is one supplier's ledger for the days chosen: the opening balance, then each bill, payment, TDS, credit note and reversal by date with the balance after it, and the closing balance. Pick the supplier and the From and To dates.

TDS on suppliers (/reports/tds) lists the TDS deducted on supplier payments for the days chosen, by section (194C, 194Q, 194J, 194I) and supplier: the figures for the quarterly TDS return (26Q). Both are read by the Accountant, the GM, the owner and an Admin, and download as PDF, Excel and CSV (D27-372).

Why it works this way

The city ledger's Debit is the bills transferred to the account: D17-74. Company payments are recorded on Company accounts and counted by the day they reached the hotel: D27-242. Money owed and money taken are not the revenue manager's: D27-147.

A11.7Statutory: Lodging tax, GST sales register, Form III register

These are filed with the government, not just read. This system files nothing: they are what the accountant, or whoever files, works from.

Lodging tax

Lodging tax (/reports/lodging-tax) is the month's check-out bills, one row for each day a bill was issued, with that day's first and last bill number, the room rent, and CGST and SGST.

The tax columns carry the room rent less its discount. Everything else on those bills is in Other Bills, except alcohol and its VAT, which are not on this sheet.

GST sales register

GST sales register (/reports/gst-sales) is every invoice and credit note issued from the 1st of the month to the business date, as GSTR-1 wants them: invoice, date, recipient, GSTIN, type (B2B or B2C), place, taxable value, CGST, SGST, total.

An invoice is listed on the business day it belongs to, not the moment it was printed. A credit note says which invoice it credits.

An invoice made out to a registered business - a company billed, or the guest's own company with the GSTIN typed on the booking (A2.2) - has its name under Recipient and its GSTIN in the GSTIN column, and Type says B2B. A dash under GSTIN is an unregistered recipient, and Type says B2C. A credit note has the type of the invoice it credits. The CSV carries the same Type column: D27-372.

Form III register

Form III register (/reports/c-form) is every foreign guest who arrived from the 1st of the month to the business date, each foreign adult in a room on a row of their own: passport and visa, arrival, departure, and the FRRO portal's numbers.

A guest not yet filed shows “not filed” in red; one who has left without the departure filed, “departure not filed”. A passport not captured at check-in shows “not on record”. The CSV carries every Form III field.

Watch out

  • Nothing here is filed by itself. The Form III register is the record of what was filed on the portal; the portal numbers are typed on the Form III screen (Finance part of the menu).
  • The Form III register is for the Owner, Admin, General Manager, Hotel Manager and Reception only: it shows passports. The Accountant does not see it.
  • Lodging tax counts the bills issued. A stay still in house is not on it yet (compare the Accounting export, A11.8).

Why it works this way

Lodging tax reads the room rent less its discount, and the group's accountant confirms before anything is filed: D17-99. No rate in the CGST and SGST headings: D24-23. Alcohol and its VAT kept off the sheet: D22-45. Passports only for the people who check guests in: D25-38; not the Accountant: D27-122.

A11.8Accounting export: the month for the accountant

Accounting export (/reports/accounting) hands the accountant the hotel's days as a file. It reads the same figures the reports print, and changes nothing. It is for the Owner, Admin, General Manager and Accountant; find it with Find a screen (“tally”, “day book”, “export”).

To export the month

  1. Open Accounting export. From is the 1st of the month and To is the business date. Change them if you need to. Both days are included.
  2. Under Format, choose Day book (CSV) or Day book (Excel), whichever your accountant asks for.
  3. Check the table below: it is the file, drawn on the screen.
  4. Tap Export. The file is saved on this computer.

The day book has one row a day: room revenue and its tax, extra charges and their tax, the restaurant and its tax, discount, alcohol and its VAT, sales, GST, receipts, what companies paid, and whether the night audit has run.

Under the table, four totals for the range: Sales, Tax, Receipts and Received from companies, then each company payment on its own line.

Watch out

  • A day not closed is still exported, and says so (Not run). Tell the accountant before sending a month that is not closed.
  • Tally Prime (XML) and Tally ERP 9 (XML) make no file yet: “Waiting on the ledger names in your books.” Export stays grey while one of them is chosen.
  • A range where nothing happened says “Nothing was charged or received in this range. The file will have a row for each day, all of them nil.”
  • This and the Lodging tax report will differ in a month with a stay still in house. Both are right.

Why it works this way

All three kinds of export offered, whoever exports picks: D18-16. Both a CSV and a real Excel file: D22-37. The Accountant opens it: D27-122. Company payments in the day book as receipts of their own: D27-242. The Tally files wait for the ledger names in the hotel's own books, because a name made up here would be created inside the hotel's Tally on import.
