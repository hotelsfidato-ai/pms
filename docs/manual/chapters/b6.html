Chapter B6

Each module in depth

For the owner. The system is divided into services, one per job of the hotel. For each: what it is and holds, the decisions that shaped it and why, what was left out and why, and what is still open. Taken from each service's own brief (src/modules/<service>/MODULE.md), its list of faults (DEFECTS.md), the code and your decisions, as they stand on 9 October.

B6.1How the system is divided

Since 15 September the code is sorted by service, at your request, so that several helpers can work at once, each in one service, without breaking another. A service lives in three places: its screens in src/modules/<service>/, its server routes in api/src/modules/<service>/, and its database checks in db/tests/modules/<service>.sql. Each keeps three notes:

FileWhat it holds
MODULE.mdThe service's brief: its screens, routes and tables, the decisions that bind it, what is open, and requests to other services.
DEFECTS.mdEvery fault found in it, numbered with the service's prefix (FD-1, POS-1, FIN-1 …), what was done about it, and the fix waiting for the open ones.
CHANGELOG.mdWhat each working session did in it, and what it proved.
  • One front door each. A service uses another only through that service's index.ts. A test refuses any reach past it (B8.6), so a change inside the restaurant cannot quietly break finance.
  • The shared core (the design system, the frame of every screen, the data seam, the server's wiring and the one sequence of migrations) is changed only by the main chat.
  • Switches per hotel. Some services are switched on per hotel in Settings: the restaurant, banquets, laundry, spa, alcohol (VAT), the channel manager, loyalty and "Reception can use the POS", among others. A switched-off service is not in that hotel's menu.
  • Not built yet. On 16 September every missing service got a folder and a "Coming soon" page; on 28 September each moved to where it will belong, marked "Not built yet", seen by the owner, admins and GMs only (D27-87). On 30 September you asked for every one to be built (D27-178), and the booking engine was dropped in favour of the channel manager's (D27-180). Today only Online payments and Room systems still carry the mark.
  • Nothing is decided by a helper. Each service's choices are your decisions, asked one by one with a recommendation (HANDOVER § 5). Where your answer went against the recommendation, the decision says so.

In each section below, Open lists the faults still open in that service's DEFECTS.md on 9 October; a fault that is fixed is not listed.

Why it works this way

On 27 August two helpers changed the same folder at the same time for two hours, and a day's work was lost beyond recovery (AGENTS.md). One service per helper, one front door per service, and a claim taken before the first change (npm run claim) are what stop that happening again.

B6.2Front desk

Check-in, check-out, and everything the desk does to a stay in between. Built, always on.

ScreensRooms, Calendar, Arrivals (Today, Upcoming), In house (In house now, Due out), Walk-ins (a record: a walk-in is now New booking opened from an empty room), the stay panel; in Settings: Front desk rules, Market segments, Reservation types, Ready reasons, Holidays, ID types, VIP levels.
HoldsEach stay's state (arriving, in house, checked out), which room it is in, its inclusions, the hotel's desk lists and holidays.
Decided
  • One meaning for "In house" and "Empty" everywhere: In house is checked in and not checked out, whatever the departure date says; Empty is nobody in it and sellable tonight (D21-36, D21-37, D23-9). Two screens once disagreed about who was in a room.
  • Nobody is put into a room somebody is still in; a room given ahead of time to a booking not yet here is allowed (D23-12, D24-15, D24-26; B7.2).
  • One page, three buttons: Save booking, Save and assign room, Check in now. Your standing rule: nothing takes more than two pages or three clicks (D27-158, D27-163, D27-236).
  • The first night is charged at check-in, with its GST, dated the business date; the night audit posts each later night and never the first one twice, so a bill printed after check-in never misses the room (D27-204, D27-205).
  • Express check-in counts only this stay's own confirmed online ID, never a document from an earlier visit, and never applies to a foreign guest (D27-236, D27-242).
  • A late departure is asked only on or after the departure date, and once: a guest leaving before it is leaving early, whatever the hour, and is never asked or charged a late fee; one already recorded is not asked or charged again (D27-314; A1.6, B7.11). The date is the hotel's business date, not the clock's.
  • The desk's lists read only their own days: Arrivals, In house, Due out and the check-in's room list ask the server for the day's bookings, not every booking ever made (793 on the demo hotel, about three seconds each time); Rooms, the Calendar and the Dashboard read only the dates on screen; after a payment only that stay's bill and the open list are read again. Nothing a person reads changed: D27-313, D27-316.
  • A check-in days early is allowed, warned and confirmed (D27-193). A day use is charged at check-in and stays a day use (D27-225, D27-230). A returning foreign guest's nationality is never overwritten by "India" typed at check-in; a stay is capped at 90 nights (D27-197).
  • Per-hotel desk rules: the desk may overbook (on), an advance before check-in (none to start), weekend days, the default ID type, nationality and title (D27-243, D27-246).
  • Controls a person cannot use are hidden (D24-18, D24-27). When a hotel's business date is behind the real day, one warning line in the top bar of every screen (D27-237).
Left outCheck-out, moves and other changes to a stay are refused offline; a check-in is held, with a warning (A0.8). The full list of bookings is deliberately not kept on a shared desk computer. The plan's own test, somebody new doing a stay alone, waits for you to arrange it (D21-48, D21-57).
OpenNothing.
