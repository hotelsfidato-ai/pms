Chapter A3

Check-in

For reception, the hotel manager, the general manager, an Admin and the owner: everyone who may check guests in. How to check a guest in from Arrivals, give a room ahead of arrival, take the guest's ID, check in a guest from abroad and keep the Form III register, ask for an advance, let a guest in early, and check in a guest who sent their ID from their own page.

A3.1Checking a guest in, from Arrivals

Check-in is one window. The room and the ID are on the left, the stay is on the right, and there is one button at the foot, Check in. A line under the button says what is still missing, so nobody has to guess why it is grey.

To check a guest in

  1. Open Arrivals (A1.3) and click the guest's row. The stay panel opens over the list (A1.4).
  2. Click Check in, the orange button at the top of the panel. The window Check in and the guest's name opens, with the booking's reference and room type under the name.
  3. The room. If the booking holds a room, it is shown on one line, for example “Room 204 · Inspected”, with Change room beside it. If it holds none, the rooms are listed for you to pick (A3.2).
  4. The ID. Choose the Document and type its Number, or give a reason for skipping it (A3.3). A guest from abroad has more to fill in (A3.4).
  5. The stay, in the box on the right: the dates and the nights (or “day use”), the adults and children, the meal plan, Rack rate and Offered. Offered is the rate this guest pays (“Complimentary” for a complimentary stay); Rack rate is the room type's listed rate, or a dash where it has none. If the guest turned up with more or fewer people, or a different meal plan, click Change beside it: the Adults and Children boxes, or the Meal plan list, open for editing.
  6. Ask the guest about offers and set Guest agreed to offers. Its own words: “Ask them. Unticked, no offers are sent. Kept against their phone and email at every hotel of the group.”
  7. Click Check in at the foot of the window.

When it goes through, a message says “A3 Test Iyer is in house” (the guest's name) with “Room 209 · the arrivals count has dropped.” and the panel behind changes: its status reads In house and its orange button becomes Settle and check out (A5). The first night's room charge and its GST are posted to the bill at that moment, so Charged at the foot is already filled in. A complimentary room is charged nothing. A hotel with no GST rule for rooms still checks the guest in, but posts nothing: the night audit, which will not run until that rule is set, posts the night.

Watch out

  • Where Check in is offered: on a booking opened from Arrivals (Today), All bookings or Walk-ins. The Upcoming list is for planning and does not offer it (A1.3). It is shown only to people who may change bookings: the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and reception. Housekeeping and the Revenue Manager read the list without it.
  • A walk-in goes through this same check-in, from New booking's Check in now (A2.5), with the same checks.
  • A group's rooms are checked in one at a time, each from its own booking. The panel lists the group's other rooms on the right (A2.6).
  • If it is refused, the window stays open and a message, “Could not check in”, says why. Nothing was saved: the guest is still due in.
  • A red line at the top of the window, “… has the phone or email of …, who is blacklisted. Reason: …. Check-in is not refused - you decide.”, means a booking site's guest matches a blacklisted guest. It warns; a person decides (A1.3).

Why it works this way

Check-in was five steps and eight clicks; the owner's rule is two pages or three clicks, so the room, the ID and the stay are on one page under one button: D27-158, D27-163. It is laid out like New booking, with the room and the ID on the left and the stay on the right, each fact with its Change: D27-236. The first night is charged at check-in and the night audit never charges it twice: D27-204; with no GST rule for rooms the guest is still checked in: D27-205. Offers only to a guest who said yes, asked face to face: D25-39. A blacklisted guest is warned of, never refused: D22-8.

A3.2The room

A guest can be given a room the night before, or choose one at check-in. Either way, only a room that is ready and free can take a guest.

To give a room ahead of arrival

  1. Click Assign room, at the top right of Rooms and of the Calendar (A1.7). It is offered only to people who may change bookings.
  2. The window Assign a room says “Give an arriving booking a room before they get here.” and the business date. Under Waiting for a room are the bookings that are confirmed, have a room type and no room number yet: the guest, the room type, the dates and the reference. Click one.
  3. Under Room, choose a room from Choose a room…. Each line is the room number, its type and its rack rate, and “(different type)” after a room of another type. The list leaves out rooms that are out of service and rooms with a guest in them now.
  4. Click Assign the room. The message is “A3 Test Desai is in room 204” (the guest and the room). If the room is not the type booked, it adds “Note: … is not the room type they booked.”

If the room is taken for part of the stay or is out of service, the message says so, for example “Room 204 is out of service.”, and you choose again. When every arrival has a room, the window says “Every arrival has a room”. A booking that already has a room is not listed. To change a room already given, choose another at check-in with Change room: the room you check the guest into replaces the one the booking held.

The room at check-in

If the booking holds no room, the Check in window lists every room, the rooms of the room type booked first, one to a button. Each shows its number and its state. A room that can take the guest is white, with inspected or clean. The others are greyed and cannot be chosen; point at one to read why:

A greyed room saysMeans
dirty: “Not cleaned yet.”Housekeeping has not cleaned it (A6). Only a clean or inspected room can take a guest.
out-of-service: “Held out of service.”It is blocked for repair (A6.7).
Occupied: “… is in this room.”A guest is in it now.

A room of another type says “Deluxe (different type)” (its own type) beside its number. Choosing one is an upgrade, as one given with Assign room: the guest is checked in to it at the booked rate (below). The window's note says so: “A room of a different type is an upgrade: the booked rate stays.” The chosen room shows its type on its one line too, for example “Room 302 · Clean · Deluxe (different type)”.

Click a free room. The list folds into one line, “Room 301 · Inspected”, and Change room opens it again. Until a room is chosen, Check in is grey and the line under it says “Pick a room.” If the room the booking holds has turned dirty, the line says “Room 304: Not cleaned yet.” and the list opens for you to choose another.

A room whose last guest has not left yet shows a yellow line, “Omkar Shinde has not left this room yet.”, and “Guest still in” beside the room. Check in is not greyed for it, but the check-in is refused: “… is still in room 203. Check them out first, or choose another room.”

Watch out

  • The list offers the rooms of the type booked, and the room the booking already holds, whatever its type.
  • Another room type: Assign room accepts it, and says “Another type is allowed - it will be noted on the assignment.” The check-in accepts it too, and its message adds “Note: Deluxe is not the room type they booked.” The stay panel's Room then shows the room's own type and the type booked: “302 · Deluxe · booked Superior”.
  • The guest keeps the booked rate. A room of another type changes the room, not the price: the bill charges the rate agreed on the booking. To charge for the better room, change the rate on the Bill tab (A4.6).
  • The server asks the same questions as the window, and refuses by itself: a dirty room, “That room has not been cleaned yet.”; a room out of service, “That room is held out of service.”; a room held by another booking for part of the stay, “Room 301 is held by … for part of that stay.”

Why it works this way

A dirty room is shown, greyed, with the reason, rather than hidden: hiding it would teach nobody why. The check-in refuses a room a late guest is still in: D23-12. The booked room on one line, the grid only when it is needed: D27-236. A button the person may not use is hidden, not greyed: D24-18. An upgrade given with Assign room is checked in and noted, at the booked rate: D27-328. The Check in window lists every room type, so the upgrade can be given there: D27-330.

A3.3The guest's ID

The ID is asked on the same window, under the room. Indian properties must record a guest's identity document. It can be skipped, but then the reason is kept in the stay's note.

  • Nationality. Two letters, “IN” for India: “Two letters, IN for India. Anything else puts the stay on the Form III register.” It starts on the nationality on the guest's record, else the hotel's own (A17.11). A guest from abroad is A3.4.
  • Document. Not recorded, Passport, Aadhaar, Driving licence, Voter ID or Other. A hotel can leave some out and choose which one starts (A17.10, A17.11). Choosing one opens Number.
  • Number. The screen checks it by its type. An Aadhaar is 12 digits, never starting with 0 or 1, with UIDAI's check digit right: “That Aadhaar number is not right - a digit is mistyped. Check it against the card.” A passport is 6 to 15 letters and digits: “A passport number is 6 to 15 letters and digits, e.g. A1234567.” Any other document: “A document number is 4 to 40 characters.” The message is in red under the boxes and Check in stays grey.
  • A photo of the ID. Where the hotel keeps ID photos, the desk can add one for a guest who sent none: the front of the ID, the back if it has one, then a tick, “The guest agreed to the hotel keeping this photo”, and Add the photo. Cover the first eight digits of an Aadhaar first: “A photo showing the full number will be removed and we will ask you again.” It is kept encrypted for the owner, an Admin and the GM. Where the hotel cannot keep ID photos, the window says why in the server's words.

If the ID cannot be taken now

  1. Leave Document on Not recorded.
  2. Type Reason for skipping. It is required, for example “Scanner offline - to be captured at breakfast”.
  3. Until the ID is recorded, or a reason is given, Check in stays grey: “Record the ID document - type and number - or give a reason for skipping it.”

The other adults in the room

Every adult in the room is recorded: their name, nationality, and ID type and number. A foreigner also has the full Form III details and a row of their own on the Form III screen (A3.5). Under the ID, Adults in the room names the guest on the booking and each adult added, with a pill: “2 adults, 1 recorded”, yellow while somebody is missing. A child is not asked for, though one can be added.

The same block is on the stay panel, on the Stay tab, for anybody who arrives later or whose ID comes later; and on the card New booking shows when a walk-in has been checked in. It never stops a check-in: Check in does not wait for it, and each person is saved on their own.

To add an adult

  1. Click Add an adult.
  2. Type their Full name, as on the document. Nationality starts on the guest's: change it if theirs is different.
  3. Choose the Document and type its Number. The checks are those above. With no document to hand, leave it: no reason is asked, the name is saved, and the line says “ID not recorded”. They are counted as recorded once the number is typed.
  4. For a foreigner the Form III boxes open. Fill in what you have: none is needed now. Same as the guest copies the lead guest's visit answers (where they arrived from, the purpose, the reference address and the rest), so a family's are typed once; their phones and document stay their own, and any box can still be changed. The button shows once the lead guest has answered. The rest can be done with Edit, or with Complete details on the Form III screen.
  5. Click Save. It is on the stay at once, whether or not the check-in goes ahead.

Edit opens the same window with their details. It says what is on record, an Aadhaar as its last four digits, “Fill in the document only to replace it.” Remove asks “Remove them and their ID?”: Keep or Remove. Removed, their ID and their answers go with them. Once a Form III number is recorded for a person, the line says “Form III filed: corrected on the register.”: they stay on the stay (the record is kept for a year), and their details are corrected with Complete details (A3.5).

Watch out

  • A nationality that is not a country, such as ZZ, is refused: “"ZZ" is not a country code. Type the two letters, e.g. IN, GB or US.”
  • "IN" typed in the box never replaces a foreign nationality already on the guest's record. A wrong nationality is corrected on the guest's record (A10.3).
  • Adults in the room is for the people who record IDs: the owner, an Admin, the GM, the Hotel Manager and Reception. Nobody else sees it, and a companion's ID is kept as the guest's is.
  • Each room of a group has its own adults, on its own stay.
  • A room move, a longer stay or a change of dates does not touch them. With fewer adults on the booking than people recorded, nobody is taken off and there is no warning.
  • A cancelled, voided or no-show booking keeps who was on it, and nobody can be added to it.
  • The ID photo is still one per stay: the guest the booking is under.

Why it works this way

The ID and Form III's boxes are one set of fields shared by check-in and New booking, so they cannot ask different questions: D27-158. The hotel's own starting nationality and ID: D27-243; ID types the hotel does not take are left out: D27-260. "IN" never overwrites a foreign guest's nationality: D27-197. The desk adds the ID photo for a guest who sent none: D27-210. One ID photo per stay: D27-212. Every adult in a room is recorded, entered at check-in or later, and check-in is never blocked; a missing person is a warning: D27-382.

A3.4A guest from abroad: Form III at check-in

A guest whose nationality is anything but IN goes on the Form III register. Form III is the report of a foreign guest to the government's FRRO portal. It was called Form C until the 2025 Act. The arrival is reported within 24 hours of arrival, and the departure within 24 hours of leaving. The hotel is responsible for it.

To check in a guest from abroad

  1. Type the two letters of the guest's country in Nationality (for example GB). The Form III boxes open under the document.
  2. Choose the Document and type its Number, as in A3.3. A passport is the usual one.
  3. Fill in the starred boxes, which are needed now: Visa or OCI number, Visa type, Phone in India, Arrived from, Purpose of visit and Previous place of stay.
  4. The other boxes can be filled in later, from the Form III screen (A3.5): mobile numbers, gender, date of birth, the passport's and the visa's issue and expiry, the address abroad, the portal's arrival details and a reference address in India.
  5. Click Check in. Until the starred boxes are filled in it is grey, and the line under it says “Form III still needs: Arrived from, Purpose of visit, Previous place of stay.” (the boxes still blank).
  • Nepal and Bhutan. A citizen of Nepal (NP) or Bhutan (BT) needs no visa, so the two visa boxes are not asked.
  • An ID skipped for a reason. The visa boxes are not asked either. The visit's boxes still are: the guest answers those, no scanner needed.
  • An expired passport or visa is warned of, not refused: Check in anyway appears with “The passport expired on 2026-01-15. It is recorded as typed.” Until you switch it on, Check in stays grey: “The document has expired: say whether to check in anyway.” The date typed is kept as typed.
  • A date that cannot be right, or a phone that is not one, is refused in words: “The passport expires before it was issued.”, “"abc" is not a phone number. Type 10 to 15 digits, e.g. +91 98765 43210.”
  • A guest from abroad is never checked in by express (A3.8): the desk always fills in Form III.

Watch out

  • The server refuses by itself if a starred box is blank: “Form III needs a foreign guest's visa or OCI number, visa type, phone in India, ….”
  • Each foreigner in the room needs their own Form III: add them under Adults in the room (A3.3), and they have their own row on the Form III screen, under an Indian guest too (A3.5).
  • A test hotel's Form III card carries “TEST · NOT VALID”, so a practice guest's card is never filed.

Why it works this way

Form C was replaced by Form III under the Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025: arrival and departure each within 24 hours, with more details: D27-25. The law's own boxes are asked before check-in finishes and the rest may wait; an expired passport is warned of and recorded as typed. Nepal and Bhutan need no visa; an ID skipped for a reason takes its visa with it. The same boxes serve check-in, New booking and Complete details, so the three cannot ask different questions: D27-25.

A3.5The Form III screen

Open Form III (under Finance in the menu, A4.1; or Find a screen, Ctrl K). It shows passport numbers, so only people who check guests in see it: the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and reception.

This system does not file with the FRRO. The screen says so itself. Filing happens on the government portal, where the guest's photo is taken. This screen is a register: it names who owes a filing, by when, and records the number the portal gives back.

To record a filing

  1. Read the badge beside the heading: “1 due” in yellow, or “1 overdue” in red. The line under the heading says “Foreign guests reported to the FRRO: arrival and departure, each within 24 hours (Form III, earlier called Form C).”
  2. Find the guest. Every foreigner on a stay has a row of their own: the person, their nationality, the room and how many are in it, and Passport (“not on record” in red where none is kept). A stay with an adult still to record says so on the row, in yellow: “2 adults, 1 recorded”. It is a warning: nothing is refused.
  3. In the Arrival cell, read the time of check-in and due or overdue with the time it is due by. File it on the portal.
  4. Click Record number, type the number the portal gives back in Portal number, and click Save. The number is required: Save stays grey until one is typed. The cell then reads filed with the number. Each person's number goes on their own row; the badge counts each person still owed. Add a number is for a second number, or for somebody who was never added: the stay's earlier numbers stay on the guest's row.
  5. When the guest has checked out, the Departure cell works the same way. Before that it says “In house”.

An Indian guest has no row here, and an adult nobody has recorded may be a foreigner. So above the list, for the stays in house with somebody missing: “Adults not recorded in house, each may be a foreigner: room 204 (2 adults, 1 recorded).” Add them on the stay.

  • Complete details opens the same boxes as check-in, with the guest's answers in them, and the Document and its Number. A passport skipped at check-in is recorded here, with the same checks as A3.3, and the row then shows it in place of “not on record”. After check-out it also asks Next destination (A5.8). Click Save.
  • The printer icon opens the Form III card: the premises, the foreigner, the arrival, the departure and the portal's other boxes, with two signature lines, one for arrival and one for departure. Click Print. A blank box prints as a dash, so the desk sees what is still to ask. The guest signs it on arrival and on departure; a guest who cannot write signs with a thumb impression. Keep the card.
  • When nobody is owed: “Every foreign national on record has been filed.” With no foreign guest at all: “No foreign nationals have stayed”.

Why it works this way

The portal has no upload and no way for a program to file, so the screen is a register and says so: a hotel that believed the PMS had filed would stop filing, and find out at an inspection. The number is required because a register with no evidence reads as compliance and is not: D27-25. Only the people who check guests in see passport numbers: D25-38.

A3.6An advance before check-in

A hotel may ask for money before it gives the room. Its rule is set in Settings (A17.11), under Advance before check-in: None (where every hotel starts), The first night or The whole stay. It asks the agreed rate with its GST. What is already paid counts: an advance taken on New booking (A2.3), or any payment on the guest's own bill.

When the guest still owes it, Check in is grey and the line under it says how much, for example “An advance of ₹4,303.95 is owed before check-in (the first night ₹4,303.95, paid ₹0). Take it on the Bill tab.” With the rule on The whole stay it reads “(the whole stay …, paid …)”.

  • It is never asked of a company's booking, a room the group's host pays for, a complimentary room, a stay with no agreed rate, or a booking prepaid on a booking site.
  • A booking-site guest has the switch Prepaid on the booking site, ticked at first, with “Untick when the guest pays at the hotel: the advance before check-in is then asked.” Untick it and the line under Check in is asked again at once.
  • Taking it: on the Bill tab (A4.7), click Settle. Before arrival it opens for the advance: the window says “₹… advance owed before check-in”, the Amount is the advance still owed, and “An advance before check-in: it is held as the guest's credit until the stay's charges are posted.” Click Take payment; no tick is asked. A part paid leaves the rest owed: Settle opens again for it, and the line under Check in shows what is left.
  • A deposit with no advance asked (the rule on None, or the advance paid): Settle still opens before arrival, as “A deposit before arrival”. The Amount is empty: type what the guest gives. “A deposit before arrival: it is held as the guest's credit until the stay's charges are posted.” Click Take payment; no tick is asked.
  • On New booking, an advance typed in Advance (A2.3) is taken first, then Check in now checks the guest in, so the advance counts.
  • There is no way round it at the desk. A hotel that does not want an advance sets the rule to None.

Why it works this way

The advance is a per-hotel rule, none to start, asked of the first night or the whole stay and never of a company's booking or a booking prepaid on a booking site: D27-243, D27-246. There is no override: the refusal names the amount, and the desk takes it on the Bill tab, or the hotel sets None: D27-246. Settle opens before arrival for it, and New booking takes it before checking in: D27-328. Settle takes a deposit before arrival with no advance asked, its amount typed, never filled in: D27-330.

A3.7An early arrival

There are two kinds of early, and a yellow box on the Check in window says which. Both need a reason, and both are allowed: this is a hotel, and a guest off an overnight train is a normal day.

Before the hotel's check-in time

The booking is for today, but it is before the time rooms are ready (Settings, Rooms ready from, A17.11). The box says “Rooms are ready from 14:00.” and then whether the hotel charges: “This property does not charge for an early arrival.” or “An early arrival is ₹….” The question is asked of the server, so the answer is the hotel's clock, not the computer's.

  1. Type Why the room is being given early. It is required, for example “Overnight train, room vacant and clean”. Until then Check in is grey: “Say why the room is being given early.”
  2. Where there is a charge, the owner, the GM and a group administrator also have a tick, Waive the charge. It reads “The reason is still recorded, and its absence from the bill is visible.” Anybody else records the reason and the charge goes on.
  3. Click Check in. The charge, with its GST, goes on the bill under Room, at the room's own GST rate.

Before the booked day

The booking is for a later day and the guest is standing here now. The box says “This booking is for 14 Oct 2026. Checking in now moves it to today, so tonight is charged.” Type the reason, as above. Checking in moves the booking: the arrival becomes today, which is what happened, and the room is held from today.

More than a day early, the first press of Check in shows the move before anything happens: “This moves the arrival from 14 Oct 2026 to 11 Oct 2026: 6 nights, ₹18,270.00 with GST.” Nothing moves until you click Confirm. Cancel leaves the booking as it was. Where there is no GST rule for the rate, the figure is before GST, and a complimentary stay says “complimentary.”

Watch out

  • Both kinds can apply at once. The box then gives the date sentence, and after it what an early arrival costs.
  • The room is still checked for the whole stay. A room somebody else has tomorrow is refused, with the other booking's reference: “Room 301 is held by … for part of that stay.”
  • Only the owner, the GM and a group administrator may waive. The server asks again: “Setting that charge aside is a manager's decision. Record it and let it charge, or ask a manager to waive it.”
  • The fee is set on Settings, Money, Extra charges (A17.9). Where none is set, the box says the property does not charge.

Why it works this way

A guest in a room that is not ready is a decision, not a formality, so it is allowed but never silent: somebody says why, their name goes with it, and any charge reaches the bill. More than a day early used to be refused; it is now allowed, warned of and confirmed: D27-193. The early fee sits under Room on the bill: D27-223. The booking moves with the guest, and its room hold with it: D27-205.

A3.8Express check-in: a guest who sent their ID online

A guest can send their ID and address from their own page before they arrive. The page's link comes with the voucher, and the desk can send it again (A2.4, A10.7). Online check-in is on for every hotel unless the hotel switches it off. A guest cannot pay or choose a room from their page.

What the guest sends is not on their record until the desk has looked at the real ID and confirmed it. Then, at check-in, the ID is not typed again.

To confirm what a guest sent

  1. Open Online check-in (under Front desk, A1.10). Under Details waiting to be checked, each card shows the guest, the booking's reference and when it was sent, then what they typed: the kind of ID and its number (an Aadhaar shows only its last four digits, a passport shows whole), a passport's expiry, a visa, the address, and the photo of the ID if they sent one, to look at beside what they typed.
  2. Look at the guest's real ID. If it matches, click Confirm against the ID.
  3. If it is not right, click Not right - take them at the desk. The desk then takes the ID at check-in as usual.

At check-in

While details are waiting that nobody has confirmed, the ID step of the Check in window says “This guest sent their ID details online - check them on Online check-in”. The link opens in a new tab, so the check-in in progress is not lost.

Once the desk has confirmed them, the ID step shows the confirmed ID in place of the boxes: “ID confirmed from online check-in: Aadhaar ••••••••••3458”, with only the last four digits. Nothing more is asked about the ID, and Check in is orange as soon as the room is chosen. Use a different ID opens the usual boxes again.

Watch out

  • Only this stay's own online check-in counts. A document kept from an earlier visit never does.
  • A guest from abroad is never express. The desk fills in their Form III (A3.4).
  • Only the people who run the desk see these screens and the link: the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and reception.
  • Confirming is the clerk's act, under the clerk's name. Nothing the guest sends reaches their record or Form III unchecked.

Why it works this way

Everything the guest sends is a submission that the desk confirms against the ID, under the clerk's name: D21-8. An Aadhaar is shown as its last four digits on every screen: D21-18. An Indian guest whose ID the desk confirmed just collects the key; a foreign guest still goes through the full check: D27-236. Express counts only this stay's own confirmed submission: D27-242. Online check-in is on for every hotel: D27-281.

A3.9The registration card

The guest's registration card, the hotel's old card in Fidato's design, for the guest to sign. It is printed from a button, never by itself.

  1. Open the guest's stay panel: In house (A1.5), or the panel of a guest you have just checked in.
  2. Click Print registration card, in the row of buttons at the top. The card opens in a new tab.
  3. Click Print. Give it to the guest to sign, and to initial the box at the foot if they want no offers. With other adults recorded on the stay (A3.3), the card lists them under Other adults in the room: name, nationality and ID, an Aadhaar as its last four digits.

It shows the hotel; the guest's name, address, e-mail and phone; the stay: arrival, departure, room, persons and the tariff a night before GST, with the meal plan; the company that pays, and the travel agent with its voucher number; the ID and the nationality; where a guest from abroad arrived from and goes next (Form III, A3.4); a Remark box to write in; the hotel's house policies; the signature box and the box for no offers. Anything not known prints a dash.

  • G.R. card no: the card's number, 1, 2, 3 and on for each hotel, given when the guest is checked in. The tax invoice prints it too (A4.11). A guest checked in before 19 October has none, and the card prints a dash.
  • An Aadhaar prints as its last four digits only, for example “Aadhaar · XXXX XXXX 5674”. A passport or any other ID prints whole.
  • The house policies are the hotel's own lines, the same ones the booking voucher prints (Settings › Policies, A17.4).

Watch out

  • The Remark box is left blank, to write in. A booking's notes can be the hotel's own, so they are never printed on it.
  • Printing it changes nothing. Print it again whenever it is needed.
  • The button is for the people who may read guests' IDs: the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager and reception.
  • A test hotel's card is stamped “TEST · NOT VALID” and carries no GSTIN.

Why it works this way

The card as the old system printed it, numbered, with Aadhaar as its last four digits until the lawyer answers (D25-37), printed from a button: D27-344.
