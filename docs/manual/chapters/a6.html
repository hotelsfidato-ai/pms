Chapter A6

Housekeeping

For housekeepers, their supervisors and the desk. How to read the Housekeeping board, give rooms out, mark a room clean and then inspected (with or without a signal), report a repair, take a room out of service and put it back, follow work orders and what they cost, and look after guests' requests, wake-up calls and lost property.

A6.1The housekeeping screens

A housekeeper signs in and lands on the Housekeeping board. Their menu is short: the screens they work on. Every other screen they may use is still one search away (Find a screen, A0.6).

ScreenWho uses itWhat it is for
HousekeepingHousekeeping; reception and the managers see it tooThe board: every room, what it needs, and who is cleaning it. Clean, Inspected, Repair; Put back for a room out of service.
Work ordersHousekeeping, reception, the managersWhat needs fixing, who is fixing it, what it cost.
Guest requestsThe desk sees every request; housekeeping, the Inventory Manager, the kitchen manager, the chef and the waiter see their own team'sWhat guests in house have asked for: towels, a broken tap, a pot of tea.
Wake-up callsThe desk: the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager, receptionCalls to make, on the hotel's clock.
Lost propertyThe desk and housekeeping log items; only the desk returns or disposes of themWhat was found, kept until it goes back to its owner or is disposed of.

The housekeeper's menu also has Rooms, the house today (A1.7), and Store, Equipment and Purchasing (A9). Laundry is in A13.

The Start here card lists the housekeeper's three jobs: See what needs cleaning, Mark a room clean, Report something broken. Tap Got it to put it away; the account menu's Show Start here again brings it back (A0.2).

A6.2The Housekeeping board

The board says what it is for under its title: “Tap a room to move it on. Clean, then inspected.”

  • Empty rooms: the big figure is the rooms with no guest that are clean or inspected, of all rooms with no guest (“4/6 clean”). The two bars split it: Clean and Inspected. A room out of service is not counted.
  • The six counts: Needs cleaning, Clean, Inspected, In house, Due out, Out of service. A count with an arrow jumps to its group below. Due out has no group of its own, and a zero has nothing to jump to, so they stay flat.
  • Online means the board is connected. It refreshes by itself: it is told the moment a room changes anywhere, and reads again every 30 seconds (60 while it is live). Nobody needs to reload it.

The rooms are in groups, in this order:

GroupMeans
Needs cleaningNo guest, and not clean yet. Clean is ready.
CleanCleaned, not yet checked. Inspected is ready.
InspectedCleaned and checked: ready for the next guest.
In houseEvery room with a guest in it. The card shows the guest's name and a bed; a guest leaving today makes the card yellow, with “Due out”.
Out of serviceNot for sale (A6.7). The card has one button, Put back.

Watch out

  • No button sends a room for cleaning. A check-out does it by itself: the room the guest leaves goes to Needs cleaning. A room nobody slept in (a booking cancelled or a no-show) stays as housekeeping left it.
  • A room out of service cannot be marked clean: put it back first (A6.7).

Why it works this way

The words under the buttons, and a grey one saying why: D17-8. Each count jumps to its group, with an arrow; Due out and a zero stay flat: D27-95. The board refreshes itself: D17-39. The board uses the same words for a room as Rooms does, so the desk and housekeeping name a room the same way.

A6.3Giving rooms to a housekeeper

A supervisor gives each room to somebody before the shift. Everyone working at the hotel is in the list, housekeepers first.

  1. On the room's card, open the list that says Unassigned.
  2. Choose the person. It is saved at once; there is no Save button.
  3. The names along the top count the rooms on each person's sheet: Test Housekeeper 2. Unassigned, at the end, shows the rooms nobody has yet.

On a phone, a housekeeper taps their own name and sees only their rooms. All rooms shows every room again.

  • Rooms are given for the business day. When the night audit moves the date on (A7), the new day starts with every room unassigned.
  • Giving rooms out works with no signal too: it waits on the device and is sent when the line is back (A6.5).

Why it works this way

Everyone sees their own hotel's staff, names and roles only, so a hotel manager can give rooms out: D27-96. The list on each card stays as it is: D17-40.

A6.4Clean, then inspected

A room is cleaned, then inspected, in that order.

  1. When the room is clean, tap Clean on its card. The message “Room 210 is clean.” appears at once, and the room moves to the Clean group.
  2. When the room has been checked, tap Inspected. The message says “Room 210 is inspected.”, and the room moves to Inspected.

A grey button says why

A button that cannot be used yet is grey. Tap it anyway, and the card says why in one line, for example “Room 210 needs cleaning first.” or “Room 202 is already inspected.”. The line goes when the room moves on.

Why it works this way

The message at once, and the card moving straight away: D27-94. A grey button that says why when tapped: D17-8. The server refuses the same order: “A dirty room is cleaned before it is inspected.”

A6.5With no signal

The board is made for a phone on a stairwell. With no signal, carry on: each tap is kept on the phone and sent by itself when the connection is back.

  1. The pill at the top says Offline.
  2. Tap Clean as usual. The message says “Saved on this phone - Housekeeping is held and will be sent when the connection is back.”
  3. Under the title: “1 change waiting to sync”. The bar at the top of the page says the same (A0.8).
  4. The room's card turns yellow and says “Queued: mark clean”. The next button, Inspected, can be tapped too; it waits the same way.
  5. When the signal is back, the held taps are sent by themselves, and the lines go away.

Watch out

  • What waits on the phone: Clean and Inspected, giving rooms out, and a repair (A6.6). Taking a room out of service, putting it back, and a repair's cost need the connection.
  • The held taps stay on the phone even if it is locked or the page is reloaded. A private window cannot keep them: the bar says so (A0.8).
  • A grey button on a held room says so: “Room 209 is already marked clean on this phone, waiting to sync.”

A6.6Something broken: Report a repair

A dripping tap, a fused bulb, a broken chair. Most repairs do not stop a room being sold, so reporting one does not take the room out unless you say so.

  1. On the room's card, tap Repair. Report a repair · Room 209 opens.
  2. Type What needs fixing, in a few words.
  3. If it is a tagged piece of equipment, choose it under Equipment; the things in this room are listed first. Otherwise leave Not a piece of equipment.
  4. Choose the Kind (maintenance, housekeeping, electrical, plumbing, furniture, it, other) and the Priority (Urgent, High, Normal, Low). Add Anything else if it helps.
  5. Tap Raise it. The message says “Repair raised”, and it is on Work orders (A6.8).

When the room must come out too

  1. Tick Take room 209 out of service too.
  2. Choose Why is it coming out?, the From date (today to start) and, if known, Until. Leave Until empty if nobody knows.
  3. Tap Raise it. The message says the repair is raised and the room is out: “Room 209 is out of service, no return date set.” On Work orders the repair says “Room is out”.

Watch out

  • A room with a guest in it cannot be taken out: the window says “Somebody is in room 209, so it cannot be taken out of service now.” Report the repair; take the room out after the guest leaves.
  • A piece of equipment chosen here shows as Under repair until its last open repair is closed. Only a tagged item can be chosen; one waiting in the approvals inbox is listed, but cannot be.
  • With no signal, the repair waits on the phone (“Saved on this phone”), but taking the room out does not: the message says to do it when you are back online.

Why it works this way

Repair reports a repair for the room, and taking the room out is a second choice inside it, because a dripping tap does not cost a night's sale: D17-8. Opening a repair on an item makes it Under repair, closing it makes it In use: D17-7.

A6.7A room out of service, and back

A room that cannot be sold is taken out for a period: a refurbishment, a broken shower, a room kept for house use. A room out of service is not offered for sale anywhere.

To take a room out with no repair

  1. On the room's card, tap Repair, then Out of service without a repair at the foot of the window.
  2. Choose Why is it coming out?:
    • Out of order - something is broken
    • Out of service - held back deliberately
    • Refurbishment
    • NC - not for sale (house use: staff, storage, an office)
    • Owner occupied
  3. Set From, and Until if it is known. The grey pill says the period, or “No end date”.
  4. Write in Note what is actually wrong.
  5. Tap Take out of service. The message says “Room 209 is out”, with “No return date set.” or the date it is back on sale.

To put a room back

  1. Find the room under Out of service (or tap that count at the top) and tap Put back.
  2. Under ALREADY OUT, each period the room is out is listed with its dates. Tap Put back beside the one that is over.
  3. The message says “Room 308 is back”. The room returns under Needs cleaning, so somebody looks at it before a guest sleeps in it.

Watch out

  • A room with a guest in it cannot be taken out: “Somebody is in this room. Move them out before taking it off sale.”
  • Closing the repair that took a room out does not put the room back. Put it back on the room itself, so somebody decides.
  • Taking a room out and putting it back need the connection.
  • A hotel can choose that a room back from a block returns as it was before, not as Needs cleaning: Settings, Front desk rules, Housekeeping, A room back from a block is sent for cleaning (on to start).

Why it works this way

Out of service is a period with dates, not a switch: a room can be out from Thursday for the plumber, and the hotel can still say what was for sale last Tuesday. Until is empty unless somebody knows it, so the room never comes back on sale on a day nobody chose. The housekeeping switches in Front desk rules: D27-260.

A6.8Work orders

Work orders is the list of what needs fixing: “What needs fixing. Most of it does not take a room out of service.” Open lists what is still to fix, most urgent first and the oldest first within that; Closed keeps what was done.

  • A repair that is not about a room (a lift, the lobby) shows a dash where the room would be.
  • The days open stand out once a repair has been open a week.
  • “Room is out” marks the few repairs that took their room out of service.
  • Cost is shown only to those who may see money (below). No cost recorded is a dash, never ₹0.

To raise one from Work orders

Raise one opens the same form as the room's Repair button (A6.6), called Raise a work order, with a Room box: “Leave empty for anything that is not a room - a lift, the lobby.” Raise it stays grey until What needs fixing is typed.

To start, and to close

  1. When somebody begins the work, tap Start. The repair says “Being fixed”. Put back makes it wait again.
  2. When the work is finished, tap Close.
  3. Write What was done: “The line somebody reads the next time this breaks.”
  4. Tap Fixed; or Not needed if it needed no fixing after all. Both need What was done.
  5. It moves to Closed, marked “Fixed” or “Not needed”, with who closed it and when, and what was done.

What it cost

A repair's cost is recorded with its proof. Tap Costs on the repair, open or closed.

  1. Under Record a cost, type the Amount, in rupees. The box is empty: the grey 1800 is only an example.
  2. Under Proof, choose the photo or screenshot of the bill, the payment or the transaction. “A cost is not recorded without it.”
  3. Add a Note if it helps: who was paid, for what.
  4. Tap Record the cost.
  • A repair may have several costs, each with its own proof. One bill proves one cost.
  • Each cost has See the proof. A wrong one is cancelled with Cancel this cost and a reason: it stays on the record, out of the total. Nothing is deleted.
  • A bill that arrives after the repair is closed is recorded under Closed, on the same Costs button.

Watch out

  • Start and Close are for the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager, the Inventory Manager and housekeeping. Anybody else sees one sentence saying so.
  • Costs are recorded by the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager or the Inventory Manager, with no approval but never without proof. The accountant sees them too. Everybody else sees the repair without the money.
  • A cost needs the connection: a photo cannot wait on the phone.
  • A repair already closed cannot be closed again: “This repair is already closed.”

Why it works this way

Who updates a repair: D22-34. Who records a cost: D17-5; no approval, but never without proof: D17-6. Closing the last repair on a piece of equipment puts it back in use: D17-7. Closed repairs are kept, because “how many times have we fixed this room” needs them.

A6.9Guest requests

A guest in house wants two more towels, or says the tap is broken. A request goes to the team it is for:

TeamWho receives it
HousekeepingHousekeeping
Something brokenWhoever updates a repair: the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager, the Inventory Manager, housekeeping. It is also a repair on Work orders.
Food and drinkThe kitchen manager, the chef, the waiter

The desk (the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager, reception) sees every team's requests and may take, close or cancel any. Anybody who sees the screen may raise one.

To raise a request

  1. Tap New request.
  2. Choose the Room. Only guests in house now are listed.
  3. Choose the team: Housekeeping, Something broken or Food and drink.
  4. Type What is wanted, and When only if the guest said.
  5. Tap Raise it. The message says “Raised”, with the team and the words.

To do a request

  1. Open Guest requests. If you receive more than one team, they are listed beside All; tap one to see only its requests.
  2. Tap Take it. It says “Being done” and, for everyone to see, “Test Housekeeper has it”.
  3. When it is done, tap Done. A Housekeeping or Food and drink request closes with that one tap. Something broken asks What was done first, like a repair.
  4. Done, last 24 h lists what was finished, with how long each took and who did it.

You may also tap Done straight away, without Take it. Cancel it asks Why it is cancelled, then Cancel the request.

Watch out

  • Somebody else has it: Take it on a request already taken is refused, naming who: “… has already taken it.”
  • Guests ask from their own page too, while they are checked in. Their requests arrive here saying “from the guest's page”, and the guest sees them as Open, Being done, Done or Declined.
  • The guest's name is shown only to those who may read guests' details: a housekeeper sees the room, not the name.
  • Food and drink carries no price. Anything to be paid for is still rung on the Till and charged to the room (A8).
  • A request needs the connection: it is not held on the phone.
  • While nobody has taken a request, the bell says so: “1 guest request waiting”.

Why it works this way

A request is a work order that carries the stay; three kinds, one per team; the desk sees and handles any; Something broken is also on Work orders and asks what was done; no time targets, so nothing turns red: D27-181.

A6.10Wake-up calls

Wake-up calls is for the desk: “Calls to make, on the hotel's clock. Due from fifteen minutes before.”

  1. Tap Set a call.
  2. Choose the Room (guests in house now), the Day (today to start) and the Time.
  3. Add a Note only if there is something to say on the call.
  4. Tap Set the call. It is listed under To make.
  5. From fifteen minutes before, it says “Due”, and the bell reminds the desk. Make the call, then tap Done.
  • A call stays Due until it is marked done: there is no “missed”.
  • Cancel it asks Why it is cancelled, then Cancel the call. The reason is kept.
  • A guest can ask for a call from their own page. It shows “Asked” and “asked from the guest's page”, with Confirm; once confirmed it is a call like any other.
  • A time already past is refused: “That time has passed.”

Why it works this way

Set by the desk and asked for from the guest's page; the bell from fifteen minutes before until done, with no missed state; in reception's menu: D27-241.

A6.11Lost property

Lost property is “What was found, kept until it goes back to its owner or is disposed of.” The desk and housekeeping log what they find; only the desk marks an item returned or disposed of.

To log an item

  1. Tap Log an item.
  2. Type What it is and Where it was found.
  3. Type the Room if it was in one, and Where it is kept now.
  4. Tap Log it. It is listed under Kept, with how many days it has been kept.

When it goes back to its owner (the desk)

  1. Tap Returned on the item.
  2. Type Who it went to.
  3. Tap Mark returned. It moves to Returned or disposed, saying to whom, by whom, and when.

When nobody claims it (the desk)

  1. Tap Disposed on the item.
  2. Type Why it was disposed of.
  3. Tap Mark disposed. It moves to Returned or disposed, marked “Disposed of”, with the reason, who and when.

Watch out

  • Nothing is disposed of by itself. The days kept are shown so the desk can decide.
  • A room number must be one of this hotel's rooms, in small letters or capitals (111a is room 111A): “There is no room 999 here.”
  • There is no photo of the item for now: describe it well in What it is.

Why it works this way

The desk and housekeeping log; only the desk returns (to whom) or disposes (with a reason); days kept shown; no automatic disposal; no photo for now; in reception's and housekeeping's menus: D27-241.
