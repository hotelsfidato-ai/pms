Chapter A0

Getting started

For your first day. How to sign in, how the screen is laid out, how to find any screen, and what happens when the internet drops.

A0.1Signing in and out

Fidato PMS runs in a web browser, on a desk computer, a tablet or a phone. Nothing needs to be installed. Your property gives you an email and a password. Use only your own: everything you do is saved under your name.

To sign in

  1. Open the browser and go to pms.fidatohotels.com.
  2. Type your email in Email.
  3. Type your password in Password. To see what you typed, click the eye at the end of the box (Show password). Click it again to hide it. It starts hidden because the desk screen faces the lobby.
  4. Click Sign in.
  5. For a moment the screen says “Checking your session…”. Then your first screen opens (A0.2).

Watch out

  • “That email and password do not match.” The message is the same whether the email or the password is wrong, so check both. (A different message for each would tell a stranger which emails belong to staff.)
  • “No active staff record for this account.” Your password was right, but your account is switched off or not set up at any property. Ask your general manager.
  • Forgot your password? The page says it: “Your general manager can reset your password from the People screen.”
  • “Set a longer password”. If your role was raised to owner, Admin or General Manager and your password is shorter than 10 characters, this box appears right after you sign in. It cannot be closed. Type a new password and click Set it. Everybody else needs at least 8 characters.
  • The first sign-in on a device needs the internet. After that, if the line drops, a reload keeps you signed in (A0.8).

To sign out

  1. Click your name at the top right. This is the account menu.
  2. Click Sign out. The sign-in page opens.

Closing the browser or the tab does not sign you out. On a desk computer that other people use, always sign out when you leave.

Watch out

  • Signing out clears the lists kept on the computer, so the next person does not open on your screens.
  • If you made changes with no connection and they are not sent yet, they stay on the computer, under your name. The sign-in page says “1 change is kept on this computer and will be sent when you sign in here again.” They go when you sign in on this computer again, not on another one.

Why it works this way

Longer passwords for the people who reach the most: D27-243 (10 characters for the owner, Admin and GM, 8 for others) and D27-245 (a person given one of those roles sets a 10-character password at their next sign-in). Clearing the computer at sign-out was one of the four security fixes the owner asked for before the release: D27-88. Unsent work stays under the name of the person who did it, because the bill and the ledger never forget who made an entry (the owner, 28 September).

A0.2Your first screen and the Start here card

After signing in you land on your first screen. For most people it is the Dashboard. It can also be Rooms or Calendar. Your general manager sets it for each person, and you can change your own.

To change where you start

  1. Click your name at the top right.
  2. Under Start on, click Dashboard, Rooms or Calendar. A tick shows the one chosen.
  3. The message “You will start on Rooms. From your next sign-in, on any device.” confirms it.

Some people have no Dashboard, because it shows the hotel's revenue and their day starts at their own work. They land on the top of their own menu: housekeeping on Housekeeping, the chef and the kitchen manager on Kitchen, the waiter (Till Operator) on Tables, the Inventory Manager on the Store.

The Start here card

Reception, housekeeping, the waiter and the chef see a card called Start here on their first screen. It lists the jobs they will do most. Each line opens the screen where the job is done and says which button finishes it. (Reception's card is in A1.9.)

  1. Click a line to go to that screen. A job done on the screen you are already on says “On this screen” instead.
  2. When you know your way, click Got it. The card goes away: “Put away. Bring it back any time from your account menu.”
  3. To see it again: account menu, Show Start here again.

Why it works this way

The first screen per person, set by the GM and changeable by the person: the owner, 14 September. No Dashboard for housekeeping, the kitchen, the restaurant floor and the store, because it carries revenue: the owner, 15 September, made firm in D27-141. A Start here card for reception, housekeeping, the waiter and the chef, none for the GM or the owner, gone when they tap Got it: the owner, 15 September.

A0.5Choosing the hotel

Every screen works on one property at a time. The property's short code is in the address, for example /h/5EL/arrivals for 5 Elements Akkalkot. Its name is under the heading of the Dashboard, Rooms and Calendar.

Most staff work at one property. The system opens on it, and the account menu shows it under Property (its name, then its code and city).

If you work at more than one property

  • Account menu. Under Property, each property you may work in is listed; a tick marks the chosen one.
  • Dashboard. A Property box at the top right picks which hotel the Dashboard's figures are for. It changes the Dashboard only; the rest of the system stays on the property it was on. The four tiles at the top then open that hotel's lists (A1.2).
  • The owner and Admin. All properties, in the Across properties section of the menu, shows every property on one page. Click a property's name to open its Rooms.

A test hotel

A test hotel is a practice copy of a real hotel, for learning the system without touching the real one, for example “Hotel Amrapali · TEST”. It starts with the real hotel's whole setup (rooms, rates, the menu, settings) and no guests, bookings or money.

  • The orange stripe. On every screen of a test hotel, an orange stripe across the top says “TEST HOTEL · practice only · nothing here is real”. It cannot be closed. While you see it, nothing you do counts.
  • TEST beside its name. In the account menu under Property, in the Dashboard's Property box and on People, a test hotel has TEST beside its name. It is never in All properties.
  • Its paper. Every bill, KOT, invoice, credit note, report and Form III printed there says “TEST · NOT VALID” in large letters and carries no GSTIN.
  • Nothing leaves it. Send voucher, Send check-in link, Send invoice and the review requests are off, with the line “This is a test hotel: nothing is sent from it.”
  • Who works in it. Only the people the owner or a group administrator puts in it. Somebody added on People with Test on works only in test hotels, never the real one. A member of staff may also be given the test hotel as a second place.

The owner and group administrators make a test hotel on Properties, under the real hotel: Make a test hotel. Once it is made, the same place has Open (go to it), Reset (deletes everything done in it, keeping its setup and its people; type RESET to go on) and Re-copy setup (brings the real hotel's rooms, rates, menu and settings again, deleting the test activity first; type RE-COPY).

Being checked before this page is final

What picking a property in the account menu changes on the screens is being confirmed with the owner. Until then, use the Dashboard's tiles or All properties to move to another property.

Watch out

  • Each property has its own business date. Two hotels can be on different days (A1.1).
  • Your role can be different at each property, for example GM at one and Reception at another. The screens use your role at the property in the address, the same rule the server uses.

Why it works this way

The property is in the address so a booking can be linked to and a task survives switching. The role read for the property in the address: D26-13. All properties on one page for the owner, in place of the table that was at the Dashboard's foot: D27-243. Test hotels inside the live system, one per real hotel, kept apart from everything real: D27-296.

A0.6Find a screen (Ctrl K)

The quickest way to anywhere. It finds every screen you may open, whether it is in your menu or not, and, if you work with bookings, guests and bookings too.

  1. Press Ctrl and K together (on a Mac, ⌘ K). Or click Find a screen… at the top. On a phone, tap the magnifying glass.
  2. Type a word: the screen's name, or the word you would say. Old and trade words work too: folio finds Folios, day end finds Close restaurant day, floor finds Tables.
  3. Move with the arrow keys ↑ ↓ and press Enter, or click the line.

Each line shows the screen's section on the right. A screen that is yours to open but not in your menu says “Not in your menu”. A service not built yet says “Not built yet”.

To find a guest or a booking

  1. Open Find a screen. If you work with bookings, the box says “Find a screen, a guest or a booking”.
  2. Type at least three letters of the guest's name or of the booking reference, or at least four digits of their phone number.
  3. Up to eight bookings appear under Bookings, below the screens: guests in the house first, then those coming, then those gone. Each line shows the reference, the dates and the room.
  4. Click one. It opens in All bookings with its panel open.

If nothing matches: “Nothing called that. Try a shorter word, or what a guest would call it.”

Watch out

  • It lists only what your role may open. A screen you may not open is not listed at all.
  • Bookings are found only by people who work with bookings. A phone number finds a guest only for people who may see guests' details; for others the phone is blank.

Why it works this way

“Off the menu, still findable by search” was the owner's condition for shorter menus (15 September). Finding guests and bookings from the same box: D27-239. Guests' phone and email kept from roles that read bookings but not guests: D27-143.

A0.8When the connection drops: the offline bar

Hotels lose the internet. The system keeps working wherever that is safe, and a bar at the top of the page says what is happening. It decides “no connection” from whether its last request reached the server, not from the Wi-Fi sign: the Wi-Fi can be up while the line behind it is down.

The bar saysWhat it means, and what to do
“No connection. Working offline. Anything you can still do will be held.”The line is down. Nothing is waiting yet. Carry on.
“No connection. 1 change held: Housekeeping. They go when the line is back.”Your work is safe on this device and is sent by itself when the line returns.
“2 changes held.” with Send nowThe line is back and the changes are going. Send now sends them at once.
“One change was refused by the server.” with the reason and DiscardSomebody must look. Example: the room was given to another guest by another desk while you were offline. Read the reason, put it right, then Discard the line.
“1 change was made by someone else signed in on this device. They will send when that person signs in again.”Not your work, and not an error. It waits for that person.
“This browser will not keep a queue - a private window, or site data turned off…”Nothing can be held here. Work in a normal browser window.

What can be done offline, and what cannot

 Examples
Held, sent laterMarking rooms clean or inspected, housekeeping assignments, work orders, marking a guest VIP, restaurant orders and the kitchen, a dish on or off the menu, a function enquiry.
Held, with a warningA check-in or a room given to a booking. You are told: “Check-in is held and will be sent when the connection is back. It is not confirmed until then - the room could be taken by another desk in the meantime.” A restaurant bill charged to a room is held the same way.
Refused, with a reasonAn invoice, a payment, the cash drawer, settling a restaurant bill in cash, card or UPI, closing the restaurant day, the night audit, a charge or a correction on a bill, check-out, moving a guest, Form III, confirming a banquet function. Anything not named above is refused too. Each refusal says why and what to do instead, for example: “Take it on paper and enter it when the connection is back.”

What you can still see

The screens below keep a copy on the device for a week, so they open with no connection: the properties and rooms, Arrivals, In house, Due out, Rooms and Calendar, Housekeeping, Work orders, the banquet diary, the restaurant menu and the rooms a bill can be charged to. Guests' documents, bills and reports are not kept: a shared desk computer is no place for a passport scan. A reload with no connection keeps you signed in, for up to a week.

Watch out

  • A room that reads Free on a copy from an hour ago may have been sold since. Check it again when the line is back, before you promise it to a guest.
  • Bills are not kept on the device. The In house and Due out lists do keep their last copy, balances included: with no connection, treat a balance there as possibly old. Online, every money figure is read again each time a screen opens.
  • A charge to a room held offline can reach the server after the guest has checked out. The server then refuses it, and the bar keeps the refusal on screen so somebody follows it up.

Why it works this way

A tax invoice number comes from a legal series; two desks offline could give two guests the same number, and it cannot be undone, so invoices are refused offline. Cash is counted against what the server knows, so payments are refused too. A waiter must be able to charge a meal to a room on a bad line: the owner, 8 September. Rooms kept on the desk computer for a reload with the server down: D22-23. Money figures re-checked every time a screen opens: D17-58.

Chapter A1

The front desk day

The screens reception lives in: the Dashboard to see the day, Arrivals to receive guests, In house and Due out to look after them and send them off, Rooms and Calendar to see the whole house.

A1.1The business date

The business date is the hotel's working day. It is not always today's calendar date: it moves on only when the night audit closes the day (chapter A7). Every “today” in the system means the business date: today's arrivals, who is due out today, the Dashboard's Today figures.

The Dashboard, Rooms and Calendar show it above the heading: “Business date · Sunday, 4 October 2026”.

When the business date falls behind the real date, a yellow line appears under the top bar of every screen, for example: “Business date 11 Sep, 21 days behind today (2 Oct). Close the days in Night audit.” If you may run the night audit, Night audit in the line is a link to it.

Watch out

  • “Today” for this line is the real date at the hotel, from the server. It never comes from your computer's clock.
  • When the night audit is run on another desk, your screens pick up the new business date the next time you open a screen. Open any screen before you start the new day's work.

Why it works this way

One warning line on every screen while the date is behind: D27-237 (4.2), against the recommendation of showing it on the calendar only. Other devices kept yesterday's date after an audit until this was fixed: D27-89.

  • City ledger: what companies and booking sites owe on their accounts, with “Paid today ₹…”.
  • No-shows: today's, with the month so far. Click it for the No show report.

The house now, and quick reports

The house now draws every room as a small square in the Rooms colours: In house, Due out, Arriving, Free, Out of service. The line beside the heading gives the rooms, how many are empty and how many are out of service. Under it, Quick reports: Manager report, Monthly statistics, Lodging tax, City ledger, No show.

Watch out

  • A dash (-) in a money figure means the system does not hold that figure yet. It is never a zero. A count, such as arrivals, can be a real 0.
  • If the figures could not be read, a red box says “Today's figures did not load” with Try again. The dashes under it then mean “not loaded”, not “nothing”.
  • Housekeeping, the kitchen manager, the chef, the Inventory Manager and the waiter have no Dashboard. The Revenue Manager sees it, but not what departing guests owe.
  • A brand-new system with no property shows “No property yet” and an Add a property button.
  • The Dashboard's figures come from the same place as the Manager report, for the same business date, so the two always agree.

Why it works this way

The page is called Dashboard and carries quick reports: the owner, 14 September. Built as sketched, with today's four tiles first, colours by what staff must do, “ARR” on screens, Nights ahead and the Due out tile showing what departing guests owe: D27-243. No Dashboard for the working roles: D27-141. Money hidden from roles that do not handle bills: D27-143, D27-147. A fresh system asks for a property first: D27-248.

A1.3Arrivals

Who is coming today. Check-in starts here.

  1. In the menu, under Front desk, click Arrivals. It opens on Today.
  2. The line under the heading says “Expected on Sunday, 4 October 2026. Check in from the row - the panel opens over this list.” The pill beside the heading says how many are expected.
  3. To find one guest, type part of the name, the booking reference or the room number in Guest, reference or room…. The count on the right shows how many rows are left, for example “1 of 6”. Clear empties the box.
  4. Click the guest's row. The stay panel opens over the list (A1.4).
  5. Click Check in, the orange button at the top of the panel. Chapter A3 goes on from here.

The next fortnight

Click Upcoming beside Today (the same word as in the menu, under Arrivals). It lists bookings arriving from tomorrow to 14 days ahead, without cancelled ones. It is for planning: a row opens the panel, but this list does not offer Check in.

The columns

Room, Guest (with the booking reference under the name), Type, Plan, Source, Departure, Nights (“Day use” for a room used within one day), Status and Rate per night (“Comp” when complimentary). On a narrower screen Plan and Source are hidden, then Type. A source the system does not know shows a dash; it is never guessed.

Watch out

  • A red line above the list such as “… has the phone or email of …, who is blacklisted. Reason: …. Check-in is not refused - you decide.” means a booking site's guest matches a blacklisted guest. The system warns; it does not stop you.
  • An empty Room means no room is given yet. You give one at check-in (A3).
  • Check in is shown only to people who may check guests in. Housekeeping and the Revenue Manager see the list without it.
  • Nobody expected? “No arrivals today. Nobody is expected. A walk-in can be taken from any empty cell on the Calendar.”

Why it works this way

The blacklist warns and shows the reason, a person decides: D21-21, and the warning on Arrivals and at check-in: D22-8. A control is shown only to a role the server lets use it: D24-27.

To take an order

  1. Tap a free table. The order pad opens over the room (Figure A8-02).
  2. Tap a dish to add it. Tap it again for two. To find a dish, type in Search the menu, or tap a section button (Starters, Main course…); tap it again to see all.
  3. A dish with a choice to make (a size, a crust, Sides) opens a small window first: choose, add a note for the kitchen if needed (“no chilli”), and tap Add.
  4. Check This round on the right. Use − and + for the number, the bin to take a dish off, Customise for add-ons (A8.3).
  5. Tap Send. The message “N4 sent · Kitchen docket 14.” means the kitchen has it and the KOTs are printing (A8.4).

Watch out

  • Nothing reaches the kitchen until you tap Send. The round is kept on your device while you build it. A round left on a pad shows on the table's tile as a dashed border with “not sent”.
  • No connection? Send says “N4 is held. The kitchen does not have it yet. It goes as soon as the connection is back.” The kitchen is not cooking it yet: tell them, or wait for the line.
  • A dish refused (off the menu today, a choice not made, the restaurant closed): nothing at all is sent, the round stays on the pad, and the message names the dish. Fix that dish and Send again.
  • The total at the foot of the pad is what the dishes cost before tax. The bill (A8.6) has the tax.
  • The same dish again is one more of it, but only until the kitchen has it. After that, more of it is a new line, so the kitchen gets a new KOT for it.

Why it works this way

The pad works with no network until Send, so a waiter at the table never waits on the internet; the whole round is sent at once, all of it or none, so the kitchen never cooks half an order. The same dish is one more of it: D27-189. The note after each tap: D27-290.

A8.3Add-ons: numbered, and custom

A guest wants something extra on a dish. There are two ways, both under Customise:

  • A numbered add-on, from the restaurant's list: “2 is Kebab, ₹120”. Staff remember the number.
  • A custom add-on, typed on the spot: something not on the list, with a name and a price, or Free.

A numbered add-on, before the dish is sent

  1. Tap Customise beside the dish (on the pad, the Till or the Terminal).
  2. Tap the add-on's number. Tap it again for two of it; × takes one off. An add-on that is off today shows, but cannot be tapped.
  3. The top of the window shows what the line will read, for example “Veg Biryani with add-on Kebab”, and the price moves as you tap.
  4. On a line of several of the dish, choose All 3 (the add-on on each) or Just 1 (one of them, as its own line). Done waits until you choose.
  5. Tap Done.

The dish stays one line, taxed at its own rate. The bill shows the breakdown under it (“Veg Biryani 310 · Kebab +120”), and the KOT prints “+ Kebab” under the dish so the cook does not miss it.

A custom add-on

  1. In Customise, tap Custom add-on.
  2. Type its Name, as the kitchen should read it: “Extra papad”.
  3. Type its Price in rupees (40, or 40.50), or tick Free. There is no default: a blank price, or 0 without Free, is not accepted.
  4. Set How many with − and +. It starts at 1 and is its own number: one papad for two thalis is one.
  5. Tap Add. It is listed under the add-ons as “+ Extra papad ×1” with its price. Add more if you need to; × takes one away.
  6. Tap Done.

A custom add-on is always its own line under its dish: “Paneer Tikka ₹320 / + Extra papad ₹40” on the bill. It is taxed at the dish's rate, and it goes to the kitchen with its dish.

On a dish the kitchen already has

The thali has reached the table, and the guest wants one more thing with it.

  1. On Tables: tap the table. The pad lists the dishes Already sent; tap Customise beside the dish.
    On the Till or the Terminal: tap Customise beside the dish.
  2. The window is called “Add to Paneer Tikka”. Tap numbered add-ons, or add a custom one, as above.
  3. Tap Done. Each add-on goes on as its own line under the dish.
  4. Send it, like any new dish: the pad's Send, or the Till's Send to the kitchen. Its KOT names the dish it is for: “+ Extra papad · for: Paneer Tikka (KOT 12)”.

Watch out

  • An add-on's number is set when it goes on. To change it, strike it (× on the Till) and add it again.
  • Taking off an add-on the kitchen already has is like taking off a dish: only the owner, an Admin, the GM, the hotel manager or the kitchen manager can, and they must say why (A8.7). Striking a dish strikes its add-ons, with the same reason.
  • Customise needs the connection on the Till and the Terminal: a change held offline could reach the dish after the kitchen has it.
  • Whoever takes orders can add a custom add-on. Each one records who added it and when.

Why it works this way

The numbered list, one line per dish, the breakdown on the bill and “+ Kebab” on the KOT: D27-289. “All 3 / Just 1”: D27-290. Custom add-ons, add-ons on a dish already sent, Free never a default, the dish's own tax, their own number: D27-291. The common custom add-ons can be given a number in the Menu (A8.11).

A8.4Sending to the kitchen: the KOTs

When you tap Send, the round gets one KOT number. The kitchen's dishes print at the kitchen's printer and the drinks at the bar's: one slip for each kind of KOT, each saying in big letters what it is.

  • What a KOT shows: the time, what it is (KITCHEN KOT 14), dine in or take away, the table, each dish with its number, its add-ons and Sides under it, and the kitchen note. Never a price.
  • Which kind a dish is depends on its menu section: Bar and Alcohol sections print at the bar, the rest in the kitchen, unless the manager chose otherwise (A8.10). It is decided when the dish is sent, so a reprint goes where the first slip went.
  • A dish taken off after it was sent prints, on a reprint of its KOT, under “STOP - TAKEN OFF”, with the reason.
  • A lost or torn KOT: on the computer that printed it, open Restaurant setup, the KOT section, This computer's last 20 KOTs, and tap Reprint. The slip says REPRINT.

A8.5The Kitchen screen

The Kitchen screen stays open on a screen in the kitchen (and one at the bar). Every dish the kitchen holds is a ticket, oldest first.

  1. When you begin a dish, tap Start. It moves to On the fire.
  2. When it goes out, tap Ready. It leaves the screen and is the waiters': the table's tile on Tables says “2 ready”.
  3. The waiter carries it out and taps “2 ready” on the tile (or Taken on the Terminal). It is served.

  • All / Kitchen / Bar shows one kind's tickets. Each screen remembers its choice: set the bar's screen to Bar once.

Why it works this way

Two buttons, Start and Ready; a Ready dish is the waiter's to carry out: D27-221, D27-222. The All / Kitchen / Bar filter in place of the old KOT printer button, and the two-row header on a phone: D27-292, D27-293.

A8.9The cash drawer: Close restaurant day

Close restaurant day is where the cashier opens the drawer with a float, records money that was not a sale, counts the drawer, and closes the day.

Once, before the first cash: open the day

  1. Open Close restaurant day. The card Open the day shows a row for each note and coin.
  2. Count the float in the tin and type how many of each: ₹500 × 4, ₹100 × 10. The total shows on the button.
  3. Tap Open the day - ₹3,000.

From then on each day opens by itself, on the float the last close kept.

During the day: money that was not a sale

  1. Open the Cash step. Under In and out of the drawer, tap Record.
  2. Choose What happened: Expense (paid out, such as milk), Cash in (a top-up), To the safe, or Due paid (money owed, collected).
  3. Type How much and What was it for. This line is what somebody asks about tomorrow, so say it plainly.
  4. Tap Record.

An expense may wait for a manager's yes: it shows “Waiting for a yes - not taken off the tin yet” and is not taken off what the drawer should hold until it is approved (A12).

At the end of the shift: count the drawer

  1. Open the Cash step. Under Count the drawer, type how many of each note and coin come out of the tin.
  2. Look at The sum: the opening float, plus the cash taken, plus or minus the money in and out, is what the drawer is Expected to hold. Under it, Counted.
  3. If they differ, a yellow box says “Short by ₹217” or over, and how much of it is a paid-out waiting for a yes.
  4. Tap Save the count - ₹3,140.

Close the day

  1. Open the Finalize step. Before it closes lists what a person must decide first: bills still open (settle them, or close them unpaid with a reason), tables never cleared after payment.
  2. Under The drawer, if the count is short or over, type Why. It does not stop the close; it has to be written down.
  3. Type the Float kept for the next day. The rest is deposited.
  4. Tap Close with the date. “A closed day cannot be edited.”

Watch out

  • Close restaurant day refuses while any bill is still open. It will not decide for you whether a bill is paid or closed unpaid.
  • After the close, new bills and payments wait for the night audit (A7), which starts the next business day.
  • Close restaurant day needs the connection.

Why it works this way

The first float is counted once; after that each day opens on the float the last close kept, so nobody types it again every morning. A closed day is closed: the owner, 28 September. A paid-out is not taken off the drawer until somebody says yes: D27-242. A difference never stops the close but must have a sentence, because a manager needs the sentence, not the number.

A8.10Setting up the restaurant and its printers

Restaurant setup is for the owner, an Admin and the GM. The kitchen manager sees its KOT section only.

  • Sections and tables: add a section (A/C, Non A/C, the bar), then its tables, one or a numbered run, four to a row. Taking one away switches it off; it is refused while a bill sits on it. Nothing is deleted.
  • Table booking hours: when hotel guests may ask for a table from their own page.

The KOT section

To make a computer print: the one-click way

  1. On the Windows computer the printer is plugged into, sign in, open Restaurant setup, the KOT section, and under Set up this computer for printing tap Download the setup file. Chrome may ask whether to keep it: Keep.
  2. Open the file (KOT-station-setup-<code>.cmd). If Windows says “Windows protected your PC”, tap More info, then Run anyway.
  3. It lists the computer's printers: type the receipt printer's number and press Enter. It makes it the default printer, puts a KOT station shortcut on the desktop and in Startup, and stops the computer sleeping while plugged in.
  4. It then opens the KOT station window on this section. Sign in: you come straight back here. Under This computer, type its name (“Bar PC”), tick what it prints, and tap Save.
  5. Go to the Kitchen screen in that window and leave it open. The printing line on every till now reads “Bar KOTs: Bar PC - online”.

By hand instead, step by step: src/modules/pos/KOT-STATION-SETUP.md (the default printer on 80 mm paper, the KOT station shortcut, then This computer). If slips come out too wide, set the printer's paper to 80 mm once in its Printing preferences.

  • One computer per kind. Ticking a kind on another computer moves it there; the old one stops printing it by itself.
  • One computer can print everything: tick every kind and the bills on it. Each slip still says what it is.
  • KOT kinds: Kitchen and Bar can be renamed (“Restaurant”) but never switched off; add a kind (Tandoor, Desserts) with Add a KOT. Its name heads its slip: TANDOOR KOT 14.
  • Which section prints where: each menu section starts with its tax heading's KOT (Bar and Alcohol to the Bar, the rest to the Kitchen); change the odd one, such as Mocktails to the Bar.
  • A computer replaced: tick its kinds on the new one. The old one then prints nothing and can be Removed from the list.
  • Every computer that prints keeps its last 20 KOTs here, with Reprint.

Why it works this way

Restaurant setup without a developer: D22-6. The KOT section, its kinds, one computer per kind, the routing by section and the bill printer: D27-292. The kitchen manager may open the KOT section: D27-292. The one-click setup file, and signing in coming back to the page asked for: D27-299.

A8.11The menu and the add-ons list

  • Prices are yours. Nothing was shipped with the system. A price changed today applies to the next order; bills already printed keep the price they were sold at.
  • Each section has a tax heading (Restaurant, Bar, Alcohol, Banquet). A dish is taxed by its section's rule unless it has its own rate.
  • Variations (a size, a crust): the first type's prices are the dish's; a further type's add to it, or are Free.
  • Sides: groups of options linked dish by dish, with how many a guest may choose.
  • Take off a dish until a time (“out of paneer”): the pad shows it, and refuses it, until then.

The Add-ons tab

  • The numbered list: number, name, what it is, price or Free. Change (never the number), Off for a while, Switch off / Bring back. A number is never given to a second add-on while the first is in use.
  • Custom add-ons used: what staff typed in the last 30 days, most used first, with who. Number it opens a new numbered add-on with its name and price filled in.

A8.13Trying it without a KOT printer

On the test computer only, two virtual printers stand in for real ones, so everything in this chapter can be tried with no printer: “Virtual Kitchen” prints Kitchen KOTs, and “Virtual Bar” prints Bar KOTs and the bills, onto a page instead of paper (Figures A8-09, A8-14 and A8-19 were printed this way). They are a testing tool kept on that computer only: they are never part of the live system, and they cannot reach it. How to start them is in .local/virtual-printer/README.md on that computer.
